## Task

Do an **AI-for-math project**. As in Assignment 1 and 2, you **drive an agentic coding
assistant with natural-language prompts** and let it do the heavy lifting.

Assignment 3 focuses on the **code**; the detailed write-up is the **Final report**
(40%), submitted separately — see `final-report.md`.

## Project options

Pick one of these, or propose another that makes sense (ask the instructor first):

- **Continue your Assignment 2 formalization.** Close remaining `sorry`s, formalize
  more of the proof, or move to a larger target if your Assignment 2 theorem is already
  complete. (Easiest starting point — no new setup.)
- **Improve a class tool and open a pull request:** Rethlas, Archon, or mdblueprint.
- **Contribute a formalization by pull request to a real project** (aim for an
  **accepted** PR):
  - **Mathlib** — Lean's mathematics library.
  - **Cslib** — the Lean Computer Science Library
    (https://github.com/leanprover/cslib).
  - **EconCSLib** — computational economics / game theory in Lean
    (https://github.com/gametheoryinlean/EconCSLib).
- **Discover mathematical patterns with AI / data-science methods.** Use computation,
  machine learning, or data analysis to find and test a mathematical pattern or
  conjecture (state it precisely, give the evidence, and a proof or proof attempt if you
  can).
- **Another sensible AI-for-math project** of your own, agreed with the instructor.

For a formalization project, expect at least **300+ lines** of correct Lean code,
typically produced by the AI agent.

## Form

- **Individual, or a team of at most 3 students.** Teaming is optional, not required.
- A team picks **one shared target** and splits the work (for a formalization, split
  the proof into lemmas — a blueprint — and each member fills in their lemmas). Work in
  the open: each member's contribution should be visible in the Git history.

## What to submit

- A **GitHub repository link** (or a `.zip` of the repository). For a pull-request
  project, the **PR link(s)** as well.
- A **short `README`** in the repository with:
  - what the project is (the theorem, or the tool change you made);
  - which AI agent / system you used.

The `README` is a short pointer, not a report — the detailed write-up is the Final
report PDF.
