The orbit categories $\,]X/G[\,$ and $\,[X/G]\,$

Let $G$ act cellularly on the cell complex $X$ (Locally finite regular CW complex with cellular G-action). The $G$-action combines with the face-order to produce two related categories.

Definition

Definition ($]X/G[$). The orbit category $]X/G[$ has:

  • objects: cells of $X$;
  • morphisms: $\Hom_{]X/G[}(E, E') := {g \in G : g \cdot E \preceq E'}$;
  • composition: $g_2 \circ g_1 := g_2 g_1$ (well-typed because $(g_2 g_1) \cdot E = g_2 \cdot (g_1 \cdot E) \preceq g_2 \cdot E' \preceq E''$ using cellularity of the $G$-action);
  • identity: $\mathbf 1 : E \to E$ from $\mathbf 1 \cdot E = E \preceq E$.

We write $g : E \to E'$ for the morphism corresponding to $g \in \Hom_{]X/G[}(E, E')$.

Definition ($[X/G]$). $[X/G] := ]X/G[^{\mathrm{op}}$.

The inversion $g \mapsto g^{-1}$ on $G$ induces a bijection $\Hom_{]X/G[}(E, E') \cong \Hom_{[X/G]}(E', E)$, so equivalently $$ \Hom_{[X/G]}(E', E) = \{g \in G : E \preceq g \cdot E'\}. $$

For the trivial group, $]X/\{e\}[ \,=\, ]X[$ and $[X/\{e\}] = [X]$ (see [[node:cellular_categories.category_jXk]]).

Endomorphisms are stabilisers

An endomorphism $g \in \Hom_{]X/G[}(E, E)$ has $g \cdot E \preceq E$; because the $G$-action preserves dimension (Cellular G-action on an abstract cell complex) and $\dim$ is strictly monotone on proper faces (Abstract cell complex), $g \cdot E = E$. Hence $$ \End_{]X/G[}(E) \;\cong\; \Stab_G(E) \qquad \text{as monoids}, $$ and dually $\End_{[X/G]}(E) \cong \Stab_G(E)$. This identification is what makes stalk actions of equivariant sheaves/cosheaves the endomorphism actions of the corresponding functors.

Why two categories

The two orbit categories carry the same information (each is the opposite of the other), but their "natural" targets for functors are different:

  • Functors on $]X/G[$ are naturally identified with $G$-equivariant sheaves: for $g : E \to E'$ (meaning $gE \preceq E'$), the map $F(g) = r^V_{gE, E'} \circ a^V_{g, E}$ points $V_E \to V_{E'}$ — matching the restriction direction of a sheaf.
  • Functors on $[X/G]$ are naturally identified with $G$-equivariant cosheaves: the opposite direction matches corestriction.

See G-equivariant (co)sheaves as functors on ]X/G[ resp. [X/G] for the precise equivalence in both directions.

Lean formalization

Only $]X/G[$ is defined as a fresh type in Lean; $[X/G]$ is accessed as the opposite category via Mathlib's CategoryTheory.Opposite.

abbrev OrbitCat (G X : Type*) : Type _ := X
def Mor (E E' : OrbitCat G X) : Type _ :=
  {g : G // g • (E : X) ≤ (E' : X)}
scoped instance category : Category (OrbitCat G X) where
  Hom := Mor
  id  := Mor.id
  comp := Mor.comp

Design choices:

  • OrbitCat G X is an abbrev for X, so all MulAction G X/PartialOrder X/AbstractCellComplex X instances remain visible without manual transfer.
  • Mor E E' is a Subtype (not a fresh structure) so its universe matches the universe of G, keeping the Category typeclass happy.
  • The Category (OrbitCat G X) instance is scoped so it does not clash with X's own Preorder-derived category structure.
  • [X/G] is (OrbitCat G X)ᵒᵖ — no separate Lean type needed.

The endomorphism-stabiliser identification is packaged three ways (useful in different downstream contexts):

def endMorEquivStabilizer (E : OrbitCat G X) :
    Mor E E ≃ MulAction.stabilizer G (E : X)         -- Equiv
def endMulEquivStabilizer (E : OrbitCat G X) :
    End E ≃* MulAction.stabilizer G (E : X)          -- MulEquiv
def endToStabilizer (E : OrbitCat G X) :
    End E →* MulAction.stabilizer G (E : X)          -- MonoidHom

The ≃* upgrade requires care with CategoryTheory.End's convention $x * y = y \circ x$ (opposite of standard group multiplication); endMulEquivStabilizer handles this.
