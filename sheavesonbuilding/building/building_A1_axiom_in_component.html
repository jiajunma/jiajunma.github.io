Bruhat–Tits axiom (A1) inside each connected component of $\BSigma$

This node delivers Step C.2 of Plan: identify each connected component of B(Σ) as K_x · A. The component-global form is the key ingredient for the main statement $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ in [[node:building.component_is_KxA]].

Statement: three forms

Let $\BSigma = G \times^K A$ as in Generalized building B(Σ) = G ×^K A, with apartment system $\mathfrak A := \{gA : g \in G\}$ and partition into connected components $\BSigma = \coprod_{C \in \pi_0(\BSigma)} C$ (π₀(X) ≅ G/J for X = G ×^P X' (with applications to B(Σ))).

(A1)-local (provable from cell intersection)

Lemma (Local A1). For any point $z \in \BSigma$ and any cell $\mathcal F'$ of $\BSigma$ with $z \in \overline{\mathcal F'}$, there exists an apartment $A' \in \mathfrak A$ with $z \in A'$ and $\mathcal F' \subseteq A'$.

Proof

Every cell of $\BSigma$ is of the form $gE$ for some $g \in G$ and $E \in [\![A]\!]$ (Sheaf7 §2.7). Write $\mathcal F' = gE$ with $g \in G, E \in [\![A]\!]$. Then $\mathcal F' \subseteq gA$ and $z \in \overline{\mathcal F'} \subseteq gA$. Take $A' := gA$. $\square$

So local (A1) is trivial.

(A1)-Kx-local (the $K_x$-local two-apartment property)

Lemma (Kx-local A1). For any cells $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ in $K_x \cdot A = \BSigma_x$ (for fixed $x \in A$), there exists $k \in K_x$ with $\mathcal F, \mathcal F' \subseteq kA$.

Status. Cited as a working hypothesis in Unit-iso assembly: V_F ≅ e_F H_0 via surjectivity + injectivity (line ~219). Used to assemble cartesian unit-iso via iterated Lanard 4.9.

Proof sketch. Translates as follows. Cells in $K_x A$ are $\{k E : k \in K_x, E \in [\![A]\!]\}$. For $\mathcal F = k_1 E_1$, $\mathcal F' = k_2 E_2$, both contained in $kA$ for some $k \in K_x$ iff $k^{-1} k_i \in \hat K_{E_i} \cdot N$ (by Apartments of B(Σ): definition, Stab_G(A), and parametrization of apartments through x Step 1 of C.3.1 proof generalised from points to cells). Equivalently $k \in k_1 N \hat K_{E_1} \cap k_2 N \hat K_{E_2}$; nonempty iff $k_2^{-1} k_1 \in N \hat K_{E_1} \hat K_{E_2} N$.

So (Kx-local A1) is equivalent to: $$K_x \cdot K_x^{-1} \;\subseteq\; N \cdot \hat K_{E_1} \cdot \hat K_{E_2} \cdot N \quad\text{for any } E_1, E_2 \in [\![A]\!].$$

Since $K_x \cdot K_x^{-1} \subseteq \hat K_x \cdot \hat K_x^{-1} = \hat K_x$ (it's contained in the fixator of $x$), the substantive content is: $$\boxed{\;\hat K_x \;\subseteq\; N \cdot \hat K_{E_1} \cdot \hat K_{E_2} \cdot N \quad \text{for all cells } E_1, E_2 \in [\![A]\!].}$$

This is an Iwahori-type decomposition for $\hat K_x = \Fix_G(x)$ relative to two arbitrary cell fixators of $A$.

When does this hold?

  • (a) Classical case $K_x = G_x$ (parahoric of $G$): the Iwahori–Bruhat decomposition gives $\hat K_x = G_x \cdot \Stab_N(x)$, and combined with BT (A1) for $G$ itself gives the required decomposition. ✓
  • (b) General K-cosheaf: not automatic. Depends on additional hypotheses on $K$.

(K3) is necessary for the proof above to even make sense: (K3) is what controls fixator interactions along geodesics, which is needed in any (A1)-type argument.

(A1)-component-global (the deeper statement)

Theorem (proposed; A1-in-component). For any two cells $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ in the same connected component $C$ of $\BSigma$, there exists $g \in G$ with $\mathcal F, \mathcal F' \subseteq gA$.

Equivalent formulation. For any $h \in J = \Stab_G(C_0)$ and any cells $E_1, E_2 \in [\![A]\!]$: $$h \;\in\; \hat K_{E_1} \cdot N \cdot \hat K_{E_2}.$$

(Derivation as in (Kx-local A1) above, with $K_x$ replaced by $J$.)

So component-global (A1) is equivalent to the Iwahori-type decomposition of $J$: $$\boxed{\;J \;=\; \hat K_{E_1} \cdot N \cdot \hat K_{E_2} \quad \text{for any } E_1, E_2 \in [\![A]\!].}$$

Status.

  • The inclusion $\supseteq$ is automatic ($\hat K_{E_i}, N \subseteq J$).
  • The inclusion $\subseteq$ is the substantive content.
  • For $h \in G' \subseteq J$: follows from BT (A1) for $G'$ — classical Iwahori–Bruhat $G' = G'_{E_1} N G'_{E_2} \subseteq \hat K_{E_1} N \hat K_{E_2}$.
  • For $h \in K_x \subseteq J$: reduces to (Kx-local A1) above (since $K_x \subseteq \hat K_x$ and we need $K_x \subseteq \hat K_{E_1} N \hat K_{E_2}$).
  • For general $h \in J = \langle G', K_x\rangle$: combine the above and use that the right-hand side is closed under products in an appropriate sense — this needs a closure argument that is non-trivial.

The abstract setting versus the two cases of interest

The generalised building $\BSigma = G \times^K A$ is built from $K$, not from a BN-pair structure of $G$. The classical Bruhat–Tits (A1) for $\mathcal B(G)$ is a non-trivial theorem of BT theory using the BN-pair structure. For abstract $K$, the Iwahori-type decomposition of $\hat K_x$ is part of the data rather than a derivation.

In this paper we care only about two concrete applications, and the $K$-cosheaf is built from well-studied lattice/Moy–Prasad data in both:

  • (b) Classical groups ($p \ne 2$): $K_x = J^\circ(\beta, \Lambda_x)$ the Stevens group at $x$ relative to a semisimple lattice sequence. $\hat K_x$ inherits Iwahori-type decomposition via Stevens enveloping ($\widetilde G = \mathrm{GL}_F(V)$) plus Glauberman $\sigma$-averaging. See [Stevens:SC §3.4], [Stevens:SS §3.5–§3.6].
  • (c) Tame groups ($p$ large): $K_x = G^{(0)}_{x, 0} \cdot J_x$ from the Kim–Yu twisted-Levi tower. $\hat K_x$ inherits Iwahori-type decomposition via the twisted-Levi Iwahori factorisation [KY §15], [AFMO2 Lemma 3.6.12], iterated over the tower.

Both verifications are written out in full in [[node:building.A1_Kx_local_verifications]] and mirrored as Sheaf.tex Lemmas in §10.3 (lem:A1-classical, lem:A1-tame).

For other applications using an abstractly given $K$-cosheaf, this remains a hypothesis to check per setting.

What we use downstream

[[node:building.component_is_KxA]] (= task C.3.4) needs component-global (A1) to prove $C_0 \subseteq K_x \cdot A$. Without it, we have:

  • $K_x A$ closed (union of closed cells).
  • $K_x A$ connected (all $kA$ contain $x$, so the union is path-connected through $x$).
  • $K_x A \subseteq C_0$.

To get equality we need: $K_x A$ is open in $\BSigma$, OR every $y \in C_0$ shares an apartment with $x$ (= component-global A1).

The "$K_x A$ open" line of attack also reduces to apartment-extension considerations equivalent to (A1).

Status (resolved 2026-05-25)

Per-case verifications now in [[node:building.A1_Kx_local_verifications]] and mirrored as Sheaf.tex §10.3 Lemmas. Both classical (Stevens) and tame (Kim–Yu–AFMO) cases are settled; the abstract case is the hypothesis (hyp:A1-Kx / hyp:A1-component in Sheaf.tex §10) and is not claimed in full generality.

Consequence: $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ ([[node:building.component_is_KxA]]) holds unconditionally in the two settings of interest. The previously listed "open question for the audit" is closed.
