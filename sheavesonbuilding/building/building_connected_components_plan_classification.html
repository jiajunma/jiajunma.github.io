Uses

  • The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A

  • Hypothesis (K3): Fix_G intersections lie in Fix_G along geodesics

Plan: identifying connected components of $\BSigma$

Goal

Give a clean geometric description of each connected component of $\BSigma = G \times^K A$, parallel to the algebraic description $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$ from π₀(X) ≅ G/J for X = G ×^P X' (with applications to B(Σ)).

Expected main statement. Fix any $x \in A$. Let $C_0 \subseteq \BSigma$ be the connected component containing $A$. Then $$C_0 \;=\; K_x \cdot A \;=\; \hat K_x \cdot A \;\;(=\, \BSigma_x\,).$$ Every other connected component is a $G$-translate $g \cdot C_0 = K_{gx} \cdot (gA)$. In particular every connected component is of the form "$K_y \cdot A_y$" for some point $y$ and some apartment $A_y$ of $\BSigma$ through $y$.

Significance. This identifies the KB lemma "$\BSigma_x = K_x A$" (currently a $K_x$-stable neighborhood with vague global role) with exactly the connected component containing $x$. It then aligns the geometric picture of $\BSigma$ with the algebraic $\pi_0 = G/J$.

Why a separate plan node

The connected-components proposition (π₀(X) ≅ G/J for X = G ×^P X' (with applications to B(Σ))) currently gives only the algebraic answer $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$. The geometric content "each component looks like $K_x \cdot A$" is not in there. It needs the Bruhat–Tits building axiom (A1) — any two facets in a common apartment — adapted to the generalized building $\BSigma$, which is a separate strand of argument.

A user-led check revealed that an earlier draft of the C-question discussion conflated $\Fix_{G'}(x)$ ("just fixes $x$") with its true role (parahoric / fixator acting transitively on apartments through $x$). Re-organising the C-questions around the right axiom (A1) restores the natural argument.

Setup recap

  • $G' \subseteq G$ reductive pair (see Reductive pair (G', G) and the apartment A);
  • $A$ standard apartment of $G'$, a Euclidean polysimplicial complex with $N := N_{G'}(A)$ stabilising $A$ setwise;
  • $K = \{K_x\}_{x \in A}$ a $K$-cosheaf of open compact subgroups of $G$, with $G'_x \subseteq K_x \cap G' \subseteq \hat K_x \cap G'$ ([[node:building.K_cosheaf]]);
  • $\BSigma := G \times^K A$ with quotient topology = CW topology (CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X');
  • $\hat K_x := \Fix_G(x)$.

The seven sub-questions (priority order)

Each sub-question becomes a small lemma in the KB. Resolved in order they give the main statement above.

C.1 — Apartment as a subset of $\BSigma$

C.1.1. Define an apartment of $\BSigma$ as any subset of the form $gA$ for $g \in G$. (Alternative: only those $g$ with $gA$ $G$-equivariantly embedded; check the candidates coincide.)

C.1.2. Compute $\Stab_G(A) = \{g \in G : gA = A \text{ as subset of } \BSigma\}$. Expected $\Stab_G(A) = \hat K_A \cdot N$ where $\hat K_A := \Fix_G(A)$ pointwise.

C.1.3. $A \hookrightarrow \BSigma$ is a closed cellular embedding, preserving the polysimplicial structure. (KB Prop 2.8 — already proved.)

C.2 — Bruhat–Tits axiom (A1) for $\BSigma$

C.2.1. (A1) does not hold globally for $\BSigma$ when $|\pi_0(\BSigma)| > 1$. Trivial: a point in one component shares no apartment with a point in another.

C.2.2. ★ Establish (A1) inside each connected component: for any two facets $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ in a single component $C$, there exists $g \in G$ with $\mathcal F, \mathcal F' \subseteq gA$. Equivalently, the apartments of $C$ cover $C$ pairwise.

C.2.3. Track which of (K1)(K2)(K3) is actually needed for (C.2.2). Conjectural: (K3) is sufficient, possibly necessary.

C.2.4. Wang-style local form: "any two cells in $K_x A$ lie in a single $K_x$-translate of $A$". This is the $K_x$-local two-apartment property cited in Unit-iso assembly: V_F ≅ e_F H_0 via surjectivity + injectivity (line ~219). Already used downstream; codify here.

C.3 — Apartments through a fixed point $x$

C.3.1. The set of apartments of $\BSigma$ through $x$: $$\mathrm{Apt}_x \;=\; \bigl\{ gA \;:\; g \in G,\; gA \ni x \bigr\}.$$ Show $\mathrm{Apt}_x$ is parametrised by $\hat K_x / (\hat K_x \cap \Stab_G(A))$ (compute via $\Stab_G(A)$ from C.1.2).

C.3.2. ★ Geometric identity: assuming (C.2.2), $$C_0 \;=\; \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A' \;=\; \hat K_x \cdot A.$$ Proof: every $y \in C_0$ shares an apartment with $x$ by (C.2.2); that apartment lies in $\hat K_x \cdot A$ by (C.3.1).

C.3.3. Reverse inclusion / factorisation: $\hat K_x \cdot A = K_x \cdot A$. This is the KB Lemma already stated in The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A but whose proof is not currently spelled out in the KB. Conjectural mechanism: every $g \in \hat K_x$ decomposes as $g = k \cdot s$ with $k \in K_x$ and $s \in \hat K_x \cap \Stab_G(A)$, using (K3) plus a Bruhat-style factorisation. Track this carefully.

C.3.4. ★★ Main statement. Combining C.3.2 + C.3.3: $$C_0 \;=\; K_x \cdot A.$$

C.4 — Reconciliation with $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$

C.4.1. $\Stab_G(C_0) = J$ where $J = \langle G',\, K_x \rangle = \langle K_x, N\rangle$. Required for $\pi_0(\BSigma) \to G/J$ to be a $G$-set isomorphism rather than just a set bijection. (KB asserts this implicitly; make it explicit.)

C.4.2. Equivalent formulation: $J \cdot A = K_x \cdot A$. The inclusion $\supseteq$ is trivial ($K_x \subseteq J$). The inclusion $\subseteq$ uses (C.3.4) plus the fact that $\mathcal B(G') = G' \cdot A \subseteq C_0$ — apply (C.3.4) inside $\mathcal B(G')$ first, then absorb into $K_x \cdot A$.

C.4.3. This explains the otherwise mysterious presentation $J = \langle K_x, N\rangle$: $N$ is the apartment stabiliser in $G'$, so $J / \Stab_G(A) \cdot K_x / (K_x \cap \Stab_G(A))$ matches $\mathrm{Apt}_x$ from C.3.1.

C.5 — $G$-translates and the other components

C.5.1. $G$ acts transitively on $\pi_0(\BSigma)$. (KB already; from $G/J$ form.)

C.5.2. For $g \in G$, the component $g \cdot C_0$ has the same geometric form: $$g \cdot C_0 \;=\; (g K_x g^{-1}) \cdot (g A) \;=\; K_{gx} \cdot (gA).$$ Every component is "$K_y \cdot A_y$" for some $y, A_y$.

C.5.3. Distinct cosets $g_1 J \ne g_2 J$ give disjoint components $g_1 K_x A \cap g_2 K_x A = \emptyset$. (Implicit in KB connected_components.md; highlight.)

C.6 — Relation to $\mathcal B(G')$

C.6.1. $\mathcal B(G') = G' \cdot A \subseteq C_0$ (the Bruhat–Tits building of $G'$ lies entirely in the component of $\BSigma$ containing $A$).

C.6.2. Each connected component of $\BSigma$ contains a $G$-translate of $\mathcal B(G')$ as a sub-building. So $\BSigma$ is a disjoint union of "$K$-puffed-up" translates of $\mathcal B(G')$, indexed by $G/J$.

C.6.3. Describe $C_0 \setminus \mathcal B(G')$ as the points contributed by apartments $kA$ with $k \in K_x \setminus G'$. This is the raison d'être for the generalisation $\BSigma \rightsquigarrow$ $\mathcal B(G')$.

C.7 — Levi $M$ interpretation

The KB lemma The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A also asserts $\BSigma_x = K_x \mathcal B(M) = \hat K_x \mathcal B(M)$ for some Levi $M$. This deserves clarification.

C.7.1. Specify $M$: of $G$ or of $G'$? Determined by $x$ or by $K_x$? (KB currently silent.)

C.7.2. Describe the embedding $\mathcal B(M) \hookrightarrow \BSigma$. Does it factor through $\mathcal B(G')$?

C.7.3. Triangle: $C_0 = K_x A = J A = K_x \mathcal B(M)$ as three equivalent descriptions for three uses (geometric / algebraic / Levi-theoretic).

Open subtleties to watch

  1. Reduced vs extended building. When $G$ has a nontrivial central torus, $\mathcal B(G)$ has a reduced and an extended form. $\BSigma$ in this paper is which? bs_subset_x writes $\mathcal B(M)$; if $M$ has central torus, same issue arises.
  2. Vertices vs general points. Some BT lemmas hold for vertices only; we use general $x \in A$ throughout. Verify the apartments-through-$x$ argument doesn't secretly need $x$ to be a vertex.
  3. Apartments vs apartment systems. $\BSigma$ may have multiple apartment-system structures depending on the $K$-cosheaf chosen; the "apartments through $x$" formulation should be intrinsic.
