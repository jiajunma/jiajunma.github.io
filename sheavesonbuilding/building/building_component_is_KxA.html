Main theorem: $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$

This node delivers Step C.3.4 (the main statement) of Plan: identify each connected component of B(Σ) as K_x · A. It identifies the previously vague "$\BSigma_x = K_x \cdot A$" (The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A) with exactly the connected component of $\BSigma$ containing $A$, aligning the geometric picture with the algebraic $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$ (π₀(X) ≅ G/J for X = G ×^P X' (with applications to B(Σ))).

Statement

Let $\BSigma = G \times^K A$ as usual, $x \in A$ a fixed point, and $C_0 \subseteq \BSigma$ the connected component containing $A$.

Theorem. $C_0 \;=\; K_x \cdot A \;=\; \hat K_x \cdot A$.

Proof

The proof assembles three pieces:

(P1) Geometric content of $\hat K_x \cdot A$ (Apartments of B(Σ): definition, Stab_G(A), and parametrization of apartments through x Cor. C.3.1'): $$\hat K_x \cdot A \;=\; \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A' \;=\; \text{union of all apartments of $\BSigma$ through $x$}.$$

(P2) $K_x A = \hat K_x A$ (The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A, C.3.3 proven there).

(P3) Union of apartments through $x$ equals $C_0$ (the substantive geometric step, conditional on [[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] component-global form):

$$C_0 \;=\; \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A'.$$

Proof of (P3)

Inclusion $\supseteq$. Each apartment $A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x$ is connected and contains $x \in A \subseteq C_0$. So $A' \subseteq C_0$ (connected set meeting $C_0$ is contained in $C_0$). Hence the union is contained in $C_0$.

Inclusion $\subseteq$. Take $y \in C_0$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the cell of $\BSigma$ containing $y$ in its relative interior. By component-global (A1) [[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] applied to the cells $\mathcal F_x$ (containing $x$) and $\mathcal F$ (both in $C_0$), there exists $g \in G$ with $\mathcal F_x, \mathcal F \subseteq gA$. Then $gA$ is an apartment containing both $x$ and $y$, so $gA \in \mathrm{Apt}_x$ and $y \in gA \subseteq \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A'$.

Assembling

By (P3): $C_0 = \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A'$.

By (P1): $\bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A' = \hat K_x \cdot A$.

By (P2): $\hat K_x \cdot A = K_x \cdot A$.

Hence $C_0 = K_x \cdot A = \hat K_x \cdot A$. $\square$

So the theorem holds for any generalized building $\BSigma$ where (A1) holds inside each connected component, which is automatic for the parahoric case and verifiable for the applications in Sheaf7 §10.

Reduction lemma: $J\cdot A = K_x\cdot A$ as an iff

Lemma. $J\cdot A = K_x\cdot A$ as subsets of $\BSigma$ iff for every $j\in J$ and every $a\in A$ there exist $k\in K_x$, $a'\in A$, $n\in N$ such that $j\cdot a = k\cdot a'$ in $\BSigma$, equivalently $k^{-1}j\in K_a\cdot N$.

Proof. The inclusion $K_x\cdot A\subseteq J\cdot A$ is automatic since $K_x\subseteq J$. The reverse $J\cdot A\subseteq K_x\cdot A$ is, by definition, that every $j\cdot a\in\BSigma$ (with $j\in J$, $a\in A$) lies in $K_x\cdot A$, i.e.\ $j\cdot a = k\cdot a'$ for some $k\in K_x$, $a'\in A$.

For the equivalence reformulation: $j\cdot a = k\cdot a'$ in $\BSigma$ unpacks (via the $\sim$-relation defining $\BSigma$) to the existence of $n\in N$ with $n\cdot a = a'$ and $j^{-1}k\cdot n\in K_a$, i.e.\ $k^{-1}j\in n\cdot K_a^{-1} = n\cdot K_a\subseteq K_a\cdot N$ (using $N$ normalises $K_a$ via $N$-equivariance of $K$ + $K_a$ being a group). $\square$

This reformulation makes explicit the role of the (A1)-in-component axiom: under (A1), the $J$-action can be implemented by a $K_x$-element + apartment translation.

Corollaries (Tasks C.4–C.6)

C.4 — Reconciliation with $\pi_0 = G/J$

Corollary (Stabiliser). $\Stab_G(C_0) = J = \langle G', K_x \rangle = \langle K_x, N \rangle$.

Proof. Since $C_0 = K_x A$, $\Stab_G(C_0) \supseteq K_x$ (obvious) and $\Stab_G(C_0) \supseteq G'$ (since $\mathcal B(G') = G' \cdot A \subseteq C_0$ is connected through $A$, so $g \cdot C_0$ for $g \in G'$ contains $g \cdot A \subseteq \mathcal B(G') \subseteq C_0$, so $g \cdot C_0 = C_0$). So $J \subseteq \Stab_G(C_0)$.

Conversely, $|\pi_0(\BSigma)| = [G : \Stab_G(C_0)]$ (by the $G$-orbit structure on components) and $|\pi_0(\BSigma)| = [G : J]$ (by π₀(X) ≅ G/J for X = G ×^P X' (with applications to B(Σ))). So $\Stab_G(C_0) = J$. $\square$

C.5 — Other connected components

Corollary. Every connected component of $\BSigma$ is of the form $K_y \cdot A_y$ for some point $y$ and apartment $A_y$ through $y$.

Proof. $G$ acts transitively on $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$. So any component is $g \cdot C_0 = g \cdot K_x \cdot A = (g K_x g^{-1}) \cdot (gA) = K_{gx} \cdot (gA)$ using $G$-equivariance of $K$ (extended cosheaf). Set $y := gx$, $A_y := gA$. $\square$

C.6 — Relation to $\mathcal B(G')$

Corollary. Each connected component of $\BSigma$ contains a $G$-translate of $\mathcal B(G')$ as a sub-building. So $\BSigma$ is a disjoint union of "$K$-puffed-up" translates of $\mathcal B(G')$, indexed by $G/J$.

Proof. $\mathcal B(G') = G' \cdot A \subseteq J \cdot A = K_x A = C_0$. For other components, apply $G$-translation. $\square$

The "puffing" content: $C_0 \setminus \mathcal B(G') \;=\; (K_x \setminus G'_x) \cdot A$ modulo overlap — points obtained from apartments $kA$ with $k \in K_x$ not in $G'$. These extra apartments are present because $K_x$ may strictly contain $G'_x$ (as compact open subgroups of $G \supsetneq G'$).

Three equivalent descriptions of $C_0$

Description Used for
$C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ Geometric (apartments + $K$-structure)
$C_0 = J \cdot A$ Algebraic ($G$-coset structure, links to $\pi_0$)
$C_0 = K_x \cdot \mathcal B(M)$ Levi-theoretic (task C.7, application to specific Levi $M$)

All three coincide; the choice is one of convenience.
