#

Uses

  • Induced space G ×^P X' along a cosheaf P
  • CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X'

Connected components of $X = G \times^P X'$

General setup

Assume $X := G \times^P X'$ is induced as in [[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]: $X'$ is a locally finite regular CW complex on which $G'$ acts regularly, $P$ is a cosheaf of subgroups of $G$ on $X'$ satisfying (P1)–(P2), and $X$ inherits a regular CW complex structure from CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X'.

Main proposition

Proposition. Assume $X'$ is connected. Then $$\pi_0(X) \;\simeq\; G / J,$$ where $J$ is the subgroup of $G$ generated by $G'$ and $\bigcup_{x \in X'} P_x$.

Proof

Step 1 — Decompose $X$ along $G$-translates of $X'$

We have $X = \bigcup_{g \in G} g \cdot X'$, and each $g \cdot X'$ is connected (by $X'$ connected + $G$-action being a homeomorphism on each translate).

Step 2 — Define $\sim$ on $G$ and identify connected components

Define an equivalence relation $\sim$ on $G$ as follows: $g_0 \sim g_1$ if there exist $g_2, \dots, g_n \in G$ with $g_n = g_1$ and $g_i \cdot X' \cap g_{i+1} \cdot X' \neq \emptyset$ for $i = 0, \dots, n-1$.

It is clear that if $C$ is an equivalence class of $\sim$, then $\bigcup_{g \in C} g \cdot X'$ is connected (as a chained union of connected sets with overlaps).

Conversely, we show that every path $\gamma : [0, 1] \to X$ has its image contained in some $\bigcup_{g \in C} g \cdot X'$ for a single $\sim$-equivalence class $C$. This is an elementary CW-complex argument; no external citation is needed.

Proof of converse. By CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X', $X$ inherits a regular CW structure from the cells $\{gE : g \in G,\ E \in [\![X']\!]\}$, and is locally finite. The image $\gamma([0, 1])$ is compact, so it meets only finitely many cells $e_1, \dots, e_N$ of $X$. Order these in time so that consecutive cells $e_i, e_{i+1}$ either are equal or share a boundary point (i.e.\ have $\overline{e_i} \cap \overline{e_{i+1}} \neq \emptyset$).

For each cell $e_i$, pick any $g_i \in G$ with $e_i \subseteq g_i \cdot X'$ (at least one exists by construction of the cell set). Then for each consecutive pair $i, i+1$: $$g_i \cdot X' \;\cap\; g_{i+1} \cdot X' \;\supseteq\; \overline{e_i} \cap \overline{e_{i+1}} \;\neq\; \emptyset,$$ so $g_i \sim g_{i+1}$. By transitivity, all of $g_1, \dots, g_N$ lie in a single equivalence class $C$, and $\gamma([0, 1]) \subseteq \bigcup_{i} g_i X' \subseteq \bigcup_{g \in C} g \cdot X'$.

So each $\bigcup_{g \in C} g \cdot X'$ is a connected component, and every connected component is of this form. Hence $$\pi_0(X) \;\simeq\; G / \sim.$$

Step 3 — Analyze the relation $\sim$

Suppose $g \cdot X' \cap h \cdot X' \neq \emptyset$. So there exist $x, y \in X'$ with $g \cdot x = h \cdot y$, i.e.\ by the equivalence relation defining $X = G \times^P X'$ ([[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]), there exists $n \in G'$ such that $n \cdot x = y$ and $g^{-1} h n \in P_x$. We conclude $$g^{-1} h \;\in\; P_x \cdot G' \quad \text{for some } x \in X'.$$

So $g \sim h$ holds iff $g^{-1} h$ lies in a finite product of factors each in some $P_x$ or in $G'$.

Step 4 — Identify $\sim$ with the cosets of $J$

Let $J := \langle G',\; \bigcup_{x \in X'} P_x \rangle$, the subgroup generated by $G'$ and all $P_x$ ($x$ ranging over $X'$).

Claim. $g_0 \sim g_1 \iff g_0 J = g_1 J$ (i.e., $g_0^{-1} g_1 \in J$).

Proof of claim.

($\Rightarrow$) Immediate from Step 3: each "adjacency" step contributes a factor in $P_x \cdot G' \subseteq J$, so the composite $g_0^{-1} g_n$ lies in $J$.

($\Leftarrow$) Every element of $J$ is a finite product of the form $p_1 u_1 p_2 u_2 \cdots p_s u_s$ with $p_i \in P_{x_i}$ for some $x_i \in X'$ and $u_i \in G'$. Each elementary factor "$p_i u_i$" can be realized as an adjacency step: $g \to g p_i u_i$ takes $g \cdot X'$ to $g p_i u_i \cdot X'$, and these share the cell containing $u_i \cdot x_i$ (since $p_i \in P_{x_i}$ means $g p_i \cdot x_i = g \cdot x_i$ in $X$, hence $g p_i u_i \cdot (u_i^{-1} x_i) = g \cdot x_i$, giving a common element). Iterating, $g_0 \sim g_0 \cdot (p_1 u_1 \cdots p_s u_s) = g_1$. $\square$

Combining Steps 1–4: $\pi_0(X) = G / \sim = G / J$, as claimed. $\square$

Specialization to the generalized building $\BSigma$

Apply the proposition with $X = \BSigma = G \times^K A$, $X' = A$, $P = K$ (the K-cosheaf of [[node:building.K_cosheaf]]). Then $$\pi_0(\BSigma) \;\simeq\; G \,/\, J, \qquad J = \bigl\langle G',\;\textstyle\bigcup_{x \in A} K_x \bigr\rangle.$$

Reduction to a single $K_x$. Because $K$ is $G'$-equivariant ($K_{gx} = g K_x g^{-1}$ for $g \in G'$), $\bigcup_{x \in A} K_x$ is $G'$-stable up to conjugation, and $J = \langle G',\, K_x \rangle$ for any single vertex $x \in A$.

By the K-condition $K_x \supseteq G'_x$ and the Bruhat decomposition $G' = G'_x N G'_x$ (with $N = N(G', S)$), we get $G' \subseteq \langle K_x, N\rangle$, hence $$J \;=\; \langle G',\, K_x\rangle \;=\; \langle K_x,\, N \rangle.$$

So: $$\pi_0(\BSigma) \;\simeq\; G \,/\, \langle K_x, G' \rangle \;=\; G \,/\, \langle K_x, N \rangle.$$

Geometric shape of each component

The above identifies $\pi_0(\BSigma)$ algebraically with $G/J$. We also describe each component geometrically:

Corollary (Geometric shape of components). Fix $x \in A$ and let $C_0 \subseteq \BSigma$ be the connected component containing $A$. 1. (Unconditional.) $C_0 = J \cdot A$. 2. (Component-by-translation.) Every other component is $g \cdot C_0 = (g K_x g^{-1}) \cdot (gA) = K_{gx} \cdot (gA)$ for some $g \in G$. 3. (Conditional simplification.) Under [[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] (A1)-in-component, $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ — so every component is of the form $K_y \cdot A_y$ for some $y$ and apartment $A_y$ through $y$.

Proof

(1). From the analysis above: $\pi_0(\BSigma) = G/J$, and the component of $A$ is $\bigcup_{g \in g_0 J} g \cdot A$ for $g_0 = e$, i.e.\ $J \cdot A$.

(2). $G$ acts transitively on cosets of $J = \Stab_G(C_0)$ in $G/J = \pi_0(\BSigma)$. For $g \in G$: $g \cdot C_0 = g \cdot J \cdot A = (gJg^{-1}) \cdot (gA)$. Now $gJg^{-1} = \langle gK_x g^{-1}, gNg^{-1}\rangle$; $gNg^{-1}$ plays the analogous role at the apartment $gA$, and $gK_x g^{-1} = K_{gx}$ by $G$-equivariance of the extended cosheaf $K$ on $\cB(G')$. Substituting: $g \cdot C_0 = K_{gx} \cdot (gA)$.

(3). Under (A1)-in-component ([[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]]), $J \cdot A = K_x \cdot A$ (see [[node:building.component_is_KxA]]). Combined with (1): $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$. Verified cases: parahoric (classical BT), classical (Stevens enveloping), tame (twisted-Levi factorisation) — see [[node:building.A1_Kx_local_verifications]].

Three equivalent descriptions

In the verified cases (a)–(c) of [[node:building.A1_Kx_local_verifications]]:

Description Used for
$C_0 = J \cdot A$ algebraic / unconditional / link to $\pi_0$
$C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ geometric (apartments + $K$-structure)
$C_0 = K_x \cdot \mathcal{B}(M)$ Levi-theoretic (centraliser of semisimple character)

Examples

Example 1 (isotropic/anisotropic mismatch). If $G/Z(G)$ is isotropic and $G'/Z(G)$ is anisotropic, then $\pi_0(\BSigma)$ is infinite. More generally, it suffices that there exists a simple factor $H$ of $G/Z(G)$ such that $H$ is isotropic and the image of $G'$ in $H$ is anisotropic.

Example 2 (split case). If both $G$ and $G'$ are split (more generally: $G'$ is a non-twisted Levi subgroup of $G$), then $\BSigma$ is connected (i.e.\ $\pi_0(\BSigma) = 1$, equivalently $J = G$).

Proof of Example 2. Let $T$ be a maximal torus of $G'$ and (hence extending to) $G$. We may assume $\Delta$ is a system of simple roots of $R(G, T)$ and $G'$ is the Levi subgroup associated to $I \subseteq \Delta$.

We show $J = G(F)$.

(a) $J \supseteq G'(F)$ by definition, hence $J$ contains the torus $T(F)$ and the root groups $U_a(F)$ for all $a \in R(G', T)$.

(b) Let $a \in R(G, T) \setminus R(G', T)$. Then there exists $r \geq 0$ such that $J$ contains the Moy–Prasad piece $U_a(F)_{x, r}$ for all $x \in A(T)$ (the apartment of $T$), because $U_a(F)_{x, r} \subseteq K_x$ for sufficiently large $r$ depending on $x$. By varying $x$ across $A(T)$, $J$ contains the union $\bigcup_x U_a(F)_{x,r}$, which generates $U_a(F)$.

(c) Hence $J$ contains $T(F)$ and $U_a(F)$ for all $a \in R(G, T)$. It is well-known that these subgroups generate $G(F)$. $\square$

The same argument works whenever $G'$ is a non-twisted Levi subgroup of $G$, via Borel–Tits §5.18.

Example 3 (unramified quadratic restriction of $\mathrm{GL}_2$). Let $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}$, $G' = R_{E/F}(\mathrm{GL}_n)$ where $E/F$ is the unramified quadratic extension. Then $G'(F)$ and all $P_x$'s are contained in the kernel $$\ker\bigl(G(F) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ord} \circ \det} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\bigr).$$ So $|\pi_0(\BSigma)| \geq 2$.

Moral. $\mathrm{coker}(G'(F) \to G(F)/G(F)^\circ)$ gives a lower bound for $|\pi_0(\BSigma)|$.

Conjecture

Conjecture. Assume that for every isotropic simple factor $H$ of $G/Z(G)$, the image of $G'$ in $H$ is isotropic. Then $\pi_0(\BSigma)$ is finite.

(An analogue of this for real groups seems to be true; the $p$-adic version remains a conjecture in this note.)

Why this matters for §7.2

The §7.2 cartesian unit-iso proof (Unit-iso assembly: V_F ≅ e_F H_0 via surjectivity + injectivity) uses the geometric setup of $\BSigma_x = K_x \cdot A$ — the connected component of $A$ in $\BSigma$ is $\langle K_x, N\rangle \cdot A = J \cdot A$ (with $J$ as above). For the cartesian unit-iso argument we never invoke a global two-apartment axiom for $\BSigma$; we only use that any two cells in $K_x A$ lie in a common $K_x$-translate of $A$ (a $K_x$-local two-apartment property).
