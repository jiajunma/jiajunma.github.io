Apartments of $\BSigma$: definition, stabiliser, parametrisation through $x$

This node delivers Steps C.1.1, C.1.2, C.3.1 of Plan: identify each connected component of B(Σ) as K_x · A. Together with the building axiom (A1) inside a component ([[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] — separate node), these give the geometric description $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$.

Setting

Recall the generalized building $\BSigma = G \times^K A$ (Generalized building B(Σ) = G ×^K A):

  • $G' \subseteq G$ a reductive pair;
  • $A$ the standard apartment of $G'$, a Euclidean polysimplicial complex with $N := N_{G'}(A)$ stabilising $A$ setwise (so $A$ is an $N$-set);
  • $K = \{K_x\}_{x \in A}$ an $N$-equivariant cosheaf of open compact subgroups of $G$ satisfying the $K$-condition $G'_x \subseteq K_x \cap G' \subseteq \Fix_{G'}(x)$ ([[node:building.K_cosheaf]]);
  • $\BSigma = (G \times A)/\sim$ with quotient topology = CW topology (CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X' (Prop 2.8));
  • $\iota : A \hookrightarrow \BSigma$, $a \mapsto [(1, a)]_\sim$, is an injective closed cellular embedding identifying $A$ with a cellular subcomplex (still denoted $A$).
  • $\hat K_x := \Fix_G(x)$ for $x \in \BSigma$ (in particular $x \in A$). By [[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]] Lemma lem:Stab_gx: $\hat K_x = \Fix_N(x) \cdot K_x$ for $x \in A$.
  • We work in the "fine CW" convention of Sheaf7 (line 537): cells are refined enough that $\Stab_G(E) = \Fix_G(E)$ for every cell $E$.

Apartment as a subset of $\BSigma$

Definition (apartment of $\BSigma$). An apartment of $\BSigma$ is any subset of the form $gA = g \iota(A)$ for some $g \in G$. The family $\mathfrak A := \{gA : g \in G\}$ is the apartment system of $\BSigma$.

Each apartment $gA$ is closed, connected, cellularly embedded in $\BSigma$, and homeomorphic to $A$ via the (cellular, $G$-equivariant) action map.

The apartment stabiliser $\Stab_G(A)$

Lemma C.1.2 ($\Stab_G(A)$). Let $\Fix_G(A) := \bigcap_{a \in A} \Fix_G(a) = \bigcap_{E \in \mathfrak F(A)} \Fix_G(E)$ denote the pointwise fixator of $A$ in $G$. Then $$\Stab_G(A) \;\supseteq\; N \cdot \Fix_G(A) \;=\; \Fix_G(A) \cdot N.$$ The second equality holds because $N$ stabilises $A$, hence normalises $\Fix_G(A)$.

Proof

Both $N$ and $\Fix_G(A)$ stabilise $A$: $N$ acts on $A$ (setwise) via the affine Weyl action, and $\Fix_G(A)$ fixes every point of $A$. Hence their product (in either order) stabilises $A$. $\square$

Remark (equality). Equality $\Stab_G(A) = N \cdot \Fix_G(A)$ holds whenever every cellular self-homeomorphism of $A$ induced by an element of $G$ already arises from $N$. This is the case in the standard Bruhat–Tits settings of this paper. Strict containment can in principle occur if elements of $G \setminus G'$ induce extra cellular auto's of $A$ (e.g.\ outer Galois actions); we never use the stronger equality below.

Remark (apartments coincide as subsets). For $g, g' \in G$: $gA = g'A$ as subsets of $\BSigma$ iff $g^{-1} g' \in \Stab_G(A)$.

Apartments through a fixed point $x$

Fix $x \in A$. The set of apartments of $\BSigma$ containing $x$ is $$\mathrm{Apt}_x \;:=\; \{ gA \,:\, g \in G,\; gA \ni x \}.$$

Lemma C.3.1 (apartments through $x$). $$\bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A' \;=\; \hat K_x \cdot A.$$ Concretely: $gA \ni x$ iff $g \in \hat K_x \cdot N$, and the map $g \mapsto gA$ descends to a bijection $$\hat K_x \cdot N \big/ \Stab_G(A) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathrm{Apt}_x.$$ When $\Stab_G(A) = N \cdot \Fix_G(A)$ (typical case), $\hat K_x N / \Stab_G(A) = \hat K_x / \bigl(\hat K_x \cap \Stab_G(A)\bigr) = \hat K_x / \bigl(\Fix_N(x) \cdot \Fix_G(A)\bigr)$.

Proof.

Step 1: $gA \ni x \iff g \in \hat K_x \cdot N$.

($\Leftarrow$) If $g = k n$ with $k \in \hat K_x$, $n \in N$, then $n^{-1} x \in A$ (since $A$ is $N$-stable and $x \in A$), so $gA = k n A = k A$, and $kA \ni k \cdot x = x$ (since $k \in \hat K_x$). Hence $gA \ni x$.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose $gA \ni x$, i.e.\ $\exists a \in A$ with $g \cdot a = x$ in $\BSigma$. Apply Sheaf7 cell-intersection lemma (lem:qfd_surj with $E = \mathcal F_a$, $F = \mathcal F_x$, $h = 1$): since $g \cdot a = 1 \cdot x$, we get $\exists n \in N$ with $n \cdot \mathcal F_a = \mathcal F_x$ (so $n \cdot a = x$, using the fine-CW convention $\Stab_N(\mathcal F_a) = \Fix_N(\mathcal F_a)$ to match points within a cell), and $g \cdot n^{-1} \in \Fix_G(\mathcal F_x) = \hat K_x$. Hence $g \in \hat K_x \cdot n \subseteq \hat K_x \cdot N$.

Step 2: $\bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A' = \hat K_x \cdot A$.

From Step 1, every $A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x$ is $g A$ for some $g \in \hat K_x N$, so $A' \subseteq \hat K_x N \cdot A = \hat K_x \cdot A$ (since $NA = A$). Conversely, every $kA$ for $k \in \hat K_x$ contains $kx = x$, hence $kA \in \mathrm{Apt}_x$.

Step 3: Bijection $\hat K_x N / \Stab_G(A) \to \mathrm{Apt}_x$.

The map $g \mapsto gA$ is well-defined on cosets of $\Stab_G(A)$ (Remark above) and surjects onto $\mathrm{Apt}_x$ by Step 1. For injectivity: if $gA = g'A$ with $g, g' \in \hat K_x N$, then $g^{-1} g' \in \Stab_G(A)$, so they represent the same coset.

Step 4: When $\Stab_G(A) = N \cdot \Fix_G(A)$.

Then $\hat K_x N / \Stab_G(A) = \hat K_x N / (N \cdot \Fix_G(A))$. Since $\Fix_G(A) \subseteq \hat K_x$ (pointwise fixator of $A$ fixes $x$ in particular), this quotient equals $\hat K_x / (\hat K_x \cap N \cdot \Fix_G(A))$ by the second isomorphism theorem. Compute $\hat K_x \cap N \cdot \Fix_G(A)$: if $k = n \cdot f$ with $k \in \hat K_x$, $n \in N$, $f \in \Fix_G(A) \subseteq \hat K_x$, then $n = k f^{-1} \in \hat K_x$, so $n \in \hat K_x \cap N = \Fix_N(x)$. Conversely $\Fix_N(x) \cdot \Fix_G(A) \subseteq \hat K_x$. So $$\hat K_x \cap \bigl(N \cdot \Fix_G(A)\bigr) \;=\; \Fix_N(x) \cdot \Fix_G(A).$$ $\square$

Consequence: identification of $\hat K_x \cdot A$

Corollary C.3.1'. The subset $\hat K_x \cdot A \subseteq \BSigma$ is exactly the union of all apartments of $\BSigma$ through $x$: $$\hat K_x \cdot A \;=\; \bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}_x} A'.$$

Proof. By Theorem above (apartments-through-$x$, part (i)), $A' = gA \in \mathrm{Apt}_x$ iff $g \in \hat K_x \cdot N$. Hence $\bigcup_{A' \in \mathrm{Apt}x} A' = \bigcup{g \in \hat K_x N} g \cdot A = (\hat K_x N) \cdot A = \hat K_x \cdot A$, using $N \cdot A = A$ in the last equality. $\square$

This is the key geometric meaning of "$\hat K_x \cdot A$" and is the starting point for proving $C_0 = K_x \cdot A$ (Plan: identify each connected component of B(Σ) as K_x · A):

  1. Show this set is all of $C_0$ — this is the building-axiom step ([[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] = task C.2.2).
  2. Show $\hat K_x \cdot A = K_x \cdot A$ — the Sheaf7 lemma lem:BSx whose proof is to be filled (The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A update = task C.3.3).
