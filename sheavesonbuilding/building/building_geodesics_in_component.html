Geodesic uniqueness and CAT(0) structure on a component of $\BSigma$

The metric setup

The generalised building $\BSigma = G \times^K A$ inherits a metric structure facet-by-facet from its apartments. Concretely:

  • Each apartment $gA \subseteq \BSigma$ ($g \in G$) is a copy of the Euclidean polysimplicial complex $A$, carrying the standard Euclidean metric $d_{gA}$ (transported from $d_A$ via $g$). By Apartments of B(Σ): definition, Stab_G(A), and parametrization of apartments through x every apartment is closed in $\BSigma$ and the action of $G$ is by isometries between apartments.
  • A path $\gamma : [0,1] \to \BSigma$ is admissible if it factors as a finite concatenation of straight line segments, each contained in some apartment. The length of an admissible path is the sum of its segment lengths.
  • The path metric $d_C(x, y)$ on a connected component $C \subseteq \BSigma$ is the infimum of lengths of admissible paths from $x$ to $y$ within $C$.

A geodesic from $x$ to $y$ is an admissible path realising $d_C(x, y)$.

Three hypotheses

Throughout we work on a connected component $C \subseteq \BSigma$ and consider three hypotheses:

(G1) Common-apartment property. Any two points of $C$ lie in a common apartment $A' \subseteq C$. Equivalently, the component-global form of (A1) ([[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]]).

(G2) Convex apartment intersections. For any two apartments $g_1 A, g_2 A \subseteq C$, the intersection $g_1 A \cap g_2 A$ is convex (in the Euclidean metric of either apartment).

(G3) Simple connectedness. $\pi_1(C) = 1$.

Step 1: apartment distance equals component distance

Lemma (apartment distance is intrinsic). Assume (G1) and (G2). For any apartment $A' \subseteq C$ and any $x, y \in A'$: $$d_C(x, y) = d_{A'}(x, y).$$ In particular $A' \hookrightarrow C$ is an isometric embedding.

Proof

$d_C \le d_{A'}$ is trivial (the Euclidean segment $[x,y]_{A'}$ is an admissible path). For $d_{A'} \le d_C$: any admissible path $\gamma$ from $x$ to $y$ in $C$ is a finite concatenation of segments $[p_{i-1}, p_i]_{A_i}$. By (G1), each pair $(p_{i-1}, p_i)$ lies in a common apartment with $A'$; by (G2), $A_i \cap A'$ is convex in both, so each segment can be transported to $A'$ without changing its length. Then the polygonal path in $A'$ has length $\ge d_{A'}(x, y)$ by Euclidean triangle inequality.

Step 2: geodesics are unique and live in any common apartment

Proposition (geodesics). Assume (G1), (G2). For any $x, y \in C$: (i) the Euclidean segment $[x, y]_{A'}$ in any apartment $A' \ni x, y$ is a geodesic from $x$ to $y$ in $C$; (ii) all such segments are equal as subsets of $\BSigma$ (independent of $A'$); (iii) any geodesic from $x$ to $y$ in $C$ coincides with this Euclidean segment. In particular, geodesics exist and are unique.

Proof. (i) by Step 1. (ii) by (G2) convexity of $A'_1 \cap A'_2$ and Euclidean uniqueness in the common convex subspace. (iii) Suppose $\gamma$ leaves $A'$ at time $t_1$ and re-enters at $t_2$. Let $p = \gamma(t_1), q = \gamma(t_2)$. Length of $\gamma|_{[t_1,t_2]}$ is $\ge d_C(p,q) = d_{A'}(p,q)$ by Step 1. Replacing this piece by $[p,q]_{A'}$: - Case A (strict inequality): new path is shorter, contradicting $\gamma$ geodesic; - Case B (equality): $\gamma|_{[t_1,t_2]}$ is itself a geodesic from $p$ to $q$ of the same length as $[p,q]_{A'}$; by Euclidean uniqueness in $A'$, $\gamma|_{[t_1,t_2]} = [p,q]_{A'}$, contradicting that $\gamma$ leaves $A'$.

Iterating over all leave-times, $\gamma \subseteq A'$, and Euclidean uniqueness gives $\gamma = [x, y]_{A'}$. $\square$

Step 3: $C$ is locally CAT(0)

Lemma (local CAT(0)). Assume (G1), (G2). Every point $p \in C$ has an open neighbourhood $U \subseteq C$ that is CAT(0) in the induced metric.

Proof. Pick an apartment $A_p \ni p$. The open star of $p$ in $A_p$ is a convex open neighbourhood of $p$ in $A_p$; by Step 1 it is also an open neighbourhood of $p$ in $C$, isometric to the same open convex Euclidean set. Convex open subsets of Euclidean spaces are CAT(0). $\square$

Step 4: CAT(0) via Cartan–Hadamard

Theorem (CAT(0) structure). Assume (G1), (G2), (G3). Then $C$ is a complete CAT(0) metric space.

Proof. Completeness: $C$ is a connected component of a locally finite CW complex with a length-space structure; the metric $d_C$ is complete because apartments are complete Euclidean and intersections preserve completeness via Step 1. Local CAT(0): Step 3. Simply connected: (G3). By the Cartan–Hadamard theorem for CAT(0) spaces ([Bridson–Haefliger, Metric spaces of non-positive curvature, Theorem II.4.1]), a complete, locally CAT(0), simply connected metric space is globally CAT(0). $\square$

Status under the Lanard cartesian framework

The cartesian-cosheaf framework of Cartesian cosheaves Cosh^cart_G(X,R,φ) and unit isomorphism does not require uniqueness of geodesics or the CAT(0) property. It works with the chain complex $C_\bullet(\BSigma, \sV)$, idempotent identities, and the QFD-style apartment argument. So (G1)(G2)(G3) and the CAT(0) Theorem are a natural geometric companion to the main theorems but are not used in (CE) (Cartesian cosheaves Cosh^cart_G(X,R,φ) and unit isomorphism) or (RD0) (i_x: C_d(B(Σ),𝒱)_x ≅ C_d(B(M),𝒲)_x).

The geometric package is used in the older contraction-based §7.1 resolution argument (via Bestvina–Savin's PL contraction of $A$ extending across apartments under (K3)). The Lanard switch lets us bypass this entirely; see [[node:building.contraction_extension_K3]] for the legacy approach.

Open questions

  1. (G2) for abstract $K$-cosheaves. What conditions on $K$ guarantee convexity of apartment intersections? Is there a clean axiomatic statement (analogous to (K3) but for apartment intersections)?

  2. (G3) for abstract $K$-cosheaves. Is simple-connectedness of components an automatic consequence of (T1)–(T5)+(K1)–(K4), or genuinely independent?

  3. Relation to (K3). The convexity hypothesis (K3) on fixators ([[node:building.K3_hypothesis]]) is a different, weaker convexity statement. Does (K3) + (G1) imply (G2), or are they independent?
