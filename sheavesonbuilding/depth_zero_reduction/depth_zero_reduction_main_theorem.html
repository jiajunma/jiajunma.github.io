Uses

  • Res sends E_Φ to E_M (Prop 8.1)
  • Res and Sat are quasi-inverse: Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) ≃ Coeff_M⁰(B(M)) (Prop 8.2)
  • Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ)[E_Φ⁻¹] ≃ Rep_Φ(G) (§7.4 Thm)
  • Cosh_G(X,R,φ)[Σ⁻¹] ≃ Rep_φ(G) (§3 Cor)
  • Serre-subcategory property (S) — a consequence, not a hypothesis

Main Theorem 8.2.1 — Reduction to depth zero

Theorem (Sheaf7 Thm 8.2.1, compare [Schneider–Stuhler, Thm V.1]). The following diagram induces an equivalence of categories between $\Rep_\Phi(G)$ and $\Rep_0(M)$: $$\Rep_\Phi(G) \xleftarrow{\;H_0\;} \Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)[E_\Phi^{-1}] \;\xrightarrow{\;\Res\;}\; \Coeff_M^0(\mathcal{B}(M))[E_M^{-1}] \;\xrightarrow{\;H_0\;}\; \Rep_0(M).$$

Proof

Three ingredients combine:

  1. Two horizontal localized equivalences. The leftmost arrow is the §7.4 equivalence Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ)[E_Φ⁻¹] ≃ Rep_Φ(G) (§7.4 Thm) for $\Phi$ on $\BSigma$; the rightmost arrow is the same for the depth-zero data on $\mathcal{B}(M)$ (the Schneider–Stuhler case).
  2. Coefficient-system equivalence. $\Res \dashv \Sat$ are quasi-inverse on the unlocalized categories Res and Sat are quasi-inverse: Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) ≃ Coeff_M⁰(B(M)) (Prop 8.2).
  3. Matching of weak equivalences. $\Res(E_\Phi) \subseteq E_M$ and $\Sat(E_M) \subseteq E_\Phi$ by [[node:depth_zero_reduction.res_preserves_E]], so the equivalence descends to the localized categories $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)[E_\Phi^{-1}] \simeq \Coeff_M^0(\mathcal{B}(M))[E_M^{-1}]$.

Compose. $\square$

Hypotheses required

The (T·), (E·), (K·) axioms split into two groups: framework (needed to define the relevant categories) and directly used (consumed in the §8.2 proof above).

Hypothesis Role Verified in
(T1) framework — facet-constancy of $\mathscr K_+, \phi$ (defines $K_{\mathcal F,+}$ per facet) [[node:coefficient_systems.semisimple_character_T_axioms]]
(T2) framework — restriction compatibility (defines restrictions in $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$) same
(T3) directly used — $G$-equivariance, called in the §7.1 resolution + the support lemma same
(T4) directly used — intertwiner, called in the support lemma + the cartesian unit-iso same
(T5) directly used — geodesic factorization, called in the idempotent (iii) for cartesian same
(S) directly used — $\Rep_\Phi(G)$ Serre subcategory of $\Rep(G)$ (called in §7.1 resolution) Serre-subcategory property (S) — a consequence, not a hypothesis
(E1)–(E4) directly used — coherent $\kappa$-extensions, called in §8.1 Res/Sat [[node:depth_zero_reduction.hypothesis_E]]
(K3) directly used — fixator convexity, called in the contraction extension to $\BSigma_x$ [[node:building.K3_hypothesis]]
(K4) directly used — reductive-quotient iso, called in §8.1 quasi-inverse [[node:depth_zero_reduction.K4_hypothesis]]

Bourbaki-style remark. (T1) and (T2) are framework assumptions (without them, $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$ does not make sense as a category). Inside the §8.2 chain, only (T3), (T4), (T5), (S), (E1)–(E4), (K3), (K4) appear directly. (T1) and (T2) cannot be dropped because they are part of the setup, but it is worth noting that they are not active hypotheses of the §8.2 argument.

Applications

The theorem applies in two situations:

  • Classical groups (§9, $p \ne 2$): see Main theorem applied to classical groups;
  • Tame types (§10, $p$ large): see Main theorem applied to tame types.
