Global β-extension existence

The pointwise data $x \mapsto (K_x, K_{x,+}, \phi_x, \kappa_x)$ constructed in [[node:applications.tame_K_F_construction]] and the parallel classical construction are defined vertex-by-vertex. To assemble them into a single $G$-equivariant family — required by (T1) constancy on facets, (T3) $G$-equivariance, and the global definition $\phi_x = \phi(K_{x,+}, \kappa_x)$ used in [[node:coefficient_systems.semisimple_character_T_axioms]] — one needs the β-extensions $\kappa_x$ to be compatible across $x$.

For quaternionic forms of $p$-adic classical groups (and by analogous arguments for inner forms and twisted Levi situations), this global compatibility is addressed by the Skodlerack–Ye preprint; see Skodlerack–Ye: compatible families of β-extensions (Thm 5.21) for the original statement and proof.

⚠️ Preprint status. The Skodlerack–Ye paper is not yet published or formally refereed. The construction below is the most complete argument currently available; earlier partial results in [Stevens:SC §4.2] (local existence), [Sko:SS] (intertwiner identity), [KSS21] (endo-parameters with $\beta$-extension data), and [HKSS §7] (the conjecture being closed) provide the surrounding context. Downstream nodes that cite this theorem are qualified as "preprint-conditional".

Stevens-side literature trail

The chain of papers leading to the SkoYe statement is:

  • [Stevens:SC] — original construction of $\beta$-extensions for classical groups (single-vertex case);
  • [Sko:SS] (Skodlerack–Stevens) — refined intertwiner identity for semisimple characters;
  • [KSS21] (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens) — endo-parameters, including a compatible-$\beta$-extension framework over a fixed maximal Levi;
  • [HKSS] §7 (Conj 7.8) — explicit formulation of the global compatibility as a conjecture;
  • [SkoYe] (preprint) — resolution of the conjecture in the quaternionic case via the quadratic-correction construction below.

The general (non-quaternionic) classical case is expected to follow by the same technique, but is not yet documented in print.

Statement in our notation

Fix a self-dual semisimple stratum $\Delta = [\Lambda, n, 0, \beta]$ and a self-dual semisimple character $\theta \in \mathrm C(\Delta)$. Let $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$ be the Bruhat–Tits building of the centralizer, and fix a chamber $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal B(G_\beta)$.

Theorem (global β-extension family). There exists a family $$\bigl\{\tilde\kappa^\circ_x \;\big|\; x \in \overline{\mathcal C}\bigr\}, \qquad > \tilde\kappa^\circ_x \in \beta\text{-ext}^\circ_{\Lambda_{M_0}}(\Lambda_x),$$ such that for all $\Lambda_x, \Lambda_y \in \overline{\mathcal C}$ with $\bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda) \subseteq \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_x) \cap \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_y)$, $$\Psi^\circ_{\Lambda, \Lambda_x, \Lambda_y}\!\Bigl(\mathrm{Res}^{J^\circ(\Lambda_x)}_{J^\circ_{\Lambda, \Lambda_x}} \tilde\kappa^\circ_x\Bigr) > \;\cong\; > \mathrm{Res}^{J^\circ(\Lambda_y)}_{J^\circ_{\Lambda, \Lambda_y}} \tilde\kappa^\circ_y.$$

Equivalently, the local $\kappa$-data glue into a single $G_\beta$-equivariant coefficient system of β-extensions on the closed chamber $\overline{\mathcal C}$, hence (via the $G_\beta$-action on chambers) on all of $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$.

(This is [SkoYe Theorem 5.21] in their notation; the extension to barycentre / midpoint-of-vertex points needed for non-vertex $x$ is §5.4, especially Propositions 5.25 and 5.27 in the GL_m(D) case.)

Construction of the family — outline

The construction is a twisting procedure: start from any β-extension, then correct it by an explicit quadratic character.

Step 1 — base point.

Fix an arbitrary vertex $\Lambda_{M_0} \in \overline{\mathcal C}$ and pick the unique ORD-$p$ β-extension $\kappa^\circ_{M_0} \in \beta\text{-ext}^\circ(\Lambda_{M_0})$ ([Sko23 Lem 9.5] (maximal-order case $M_\Lambda = \Lambda$, corresponding to a vertex)).

Step 2 — transfer to arbitrary $x \in \overline{\mathcal C}$.

For $x \in \overline{\mathcal C}$ with $\bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_x) \subseteq \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_{M_0})$, set $\kappa^\circ_{M_0, x} := \mathrm{Res}^{J^\circ(\Lambda_{M_0})}_{J^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_{M_0}}} \kappa^\circ_{M_0}$, then transfer via $\Psi^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_{M_0}, \Lambda_x}$ to obtain $\kappa^\circ_x \in \beta\text{-ext}^\circ_{\Lambda_{M_0}}(\Lambda_x)$ satisfying ORD-$p$.

Step 3 — quadratic correction $\chi^\circ_x$ (Construction 5.18).

Define $\chi^\circ_x := \chi^\circ_{x,1} \cdot \chi^\circ_{x,2} \cdot \chi^\circ_{x,3}$ where:

  • $\chi^\circ_{x,1}: J^\circ(\Lambda_x) \to \{\pm 1\}$ is the signature of the conjugation action $\sigma_g$ on the coset $\mathrm J^1(\Lambda_x) \backslash \mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_x)$ for $g \in J^\circ(\Lambda_x)$.

  • $\chi^\circ_{x,2}: J^\circ(\Lambda_x) \to \{\pm 1\}$ comes from the quadratic character $\bar\chi^\circ_{x,2}$ on the finite reductive quotient $\mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_x)/\mathrm P_1(\Lambda_x)$ (existence and uniqueness: §5.2 + Prop 5.5), inflated to $\mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_x)$ and then to $J^\circ(\Lambda_x)$.

  • $\chi^\circ_{x,3}: J^\circ(\Lambda_x) \to \{\pm 1\}$ is the analog of $\chi^\circ_{x,2}$ for the β-reduction quotient $\mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_{x, \beta})/\mathrm P_1(\Lambda_{x, \beta}) \cong \mathbb H_3 \times \mathbb H_4$.

Step 4 — final β-extension.

Put $$\tilde\kappa^\circ_x \;:=\; \kappa^\circ_x \otimes \chi^\circ_x.$$ Then $\tilde\kappa^\circ_x \in \beta\text{-ext}^\circ_{\Lambda_{M_0}}(\Lambda_x)$ and is, by Prop 5.20, the unique element of $\beta\text{-ext}^\circ$ making the diagram of transfers commute (up to isomorphism) for all pairs $(x, y)$.

Why the proof closes

The compatibility identity of Theorem 5.21 reduces (via the (ORD-$p$) hypothesis and [Sko23 Lemma 9.6]) to computing a discrepancy character $\nu^\circ_{x,y} := \det(\pi_x)/\det(\pi_y)$ on $J^\circ_{\Lambda, \Lambda_y}$. Skodlerack–Ye Prop 5.20 expresses $\nu^\circ_{x,y}(g) = (-1)^{e_{x,y}(g)}$ where $e_{x,y}(g)$ is an integer-valued index counting orbits of $\langle g \rangle$ on various double cosets $\mathrm J^\circ \backslash \mathrm P^\circ$. Using the orbit-counting identity (5) [equivariant decompositions of finite $H$-sets give additive orbit-count formulas mod 2] applied repeatedly, $e_{x,y}$ collapses modulo 2 to $e_x(g) - e_y(g)$, exactly matching the construction of $\chi^\circ_x$ and $\chi^\circ_y$. Hence $\nu^\circ_{x,y} = \chi^\circ_x (\chi^\circ_y)^{-1}$, which is the desired compatibility (after multiplying through by $\chi^\circ_x, \chi^\circ_y$ in the definition $\tilde\kappa = \kappa \otimes \chi$).

The §5.4 extension from chamber vertices to all rational points in $\overline{\mathcal C}$ uses the strong transitivity of $G_\beta$ on chambers (Lemma 5.24) plus a uniqueness argument: any two β-extensions in $\beta\text{-ext}(\Lambda_j)$ satisfying ORD-$p$ are equal, so transport-of-structure along path-connectedness in $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$ propagates the family.

Application: globalization of the K-cosheaf

The family $\{\tilde\kappa^\circ_x\}$ is the missing piece that turns the pointwise tame/classical Yu/AFMO data $x \mapsto (K_x, K_{x,+}, \phi_x, \kappa_x)$ ([[node:applications.tame_K_F_construction]]) into an actual $G$-equivariant $K$-cosheaf on $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$ (and, via the $G$-equivariant embedding $j_\beta: \mathcal B(G_\beta) \hookrightarrow \mathcal B(G)$, on the image in $\mathcal B(G)$). With this in place:

  1. (T1) facet-constancy holds because $\tilde\kappa^\circ_x$ depends only on the facet of $x$ in $\overline{\mathcal C}$ (Prop 5.27: rational points in the same facet give isomorphic β-extensions).

  2. (T3) $G$-equivariance holds because the family is $G_\beta$-equivariant on $\overline{\mathcal C}$ by construction (uniqueness of ORD-$p$ plus equivariant transfer), and $G$ acts on $\mathcal B(G) \supseteq j_\beta(\mathcal B(G_\beta))$ by isometries compatible with $G_\beta$-translation.

  3. (T4) intertwining reduces (via Prop 5.20) to the compatibility identity, which is exactly the content of the family being compatible.

The dependence chain into the §7.2 cartesian unit-iso proof is: $$\text{Skodlerack–Ye Thm 5.21} \longrightarrow \text{(T1)–(T4) for classical/tame} \longrightarrow \text{idempotent system (i)–(v)} \longrightarrow \text{§7.2 cartesian unit-iso}.$$

Editorial note

Until April 2026, the §7.2 blueprint treated "global β-extension existence" as a hypothesis listed alongside (T1)–(T5) without a specific reference. With the Skodlerack–Ye preprint now available (see Skodlerack–Ye: compatible families of β-extensions (Thm 5.21)), this is a preprint-conditional theorem in the quaternionic case: the proof technique (correction-by-quadratic-character via finite-reductive-group signature) is documented and should extend to the general classical case (and to inner forms / twisted Levi situations) by the same argument; an explicit write-up of that extension is not yet available.

For users who want a fully refereed dependency, the best current combination is:

  • [Stevens:SC §4.2] + [KSS21] for the single-vertex / single-Levi part;
  • [HKSS Conj 8.14] explicitly stated as the missing global compatibility;
  • citation of [SkoYe, preprint] for the closure in the quaternionic case, with a note that the general extension is in progress.
