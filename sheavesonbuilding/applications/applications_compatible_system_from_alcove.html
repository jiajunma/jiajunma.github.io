Construction of the compatible cosheaf $\mathscr K$ from a pointwise $\{\kappa_E\}$

This is the constructive companion to the §4 abstract Heisenberg–Weil equivalence (Heisenberg–Weil equivalence Cosh_G(X,phi) ≃ Cosh_G(X,1_{QJ})): given a pointwise compatible family $\{\kappa_E\}_E$ of irreducible representations satisfying the type axioms, it builds an explicit $N$-equivariant cosheaf $\mathscr K$ on the generalized building $\BSigma$. The construction uses the alcove fundamental domain (Quasi-fundamental domain from a closed alcove (App B)) plus a free-abelian extension of the action of $\Omega = N_{\mathcal C'}$.

Setting

We work with two cosheaves / sheaves of subgroups of $G$ on $X = \BSigma$:

  • $J$ a cosheaf of subgroups, with $J_E \trianglelefteq \Fix_G(E)$ for all $E \in [\![X]\!]$.
  • $Q$ a sheaf of subgroups, with $Q_E \trianglelefteq \Fix_G(E)$ for all $E$.
  • $Q$ normalizes $J$: $g J_E g^{-1} = J_E$ for $g \in Q_E$.
  • Set $K_{E,+} := Q_E \cdot J_E$ (the "type group" at $E$).
  • Intersection formula: $K_{x,+} \cap K_{E,+} = (Q_x \cap Q_E)(J_x \cap J_E)$ for all $x \in X$ and $E \in [\![X]\!]$.

We are given a family $\{\kappa_E\}_E$ of irreducible representations of $\{K_{E,+}\}_E$ satisfying:

  • One-dimensional intertwiner: $\dim \Hom_{K_{x,+} \cap K_{E,+}}(\kappa_x, \kappa_E) = 1$ for all $x \in X$, $E \in [\![X]\!]$.

The goal is to upgrade this pointwise data to a cosheaf $\mathscr K : [\![X]\!] \to \mathrm{Rep}(R)$ with $\mathscr K_E$ carrying the type $\kappa_E$ as a $K_{E,+}$-module, equipped with an $N$-equivariant structure $a^\mathscr K_{n, E} : \mathscr K_E \to {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE}$.

The alcove setup

Let $T \subseteq G$ be a maximal $F$-split torus, $N = N(G, T)$, $A$ the apartment of the extended building. Let $A' = A / A(Z_G)$ be the reduced apartment, $\mathrm{pr} : A \to A'$ the projection. Fix:

  • An alcove $\mathcal C'$ in $A'$.
  • $\mathcal C := \mathrm{pr}^{-1}(\mathcal C') \subseteq A$.
  • $\Omega := \mathrm{stab}_N(\mathcal C')$ — the stabilizer in $N$.
  • $\Omega_0 := \Omega \cap G^0(F)$ — the preimage of the torsion part of $\Omega / T_0$.
  • A decomposition $\Omega = \Omega_0 \times \Omega_1$ with $\Omega_1$ free abelian.
  • A point $x_0 \in \mathcal C$ fixed by $\Omega_0$.

By Quasi-fundamental domain from a closed alcove (App B), $\mathcal C$ is a quasi-fundamental domain for the $G$-action on $\BSigma$ with stabilizing subgroup $N$ (and $\Omega_1$ acts freely).

Main theorem

Theorem (Sheaf7 §12.2). Under the assumptions above, there exists a cosheaf $\mathscr K$ on $\BSigma$ with $\mathscr K_E$ of type $\kappa_E$ for each $E$, together with an $N$-equivariant structure $a^\mathscr K_{n, E} : \mathscr K_E \to {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE}$ satisfying the cocycle identity $a^\mathscr K_{n, hE} \circ a^\mathscr K_{h, E} = a^\mathscr K_{nh, E}$ for $n, h \in N$ and all facets $E$.

The construction proceeds in three steps.

Step 1 — Restriction maps on $\mathcal C$ via $\mathscr I_E$

Let $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}(\mathscr K)$ be the set of collections $\{r^\mathscr K_{F, E} \in \Hom_{K_{F,+} \cap K_{E,+}}(\kappa_F, \kappa_E) \mid E \preceq F\}$ satisfying the cocycle $r^\mathscr K_{E, D} \circ r^\mathscr K_{F, E} = r^\mathscr K_{F, D}$ for $D \preceq E \preceq F$.

Define $$\mathscr I_E \;:=\; \bigl( (\mathscr K_{x_0}^* \otimes \mathscr K_E)_{J_{x_0} \cap J_E} \bigr)^{Q_{x_0} \cap Q_E}.$$

For $E \preceq F$, there is a natural isomorphism $$e_{F, E} \colon \Hom_{K_{F,+} \cap K_{E,+}}(\mathscr K_F, \mathscr K_E) \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; \Hom(\mathscr I_F, \mathscr I_E),$$ given on classes by $$e_{F, E}(r)\bigl([{\textstyle\sum_i} v_0 \otimes v_i]_{J_{x_0} \cap J_F}\bigr) \;=\; [{\textstyle\sum_i} v_0 \otimes r(v_i)]_{J_{x_0} \cap J_E}.$$

Claim. For a collection $\{r_{F, E}\}$, the cocycle $r_{E, D} \circ r_{F, E} = r_{F, D}$ holds iff $e(r_{E, D}) \circ e(r_{F, E}) = e(r_{F, D})$.

Proof of claim. Both sides differ by a scalar (since each $\Hom$-space is one-dimensional); the $e$ isomorphism transports the scalar identically. $\square$

Existence of a coherent collection. Since $\mathcal C$ is simply connected, one can globally choose the $\{r_{F, E}\}$ to satisfy the cocycle. Two such choices differ by a collection of $\mathscr K_E$-isomorphisms $\{i_E : \mathscr K_E \to \mathscr K_E\}$, so $\mathcal R_{\mathcal C}(\mathscr K)$ is non-empty and unique up to canonical isomorphism.

Step 2 — $\Omega_0$-equivariance via the diagram

For each $E$ in $\mathcal C$, the chosen restriction $r_{x_0 \to E}$ (transported through the $r_{F, E}$-collection) gives an iso $$\mathscr I_{x_0} = \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+}}(\mathscr K_{x_0}, \mathscr K_{x_0})_{J_{x_0}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\; \mathscr I_E = \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+} \cap K_{E,0+}}(\mathscr K_{x_0}, \mathscr K_E).$$

Let $c_E$ be the image of $1 \in \mathscr I_{x_0}$ under this iso.

For $n \in \Omega_0$ (which stabilizes $x_0$), consider the commutative diagram (with $(c_E)^*$ denoting pre-composition by $c_E$ and $(c_E)_*$ post-composition):

$$\begin{array}{ccc} \Hom_{K_{E,0+}}(\mathscr K_E, {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE}) & \xrightarrow[\cong]{(c_E)^*} & \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+} \cap K_{E,0+}}(\mathscr K_{x_0}, {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE}) \\ & & \parallel \\ \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+}}({}^n \mathscr K_{x_0}, \mathscr K_{x_0}) & \xrightarrow[\cong]{(c_{nE})_*} & \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+} \cap K_{nE,0+}}({}^n \mathscr K_{x_0}, \mathscr K_{nE}) \end{array}$$

Define $$a^\mathscr K_{n, E} \;:=\; (c_E^*)^{-1}\bigl((c_{nE})_*(a^\mathscr K_{x_0}(n))\bigr) \;\in\; \Hom_{K_{E,0+}}(\mathscr K_E, {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE}),$$ where $a^\mathscr K_{x_0}(n) \in \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+}}({}^n \mathscr K_{x_0}, \mathscr K_{x_0})$ is the given $\Omega_0$-action at $x_0$.

Cocycle identity. $a^\mathscr K_{n, hE} \circ a^\mathscr K_{h, E} = a^\mathscr K_{nh, E}$ for $n, h \in \Omega_0$.

Proof of cocycle. Apply $(c_E)^*$ to both sides; the definition unwinds (using $((c_E)^*)^{-1}(T) \circ c_E = T$ for any $T \in \Hom_{K_{x_0, 0+} \cap K_{E,0+}}(\mathscr K_{x_0}, {}^{n^{-1}}\mathscr K_{nE})$): $$(c_E)^*\bigl(a^\mathscr K_{n, hE} \circ a^\mathscr K_{h, E}\bigr) \;=\; c_{nhE} \circ a^\mathscr K_{x_0}(n) \circ a^\mathscr K_{x_0}(h) \;=\; c_{nhE} \circ a^\mathscr K_{x_0}(nh).$$ Since $(c_E)^*$ is an iso, the cocycle holds. $\square$

Step 3 — Extension to $\Omega_1$ via the free-abelian basis

$\Omega_1$ is free abelian of finite rank; pick a basis $n_1, \dots, n_k$.

For each generator $n_i$: since $\Omega_1$ acts freely on $\mathcal C$ (by Quasi-fundamental domain from a closed alcove (App B)), the sheaf $E \mapsto {}^{n_i^{-1}}\mathscr K_{n_i E}$ on $\mathcal C$ has the same type structure as $\mathscr K$. By Step 1 applied to this twisted sheaf, $a^\mathscr K_{n_i, E}$ is determined up to a single global scalar; fix a choice.

For general $n = \sum_i k_i n_i \in \Omega_1$, $$a^\mathscr K_{n, E} \;:=\; \prod_{i=1}^k \bigl(a^\mathscr K_{n_i, E}\bigr)^{k_i}.$$

The cocycle identity for $\Omega_1$ follows from the free-abelian relations; commutativity reduces to the $\Omega_0$-case in Step 2 applied to the twisted sheaves.

Combining Steps 2 and 3 with $\Omega = \Omega_0 \times \Omega_1$ and extending $G$-equivariantly to $\BSigma = G \times^N \mathcal C$ (via the QFD property) gives the full $N$-equivariant structure. $\square$

Where this is used

This construction is the explicit-cosheaf counterpart to the global β-extension family of Global β-extension existence (compatible κ-family on B(G_β)): - Compatible β-extension family (Skodlerack–Ye) tells us that a pointwise $\{\tilde\kappa^\circ_x\}$ exists with the right compatibility. - The §12.2 construction here assembles that pointwise data into the actual $G$-equivariant cosheaf $\mathscr K$ needed by the §4 Heisenberg–Weil equivalence (Heisenberg–Weil equivalence Cosh_G(X,phi) ≃ Cosh_G(X,1_{QJ})) and hence by the §8 Res/Sat (Res and Sat are quasi-inverse: Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) ≃ Coeff_M⁰(B(M)) (Prop 8.2)).

Remarks

  • The construction is reminiscent of the Mackey machine (extending an $\Omega_0$-action on $\mathscr K_{x_0}$ to all of $\Omega$ via Clifford theory), but here applied at the cosheaf level rather than the representation level.
  • The role of $\mathscr I_E$ is to control the scalar ambiguity in the one-dim Hom spaces: $\mathscr I_E \cong R$ canonically (one-dim), and the maps $e_{F, E}$ make the cocycle pinning explicit.
  • The free abelian extension to $\Omega_1$ requires choosing one scalar per generator $n_i$; different choices give isomorphic cosheaves but not equal ones (a torsor for $\Omega_1^*$ acting on $\mathscr K$).
