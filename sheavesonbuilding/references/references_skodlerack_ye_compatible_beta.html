#

Skodlerack–Ye: compatible families of β-extensions

⚠️ Preprint status. D. Skodlerack & S. Ye, Semisimple types for quaternionic forms of p-adic classical groups — preprint, not yet published or formally refereed. All citations in this KB that rely on Thm 5.21, Prop 5.20, or Constr 5.18 inherit this preprint status. Until publication, results downstream that depend on this paper are qualified as "modulo [SkoYe preprint]" rather than fully unconditional.

Reference: D. Skodlerack & S. Ye, Semisimple types for quaternionic forms of p-adic classical groups (preprint).

Setup

Let $G$ be a quaternionic form of a $p$-adic classical group ($p$ odd), fix a self-dual semisimple stratum $\Delta = [\Lambda, n, 0, \beta]$ and a self-dual semisimple character $\theta \in C(\Delta)$. The centralizer $G_\beta$ has its own Bruhat–Tits building $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$.

A β-extension of the Heisenberg representation $\eta_\Lambda$ (the unique irreducible representation of $J^1(\beta, \Lambda)$ containing $\theta$) to $J(\beta, \Lambda)$ relative to a lattice sequence $\Lambda_M$ (whose associated point $\Lambda_{M,\beta}$ is a vertex of the chamber $\mathcal C$ containing $\Lambda_\beta$) is constructed in [Sko23 §6]. The set of such β-extensions is $\beta\text{-ext}_{\Lambda_M}(\Lambda)$; its restriction to $J^\circ$ is $\beta\text{-ext}^\circ_{\Lambda_M}(\Lambda)$.

Statement of the main result

Theorem (Skodlerack–Ye 5.21). Fix an arbitrary chamber $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal B(G_\beta)$ and a base self-dual lattice sequence $\Upsilon$ corresponding to a point in $\mathcal C$, together with a semisimple character $\theta_\Upsilon \in C(\Lambda, 0, \beta)$. For each $x \in \overline{\mathcal C}$ there is an explicit β-extension $\tilde\kappa^\circ_x \in \beta\text{-ext}^\circ(\Lambda_x)$ (built from any ORD-$p$ β-extension at a chosen vertex $\Lambda_{M_0}$, then twisted by the quadratic correction $\chi^\circ_x$ of Construction 5.18), such that the family $$\bigl\{\tilde\kappa^\circ_x \;\big|\; x \in \overline{\mathcal C}\bigr\}$$ is a compatible family of β-extensions, i.e. for any two vertices $\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2 \in \overline{\mathcal C}$ with $\bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_x) \subseteq \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_1) \cap \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_2)$ (so the transfers $\Psi^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_i, \Lambda_x}$ are defined): $$\Psi^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_1, \Lambda_x}\!\Bigl(\mathrm{Res}^{J^\circ(\Lambda_1)}_{J^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_1}} \tilde\kappa^\circ_1\Bigr) > \;\cong\; > \Psi^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_2, \Lambda_x}\!\Bigl(\mathrm{Res}^{J^\circ(\Lambda_2)}_{J^\circ_{\Lambda_x, \Lambda_2}} \tilde\kappa^\circ_2\Bigr).$$

Key technical ingredients

  1. §5.1–§5.2 (Signature characters of finite reductive groups). For each finite reductive group $\mathbb H$ of type $\mathrm{Sp}_{2r}$, $\mathrm{SO}(r, r_{\mathrm{an}})$, $\mathrm U_{2r}(\ell/k)$, $\mathrm U_{2r+1}(\ell/k)$, etc., a quadratic character $\chi: \mathbb H \to \{\pm 1\}$ is constructed extending the signature of the conjugation action on the unipotent radical of any Borel subgroup (Proposition 5.5; uniqueness from semisimplicity of $\mathbb H$; case-by-case existence via the determinant / spinor-norm / Norm$_{\ell/k}$).

  2. §5.3 Construction 5.18 (quadratic correction). Given an arbitrary ORD-$p$ β-extension $\kappa^\circ_{M_0, x}$ obtained from a vertex $\Lambda_{M_0}$, define a quadratic character $\chi^\circ_x: J^\circ(\Lambda_x) \to \{\pm 1\}$ as the product $\chi^\circ_x = \chi^\circ_{x,1} \cdot \chi^\circ_{x,2} \cdot \chi^\circ_{x,3}$ of three pieces:

  3. $\chi^\circ_{x,1}$: signature of conjugation on the coset $\mathrm J^1(\Lambda_x) \backslash \mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_x)$;
  4. $\chi^\circ_{x,2}$: inflation of the §5.2 quadratic character on $\mathrm P^\circ(\Lambda_x)/\mathrm P_1(\Lambda_x)$;

  5. $\chi^\circ_{x,3}$: same recipe on the β-reduction. Then put $\tilde\kappa^\circ_x := \kappa^\circ_x \otimes \chi^\circ_x$.

  6. §5.3 Prop 5.20 (compatibility identity). For any self-dual lattice sequences $\Lambda, \Lambda_x, \Lambda_y$ with $\bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda) \subseteq \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_x) \cap \bar{\mathfrak b}(\Lambda_y)$, $$\Psi^\circ_{\Lambda, \Lambda_x, \Lambda_y}\bigl(\mathrm{Res}\, \tilde\kappa^\circ_x\bigr) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Res}\, \tilde\kappa^\circ_y.$$ Proof traces the discrepancy $\nu^\circ_{x,y}$ to a sign $(-1)^{e_{x,y}(g)}$ computed from the parities of orbit counts of $\langle g\rangle$ acting on cosets $\mathrm J^\circ \backslash \mathrm P^\circ$ etc.; (5)+(8) reduce $e_{x,y}$ modulo 2 to a sum of three terms each matching one of $\chi^\circ_{x,1}, \chi^\circ_{x,2}, \chi^\circ_{x,3}$ of Construction 5.18. The cancellation $e_{x,y} \equiv e_x + e_y \pmod 2$ then forces $\nu^\circ_{x,y} = \chi^\circ_x (\chi^\circ_y)^{-1}$ on $J^\circ(\Lambda_x) \cap J^\circ(\Lambda_y)$, which is exactly the compatibility statement.

  7. §5.4 Linear group case (extension from barycentres to midpoints). Lemma 5.22, Notation 5.23, Lemma 5.24, Prop 5.25, Lemma 5.26, and Prop 5.27 promote the compatibility from vertices of $\mathcal C$ to rational points in facets whose closure contains given vertices.

Why this matters for our setup

In the Sheaf7.tex §10 tame construction ([[node:applications.tame_K_F_construction]]) and the §9 classical construction, the pointwise data $(K_x, K_{x,+}, \phi_x, \kappa_x)$ is defined vertex-by-vertex. The hypothesis (T3) ($G$-equivariance of $\sK_+$ and $\phi$) and the global definition of the type character $\phi_x = \phi(K_{x,+}, \kappa_x)$ on every point $x \in \BSigma$ require that the local $\kappa_x$ assemble into a single coherent family.

For quaternionic forms of classical groups (and by analogous arguments for the inner forms and twisted Levi situations), Theorem 5.21 produces the required global $\kappa$-family on the closed chamber $\overline{\mathcal C}$ of $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$; translates over the $G_\beta$-action then cover all of $\mathcal B(G_\beta)$, and the $G$-stable image $j_\beta(\mathcal B(G_\beta)) \subseteq \mathcal B(G)$ embeds this family equivariantly into the building of $G$.

This justifies the "global β-extension existence" used implicitly in: - [[node:applications.classical_T_axioms]] (verification of (T1)–(T5) for classical groups, especially the $G$-equivariance in (T3)). - [[node:applications.tame_T_axioms]] and [[node:applications.tame_K_F_construction]] (the tame analogue; AFMO-type Heisenberg–Weil twists provide the $\kappa_x$ data).
