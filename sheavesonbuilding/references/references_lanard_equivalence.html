#

Lanard's depth-zero block equivalence

The paper

Lanard's 2019 paper is titled "Equivalence of categories between coefficient systems and systems of idempotents". It generalizes Wang's $\mathrm{GL}_n$ result to general connected reductive groups.

Setup. Let $\mathrm{BT}$ be the (semi-simple) Bruhat–Tits building of $G$, $\mathcal H_R(G)$ the Hecke algebra, and $\mathrm{BT}_0$ the set of vertices.

A consistent system of idempotents $e = (e_x)_{x \in \mathrm{BT}_0}$ of $\mathcal H_R(G)$ satisfies:

  1. $e_x e_y = e_y e_x$ when $x, y$ are adjacent vertices.
  2. $e_x e_z e_y = e_x e_y$ when $z \in H(x, y)$ is adjacent to $x$.
  3. $e_{gx} = g e_x g^{-1}$ for $g \in G$.

A condition (*) is also imposed: for every vertex $x$ and every polysimplex $\sigma$ containing $x$, the induced idempotent $e_\sigma$ lies in $\mathcal H_R(G_x)$.

An $e$-coefficient system is a $G$-equivariant coefficient system $\Gamma = (V_\sigma)_{\sigma \in \mathrm{BT}}$ such that for $x \le \sigma$ the map $\varphi^\sigma_x : V_\sigma \to V_x$ induces an isomorphism $V_\sigma \xrightarrow{\sim} e_\sigma(V_x)$.

Main theorem (Theorem 4.10 / 4.11)

Theorem [Lan19, Thm 4.10/4.11]. For a consistent system of idempotents $e$ satisfying (*) and an $e$-coefficient system $\Gamma$ on $\mathrm{BT}$:

  • $C_\bullet(\mathrm{BT}, \Gamma)$ is exact except in degree 0.
  • The functor $V \mapsto \Gamma(V) := (\sigma \mapsto e_\sigma V)$ and $\Gamma \mapsto H_0(\mathrm{BT}, \Gamma)$ are quasi-inverse equivalences $\mathrm{Rep}^e_R(G) \simeq \mathrm{Coef}_e(G, R)$.
Proof

technique we borrow

Lanard's key technical innovation is the notion of admissible paths between polysimplices. Wang's earlier proof for $\mathrm{GL}_n$ used taut paths, which exploit the fact that the simplicial hull of two $\mathrm{GL}_n$ vertices is a simplex. For general reductive groups this fails, so Lanard introduces admissible paths (§2 of the paper) and proves (§3) that the local maps $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ they define are independent of the chosen path.

Once the local maps are well-defined (Proposition 3.14), §4 follows [MS10 §2.5] (Meyer–Solleveld 2010, J. Reine Angew. Math. 647, arXiv:1004.4361 — PDF not in our local folder, citation not independently verified) to assemble idempotents $\{e^\Sigma_x\}$ on $H_0(\Sigma, \Gamma)$ and conclude.

Terminology warning: "taut path" ≠ "admissible path"

The two terms are distinct and belong to different authors:

  • Taut path — Wang [Wan17 §2.3] (Chuijia Wang, unpublished manuscript by Sheaf7 co-author) (Chuijia Wang, unpublished manuscript by Sheaf7 co-author). A geometric object: a straight line segment in a Euclidean apartment $A$, together with the facet sequence it traverses. Requires $A$ Euclidean polysimplicial; uses no hull machinery.
  • Admissible path — Lanard [Lan19, §2]. A combinatorial object: a chain of facets inside a hull $H(\sigma, \tau) \subseteq \mathrm{BT}$ satisfying an admissibility condition (monotone with respect to a hull function). Designed to work in general reductive Bruhat–Tits buildings where hulls need not be simplicial and need not lie in a single apartment.

We use Wang's taut paths, not Lanard's admissible paths. The reason we can: we first reduce to a single apartment via Reduction lemma: cartesian unit-iso check transfers to apartment, and our apartment $A$ is convex Euclidean. See Local maps ε^τ_σ along taut paths in the apartment for the rigorous taut-path definition and a full side-by-side comparison table.

Throughout this paper, "taut path" always means Wang's notion in the apartment. We never invoke Lanard's path-equivalence machinery as a black box.
