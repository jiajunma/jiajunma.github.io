Recovering the induction data from a quasi-fundamental domain

Fix a cellular $G$-action on an abstract cell complex $X$ and a [[quasi-fundamental domain|node:equivariant_sheaves.quasi_fundamental_domain]] $S \subseteq X$ with associated subgroup $G' \le G$. When may one present $X$ as an induction $G \times^P S$ from data on $S$?

Suppose we are given a cell-wise family of subgroups $P = \{P_E\}_{E \in S}$, $P_E \le G$, satisfying:

  • (P0) Cosheaf monotonicity. For $E, F \in S$ with $E \preceq F$, $P_F \le P_E$.
  • (P1) $G'$-equivariance. For $n \in G'$ and $E \in S$, $P_{n \cdot E} = n P_E n^{-1}$.
  • (P3) Fix bound. For $E \in S$, $P_E \le \Fix_G(E)$.
  • (P4) Fix factorisation. For $E \in S$,

$$ \Fix_G(E) = \Fix_{G'}(E) \cdot P_E. $$

The paper's original axiom (P2), $P_E \cap G' \subseteq \Fix_{G'}(E)$, is a consequence of (P3): if $m \in P_E \cap G'$ then $m \in \Fix_G(E)$ by (P3), so $m \cdot E = E$; in particular $m \in \Fix_{G'}(E)$.

Reconstruction theorem

Theorem. Under the assumptions above, the equivalence relation on $G \times S$ defined by

$$ (g_1, E_1) \sim (g_2, E_2) \ :\iff\ \exists n \in G',\ n \cdot E_1 = E_2 \ \wedge\ g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1} $$

descends to a bijection

$$ (G \times S) / \sim \ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \ X,\qquad [(g, E)] \mapsto g \cdot E $$

that is $G$-equivariant, with the action $g' \cdot [(g, E)] := [(g'g, E)]$.

Proof sketch. Well-definedness of the map on classes reduces to the statement that $(g_1, E_1) \sim (g_2, E_2)$ implies $g_1 \cdot E_1 = g_2 \cdot E_2$. Given a witness $n$, axiom (P3) gives $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in \Fix_G(E_1)$, i.e. $g_1 \cdot E_1 = (g_2 n) \cdot E_1 = g_2 \cdot (n \cdot E_1) = g_2 \cdot E_2$.

Surjectivity is the surj clause of the QFD hypothesis.

For injectivity, suppose $g_1 \cdot E_1 = g_2 \cdot E_2$ with $E_1, E_2 \in S$. The [[QFD orbit lemma|node:equivariant_sheaves.qfd_orbit_lemma]] gives $n \in G'$ with $n \cdot E_1 = E_2$ and $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in \Fix_G(E_1)$. Apply (P4) to write $g_1^{-1} g_2 n = u \cdot p$ with $u \in \Fix_{G'}(E_1)$ and $p \in P_{E_1}$. Setting $n' := n u^{-1}$ yields a fresh witness: $n' \in G'$ (as $G' \supseteq \{n, u\}$), $n' \cdot E_1 = n \cdot E_1 = E_2$ (since $u$ fixes $E_1$), and $g_1^{-1} g_2 n' = u p u^{-1}$; by (P1) with $u \in \Fix_{G'}(E_1)$, $u$ normalises $P_{E_1}$, so $u p u^{-1} \in P_{E_1}$ and $(g_1, E_1) \sim (g_2, E_2)$. $\square$

Setwise versus pointwise: the (P0) obstruction

The most natural candidate cosheaf, $P_E := \Fix_G(E)$, automatically satisfies (P1), (P3), and (P4), but (P0) can fail. Explicitly, take $X$ the face poset of a $2$-simplex with $G = \mathbb Z/3$ acting by rotation. Then $\Fix_G$ of the top cell is all of $G$, while $\Fix_G$ of any vertex is trivial, so the containment $\Fix_G(F) \le \Fix_G(E)$ needed for $E \preceq F$ fails.

The paper's regular-CW setting avoids this by taking $\Fix_G(E)$ to mean the pointwise fixator of the topological interior of the cell, which is monotone by continuity. At the abstract cell-poset level no such refinement is available; a suitable $P$ must be supplied as external data or extracted from geometric structure.

Face relation on the ambient complex

Under the same hypotheses, the face relation on $X$ obeys the implication

$$ \Bigl(\exists n \in G',\ n \cdot E \preceq F,\ g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_E\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ g_1 \cdot E \preceq g_2 \cdot F. $$

The proof is the same absorption as in the injectivity step, ending in $G$-equivariance of $\preceq$ from Cellular G-action on an abstract cell complex. The converse is not forced by (P0)–(P4); paper §2.8 recovers it using regular-CW input that has no abstract analogue.

Lean formalization

The data $(P, P_0, P_1, P_3, P_4)$ is packaged as a structure InductionData parameterised by the ambient QFD instance [IsQuasiFundDomain G' S]. (P2) is exposed as a derived lemma InductionData.P2; the injectivity absorption identity as InductionData.product_le_stabilizer.

The relation on $G \times S$ is InductionData.rel, its Setoid packaging InductionData.setoid, the quotient type InductionData.Quotient'. The natural map is InductionData.toX; toX_injective and toX_surjective package the bijection, which is bundled as InductionData.equivX : Quotient' ≃ X.

The forward implication of the face relation is InductionData.smul_le_smul_of_rel; the reverse is deliberately absent, matching the abstract-level gap discussed above.
