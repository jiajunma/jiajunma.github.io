Reverse-direction induction

The paper's Proposition 2.8 goes forward: given InductionDatum d on $X'$, the induced complex $X := G \times^P X'$ is a $G$-cell complex with $X'$ as a QFD.

At the abstract cell-poset level this admits a converse: given a QFD $S \subseteq X$ with subgroup $G'$, plus a cosheaf-of-subgroups $P$ satisfying four axioms below, one can reconstruct $X \simeq G \times^P S$.

Data

Fix IsQuasiFundDomain G' S on $X$. A choice of induction data consists of $P : X \to \mathrm{Subgroup}(G)$ (defined on $S$) satisfying:

  • (P0) Cosheaf monotonicity on $S$: $E \le F \Rightarrow P_F \le P_E$.
  • (P1) $G'$-conjugation equivariance: $n \in G' \Rightarrow P_{n \cdot E} = n P_E n^{-1}$.
  • (P3) Well-definedness upper bound: $P_E \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}_G(E) = \mathrm{MulAction.stabilizer}\, G\, E$. This is what makes the natural map $[(g, E)] \mapsto g \cdot E$ well-defined on the induction quotient.
  • (P4) Fix factorisation: $\mathrm{Fix}_G(E) = \mathrm{Fix}_{G'}(E) \cdot P_E$. This is what makes the natural map injective: the QFD's orbit witness $g^{-1} h n \in \mathrm{Fix}_G(E)$ can be absorbed via (P4) as $u \cdot p$, letting us adjust the $G'$ witness to $n u^{-1}$ and the residual lands in $P_E$.

The paper's original (P2) axiom $P_E \cap G' \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}_{G'}(E)$ is a derived lemma from (P3).

Setwise vs pointwise. The canonical setwise choice $P_E := \mathrm{MulAction.stabilizer}\, G\, E$ satisfies (P1), (P3), (P4) but generally fails (P0) — the setwise stabiliser of a higher-dim cell need not fix its lower-dim faces. The paper's geometric setting uses the pointwise fixator, which does satisfy (P0) by continuity. At the abstract cell-poset level (P0) must be supplied as external data.

The reconstruction

Given InductionData d on IsQuasiFundDomain G' S, define the equivalence

$$(g, E) \sim (h, F) \ :\iff\ \exists n \in G',\ n \cdot E = F \wedge g^{-1} h n \in P_E$$

on $G \times S.\mathrm{carrier}$, and set

$$Q_d \;:=\; (G \times S.\mathrm{carrier}) / \sim.$$

The natural map $[(g, E)] \mapsto g \cdot E$ is:

  • well-defined by (P3);
  • surjective by the QFD's surj axiom;
  • injective by the sharpened orbit lemma $g \cdot E = h \cdot F \ \iff\ \exists n \in G', n \cdot E = F \wedge g^{-1} h n \in P_E$ (proved via QFD orbit_iff + (P4)).

Together these give a $G$-equivariant bijection

$$\mathrm{equivX} : Q_d \ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ X.$$

Face relation

The (←) direction of the face-relation characterisation

$$\Bigl(\exists n \in G',\ n \cdot E \le F,\ g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_E\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ g_1 \cdot E \le g_2 \cdot F$$

follows from (P3) plus $G$-equivariance of $\le$ on a GCellComplex. The (→) converse is not provable at the abstract level; the paper recovers it topologically through the CW structure.

Design note

At the paper level this is implicit in Proposition 2.8's uniqueness claim ("any CW structure on $X$ for which $\iota$ is cellular and the action is cellular must have the same cell set"). As a Lean theorem it is separated out because reconstruction is genuine content when one starts from an abstract QFD without a pre-existing induction.
