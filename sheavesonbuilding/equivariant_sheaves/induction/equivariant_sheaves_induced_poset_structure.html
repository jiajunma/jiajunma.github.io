Face relation and poset structure on the induced space

Fix the induction data $(G', P)$ satisfying (P0)–(P2) and the induced space $X = G \times^P X'$ of [[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]], with $\iota : X' \hookrightarrow X$, $E \mapsto [(1, E)]$.

The face relation on $X$ has to be introduced by hand — $X$ is only a set-theoretic quotient — and then shown to make $X$ a partial order.

Definition

Definition (face relation on $X$). For $x \in X$ and a class $[(g, E)] \in X$, $$ > x \;\preceq\; [(g, E)] > \;\iff\; > \text{there exists } F \preceq E \text{ in } X' > \text{ with } x = [(g, F)]. > $$

In prose: fix any representative $(g, E)$ of the right-hand class; then $x \preceq [(g, E)]$ means $x$ has a representative with the same $G$-component $g$ and an $X'$-component that is a subface of $E$. No reflexive-transitive closure is needed — the ambient equivalence relation $\sim$ (which lets us shift representatives) absorbs all closure steps into a single generator.

Reflexivity is built in: for any class $[(g, E)]$, take $F := E$; then $F \preceq E$ in $X'$ (reflexivity of the base poset) and $[(g, F)] = [(g, E)]$.

Well-definedness of the definition in the choice of representative $(g, E)$ of the right-hand class is verified below (Right descent).

Equivalent characterisation

Theorem 1 (single-witness form). For $(g_1, E_1), (g_2, E_2) \in G \times X'$, $$ > [(g_1, E_1)] \;\preceq\; [(g_2, E_2)] > \;\iff\; > \exists\, n \in G',\quad n \cdot E_1 \preceq E_2 \text{ in } X' > \;\wedge\; g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}. > $$

Compare with the equivalence relation $\sim$ of [[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]: replacing $\preceq$ on $X'$ by $=$ recovers $\sim$ verbatim. The face relation and the equivalence relation share the same syntactic shape.

Proof

($\Rightarrow$) Assume the definition: $\exists F \preceq E_2$ with $[(g_1, E_1)] = [(g_2, F)]$. Unpacking $\sim$: $\exists n \in G'$ with $n \cdot E_1 = F$ and $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}$. Then $n \cdot E_1 = F \preceq E_2$ and $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}$.

($\Leftarrow$) Assume the single-witness form with witness $n$. Set $F := n \cdot E_1$. Then $F \preceq E_2$ (given), and $[(g_1, E_1)] = [(g_2, F)]$ holds by applying $\sim$ with the same witness $m := n$: $m \cdot E_1 = n \cdot E_1 = F$, and $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}$ (given). So the definition holds with $F$ as chosen. $\square$

The single-witness form is the pre-quotient relation $\preceq_{\text{rep}}$ on $G \times X'$: $$ (g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2) \;:\iff\; \exists\, n \in G',\;\; n \cdot E_1 \preceq E_2 \;\text{and}\; g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}. $$ It is the form used in every downstream calculation.

Transitivity

Theorem 2 (transitivity). Under (P0) and (P1), $\preceq$ on $X$ is transitive.

Equivalently, $\preceq_{\text{rep}}$ on $G \times X'$ is transitive.

Proof. Given witnesses $n_1$ for $(g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2)$ and $n_2$ for $(g_2, E_2) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_3, E_3)$, take $n := n_2 n_1 \in G'$.

Face-chain. $n \cdot E_1 = n_2 \cdot (n_1 \cdot E_1) \preceq n_2 \cdot E_2 \preceq E_3$, using cellularity of $n_2$ (preserves $\preceq$) and transitivity of $\preceq$ on $X'$.

Residual. Set $p_1 := g_1^{-1} g_2 n_1 \in P_{E_1}$ and $p_2 := g_2^{-1} g_3 n_2 \in P_{E_2}$. Then $$ g_1^{-1} g_3 (n_2 n_1) = (g_1^{-1} g_2)(g_2^{-1} g_3 n_2)(n_1) = (p_1 n_1^{-1}) p_2 n_1 = p_1 \cdot \bigl(n_1^{-1} p_2 n_1\bigr). $$

To conclude $g_1^{-1} g_3 n \in P_{E_1}$, we show $n_1^{-1} p_2 n_1 \in P_{E_1}$:

  • $p_2 \in P_{E_2}$.
  • From $n_1 \cdot E_1 \preceq E_2$ and (P0): $P_{E_2} \le P_{n_1 \cdot E_1}$, so $p_2 \in P_{n_1 \cdot E_1}$.
  • From (P1): $P_{n_1 \cdot E_1} = n_1 P_{E_1} n_1^{-1}$, i.e.\ $n_1^{-1} P_{n_1 \cdot E_1} n_1 = P_{E_1}$. Applying to $p_2$: $n_1^{-1} p_2 n_1 \in P_{E_1}$.

Hence $g_1^{-1} g_3 n = p_1 \cdot (n_1^{-1} p_2 n_1) \in P_{E_1} \cdot P_{E_1} = P_{E_1}$. $\square$

Where each axiom enters. (P0) lifts $p_2$ from $P_{E_2}$ into the larger $P_{n_1 \cdot E_1}$ along the face relation $n_1 E_1 \preceq E_2$. (P1) conjugates $P_{n_1 \cdot E_1}$ back to $P_{E_1}$ via $n_1 \in G'$. Neither can be dispensed with — a $P$ without cosheaf monotonicity or without $G'$-equivariance already breaks transitivity of the induced face relation.

Well-definedness on classes (descent of $\preceq_{\text{rep}}$)

The definition above uses a representative $(g, E)$ of the right-hand class; we now verify that the resulting relation on classes is independent of this choice. Equivalently: $\preceq_{\text{rep}}$ on $G \times X'$ is $\sim$-invariant on both arguments.

Proposition (descent). Under (P0) and (P1), for $(g_1, E_1) \sim (g_1', E_1')$ and $(g_2, E_2) \sim (g_2', E_2')$, $$ > (g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2) > \iff > (g_1', E_1') \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2', E_2'). > $$

Proof (left descent). Take $m \in G'$ with $m \cdot E_1 = E_1'$ and $\alpha := g_1^{-1} g_1' m \in P_{E_1}$. Assume $(g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2)$ with witness $n$. Take $n'' := n m^{-1}$:

  • Face-chain: $n'' \cdot E_1' = n m^{-1} \cdot (m \cdot E_1) = n \cdot E_1 \preceq E_2$.
  • Membership: $(g_1')^{-1} g_2 n'' = m \alpha^{-1} g_1^{-1} g_2 n m^{-1}$; substitute $g_1^{-1} g_2 n \in P_{E_1}$ and use (P1) with $m$ to conclude $m \alpha^{-1} (g_1^{-1} g_2 n) m^{-1} \in m P_{E_1} m^{-1} = P_{m \cdot E_1} = P_{E_1'}$.

Reverse direction: symmetric via $m^{-1}$.

Proof (right descent). Take $m \in G'$ with $m \cdot E_2 = E_2'$ and $\beta := g_2^{-1} g_2' m \in P_{E_2}$. Assume $(g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2)$ with witness $n$. Take $n'' := m n$:

  • Face-chain: $n'' \cdot E_1 = m \cdot (n \cdot E_1) \preceq m \cdot E_2 = E_2'$.
  • Membership: $\beta \in P_{E_2}$, and by (P0) with $n \cdot E_1 \preceq E_2$: $\beta \in P_{n \cdot E_1}$. By (P1): $\beta = n p n^{-1}$ for some $p \in P_{E_1}$. Compute $g_1^{-1} g_2' n'' = (g_1^{-1} g_2 n) \cdot p \in P_{E_1} \cdot P_{E_1} = P_{E_1}$.

$\square$

Antisymmetry and partial-order structure

Theorem 3 (antisymmetry mod $\sim$). If $(g_1, E_1) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_2, E_2)$ and $(g_2, E_2) \preceq_{\text{rep}} (g_1, E_1)$, then $(g_1, E_1) \sim (g_2, E_2)$.

Proof. From witnesses $n_1$ and $n_2$ respectively, we get $(n_2 n_1) \cdot E_1 \preceq E_1$. The $G$-action is cellular (Cellular G-action on an abstract cell complex), hence dimension preserving: $\dim((n_2 n_1) \cdot E_1) = \dim E_1$. Strict monotonicity of $\dim$ along a proper face-chain (Abstract cell complex) forces $(n_2 n_1) \cdot E_1 = E_1$, so $n_1 \cdot E_1 = E_2$, and the $P_{E_1}$-membership from the $n_1$-witness is exactly the $\sim$-witness. $\square$

Assembling Theorems 1–3 with the descent proposition:

Corollary. $\preceq$ is a partial order on $X$, and the definition coincides with the descended single-witness form $\preceq_{\text{rep}}$.

Which axioms enter which theorem

Theorem Axioms used
Definition + reflexivity none (reflexivity of $X'$'s $\preceq$)
Theorem 1 (definition ⇔ single-witness) none beyond the definition of $\sim$
Theorem 2 (transitivity) (P0), (P1)
Descent proposition (P0), (P1)
Theorem 3 (antisymmetry mod $\sim$) dim preservation (cellular action) + strict monotonicity of $\dim$

Note: (P2) does not enter this node. It is the axiom needed for injectivity of $\iota$, i.e.\ for $\iota$ to be order-reflecting (ι : X' → G ×^P X' is a cellular embedding (poset form) clause (1)), not for the face relation itself.

Consequences

  • The embedding $\iota : X' \to X$, $E \mapsto [(1, E)]$, is order-preserving: for $F \preceq E$ in $X'$, the definition with $g := 1$ gives $\iota F \preceq \iota E$ immediately.
  • Order-reflection of $\iota$ requires (P2); see ι : X' → G ×^P X' is a cellular embedding (poset form) clause (2).
  • The dimension function $\dim([(g, E)]) := \dim E$ is well-defined because $\sim$ forces $E_2 = n \cdot E_1$ for some $n \in G'$, and the $G$-action is dimension-preserving.
  • Together with local finiteness of $X'$ (inherited via $G$-translates), this equips $X$ with the abstract-cell-complex and $G$-cell-complex instances used by CW structure and QFD property on G ×^P X'.

Lean formalization

The pre-quotient relation is exposed as the single-witness form (Theorem 1's right-hand side). The name mirrors InductionRel, the pre-quotient equivalence relation from [[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]: one is the "$=$" case, the other the "$\preceq$" case, both with residual in $P_{E_1}$.

def InductionLe (g₁E₁ g₂E₂ : G × X') : Prop :=
  ∃ n ∈ d.G', n • g₁E₁.2 ≤ g₂E₂.2 ∧
              g₁E₁.1⁻¹ * g₂E₂.1 * n ∈ d.P g₁E₁.2

Using the single-witness form as the primary Lean definition (rather than a Relation.ReflTransGen over the same-$g$ generator) keeps the proofs elementary — each of the required properties is a dedicated lemma:

Property Lean lemma
Reflexivity InductionLe_refl
Transitivity (Theorem 2) InductionLe_trans
Left descent InductionLe_left_descends
Right descent InductionLe_right_descends
Antisymmetry (Theorem 3) InductionLe_antisymm

The PartialOrder instance on InducedSpace d is built by Quotient.lift₂ (InductionLe d): the descent lemmas discharge the well-definedness side condition; reflexivity + transitivity give le_refl and le_trans; antisymmetry gives le_antisymm.

instance : PartialOrder (InducedSpace d) where
  le := Quotient.lift₂ (InductionLe d) ⟨left/right descent⟩
  le_refl  := ⟨InductionLe_refl⟩
  le_trans := ⟨InductionLe_trans⟩
  le_antisymm := ⟨InductionLe_antisymm⟩

The equivalence of the paper-side definition (single generator on classes, no closure) with the operational single-witness form (Theorem 1) is not separately formalised in the current library — the single-witness form is the definition of InductionLe and Theorem 1 becomes a trivial calculation once written out. Adding a Relation.ReflTransGen-based reformulation and an equivalence lemma is a well-scoped follow-up.
