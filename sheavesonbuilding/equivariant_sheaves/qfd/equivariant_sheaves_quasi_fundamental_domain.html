Quasi-fundamental domain

Let $G$ act cellularly on a regular CW complex $X$.

Throughout this node the QFD is denoted $S$ — a face-closed subcomplex of $X$. In the induction context ([[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]) the base of the induction is a separate $G'$-space typically written $X'$; when that $X'$ is embedded into $X = G \times^P X'$ via $\iota$, the resulting QFD in $X$ is $S := \iota(X')$ (a subcomplex of $X$, not a space of its own).

Definition (fundamental domain). A subcomplex $S \subseteq X$ is a fundamental domain for the $G$-action if the natural map $[\![S]\!] \to G \backslash [\![X]\!]$ is a bijection.

Definition (quasi-fundamental domain, QFD). A subcomplex $S \subseteq X$ is a quasi-fundamental domain for the $G$-action if there exists a subgroup $G' \le G$ (a distinguished subgroup) satisfying:

  • (G'-le-stab) $G' \le \Stab_G(S)$ — $G'$ set-wise stabilises $S$.
  • (Surjectivity) Every cell of $X$ is in the $G$-orbit of some cell of $S$: for every $F \in [\![X]\!]$ there exist $g \in G$ and $E \in [\![S]\!]$ with $g \cdot E = F$.
  • (Orbit relation) For $E, F \in [\![S]\!]$ and $g, h \in G$ with $g \cdot E = h \cdot F$, there exists $n \in G'$ realising $n \cdot E = F$ and $(g^{-1} h n) \cdot E = E$.

Equivalently, the natural map $$G' \backslash [\![S]\!] \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; G \backslash [\![X]\!]$$ is a bijection. We then say $S$ is a QFD of the $G$-space $X$ (with distinguished subgroup $G'$).

The three axioms are the three components of the QFD data. Choosing $G'$ smaller sharpens the orbit relation; setting $G' := \Stab_G(S)$ recovers the coarsest valid QFD, in which case the orbit relation reduces to the bijection $\Stab_G(S) \backslash [![S]!] \xrightarrow{\sim} G \backslash [![X]!]$.

Canonical examples

Each item is a QFD of the preceding one:

  1. The $G$-set $\mathcal{B}(G)$, the extended Bruhat–Tits building of $G$ (with a suitable facet structure).
  2. The $N$-set $A$, where $N$ is the normalizer of a maximal split torus $T$ of $G$ and $A \subset \mathcal{B}(G)$ is the corresponding apartment.
  3. The $\Omega$-set $\bar C$, where $\bar C := \mathrm{pr}^{-1}_{A, A^{\mathrm{red}}}(\overline{C'})$ is the preimage of a closed alcove $C'$ in the reduced apartment and $\Omega := \Stab_N(C')$. Equivalently, $\bar C$ is a fundamental domain for the (non-extended) affine Weyl group $W^{\mathrm{aff}}$ acting on $A$.

The third example is developed further in Quasi-fundamental domain from a closed alcove (App B).

Notation: $S$ vs.\ $X'$

The QFD is a subcomplex of $X$ — a face-closed subset of cells, denoted $S$ throughout. This matches the Lean type S : Subcomplex X and keeps the QFD-as-subcomplex point of view clean.

The notation $X'$ is reserved for a distinct $G'$-space: the base of an induction $X = G \times^P X'$ ([[node:equivariant_sheaves.induction_GxP_X]]). In that setting, the QFD of the induced $G$-space $X$ is the image $\iota(X') \subseteq X$ (a subcomplex, formalised as Subcomplex.ofIota d in Lean); it is a different type from $X'$ itself, related by the poset bijection $\iota : X' \to \iota(X')$. Nodes referring to $X'$ do so only in the induction context.

Lean formalization

class IsQuasiFundDomain {G : Type*} [Group G] {X : Type*}
    [PartialOrder X] [AbstractCellComplex X]
    [MulAction G X] [GCellComplex G X]
    (G' : Subgroup G) (S : Subcomplex X) : Prop where
  G'_le_stab : G' ≤ S.stabilizer
  surj : ∀ F : X, ∃ (g : G) (E : X), E ∈ S ∧ g • E = F
  orbit_iff : ∀ {E F : X}, E ∈ S → F ∈ S → ∀ (g h : G),
    g • E = h • F →
    ∃ n ∈ G', n • E = F ∧ (g⁻¹ * h * n) • E = E

IsQuasiFundDomain G' S is a Prop class parameterised by the distinguished subgroup G', not by taking $\Stab_G(S)$ implicitly. This is the sharpest form of the QFD hypothesis: choosing G' smaller than $\Stab_G(S)$ refines the orbit statement orbit_iff accordingly. Setwise-stabiliser instances are recovered by setting $G' := \mathrm{Subcomplex.stabilizer}\ S$.

The setwise stabiliser of a subcomplex is exposed as a subgroup:

def Subcomplex.stabilizer (S : Subcomplex X) : Subgroup G where
  carrier := {g | ∀ E : X, g • E ∈ S ↔ E ∈ S}

The iff form of the carrier ensures both $g \cdot S \subseteq S$ and $g^{-1} \cdot S \subseteq S$, which is what makes it a subgroup.
