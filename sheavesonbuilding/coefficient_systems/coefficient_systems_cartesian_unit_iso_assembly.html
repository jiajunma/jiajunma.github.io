Unit-iso assembly: $\mathscr V_\mathcal F \;\xrightarrow{\sim}\; e_\mathcal F H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$

This node completes the §7.2 cartesian unit-iso proof. Building on:

  • Reduction to apartment (Reduction lemma: cartesian unit-iso check transfers to apartment)
  • Abstract idempotent properties (i)-(v) ([[node:coefficient_systems.idempotent_system_from_T_axioms]])
  • Local maps $\varepsilon^\sigma_\tau$ well-defined along taut paths (Local maps ε^τ_σ along taut paths in the apartment)
  • Lanard 4.9 analogue $i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F = e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E$ (Lanard 4.9 analogue: i_F ∘ ε^E_F = e_F ∘ i_E for adjacent facets)

Statement and reduction

Theorem (cartesian unit iso). Let $\mathscr V \in \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi)$ under (T1)–(T5). For every facet $\mathcal F \subseteq \BSigma$, the unit map $$\mathrm{unit}_\mathcal F := i_\mathcal F \;:\; \mathscr V_\mathcal F \;\longrightarrow\; e_\mathcal F H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$$ is an isomorphism.

By Reduction lemma: cartesian unit-iso check transfers to apartment it suffices to check this for facets $\mathcal F \subseteq A$. Fix such an $\mathcal F$ and a vertex $x \in \overline{\mathcal F}$.

The proof has two halves: surjectivity and injectivity.

Surjectivity: $e_\mathcal F H_0 \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$

Following the structure of [Lanard 2019, Thm 4.10].

Step S1. $H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V) = \sum_{y \in \BSigma_0} j_y(\mathscr V_y)$, where $j_y: \mathscr V_y \to H_0$ is the canonical map (= $i_y$ when $y$ is a vertex viewed as a 0-facet). This is by definition of $H_0$: every class is represented by a 0-chain, which is a sum over vertex stalks.

Step S2. $e_\mathcal F H_0 = \sum_{y} e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y)$, just by applying $e_\mathcal F$ (which is $R$-linear).

Step S3. By the support lemma [[node:coefficient_systems.support_in_BSx]], $e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) = 0$ unless $y \in \BSigma_x = K_x \cdot A$. So we may restrict to $y \in (K_x A)_0$.

Step S4 — Apartment case ($y \in A$). For $y$ a vertex of $A$, apply the iterated Lanard 4.9 analogue (Corollary in Lanard 4.9 analogue: i_F ∘ ε^E_F = e_F ∘ i_E for adjacent facets) with $\mathcal E = y$: $$e_\mathcal F \circ i_y \;=\; i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^y_\mathcal F.$$ Applied to $\mathscr V_y$: $$e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) = e_\mathcal F i_y(\mathscr V_y) = i_\mathcal F(\varepsilon^y_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_y)) \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F).$$

Step S5 — Extending across $K_x A$ via apartment choice. For $y \in K_x A$ with $y = k y'$ ($k \in K_x$, $y' \in A_0$): by the diagonal $G$-action on $C_\bullet$, $$j_y(\mathscr V_y) = k \cdot j_{y'}(\mathscr V_{y'})$$ where the rightmost $k \cdot$ is the $G$-action on $H_0$.

By property (iv) ($G$-equivariance of idempotents), $e_\mathcal F k = k e_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}$. Hence $$e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) = e_\mathcal F k j_{y'}(\mathscr V_{y'}) = k \cdot e_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} j_{y'}(\mathscr V_{y'}). \tag{$\dagger$}$$

We now apply the iterated Lanard 4.9 analogue to the right side, working inside an apartment $A'' = h A$ ($h \in K_x$) chosen to contain both $y'$ and $k^{-1} \mathcal F$.

Existence of $A''$ — $K_x$-local two-apartment property. We need the $K_x$-local form of the Bruhat–Tits (A1) axiom for $\BSigma$, codified in [[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]] as the Kx-local (A1) lemma:

Any two cells of $\BSigma_x = K_x \cdot A$ lie in a common $K_x$-translate of $A$.

The equivalent algebraic content (worked out in [[node:building.A1_axiom_in_component]]) is the Iwahori-type decomposition $\hat K_x \subseteq N \cdot \hat K_{E_1} \cdot \hat K_{E_2} \cdot N$ for any cells $E_1, E_2 \in [\![A]\!]$ — which holds in the classical parahoric case and is to be verified case-by-case for the applications in Sheaf7 §10. Both $y' \in A \subseteq \BSigma_x$ and $k^{-1} \mathcal F \in k^{-1} A \subseteq \BSigma_x$ are in $\BSigma_x$, so the lemma applies.

We do not need the component-global two-apartment property (which is the stronger statement covering all of $C_0$, equivalent to $J = \hat K_{E_1} \cdot N \cdot \hat K_{E_2}$). The Kx-local form suffices because the assembly only iterates within $\BSigma_x$.

(Note: by [[node:building.component_is_KxA]], $C_0 = K_x \cdot A = \BSigma_x$ when the component-global (A1) holds, so the Kx-local and component-global forms coincide in that case. Until the component-global form is verified, the Kx-local form is the intrinsic requirement of this proof.)

Lanard 4.9 in $A''$. The proof of Lanard 4.9 analogue used only: - Cosheaf composition $r^\mathcal F_x = r^\mathcal E_x \circ r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E$ (intrinsic, apartment-independent); - Commutation of $r^\mathcal E_x$ with $e_\mathcal F$, via $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal E$ (intrinsic); - Cartesianness (a property of $\mathscr V$, not of any apartment).

So the same proof gives the Lanard 4.9 analogue in any apartment $A''$: for two adjacent facets $\mathcal E, \mathcal F$ of $A''$ sharing a common vertex, $i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F = e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E$.

Property (iii) (geodesic telescoping) likewise holds within $A''$: it was proved via (T5), which states a geometric condition for any $x, y, z \in A$ with $z \in [x, y]$ — the same condition applies inside $A''$ by $G$-equivariance (translate to $A$ if needed). So the iteration corollary applies in $A''$.

Application in $A''$. Take a taut path in $A''$ from $y'$ to $k^{-1} \mathcal F$ (which exists since both lie in $A''$ and $A''$ is Euclidean). The iterated Lanard 4.9 analogue gives: $$e_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} \circ j_{y'} = i_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} \circ \varepsilon^{y'}_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}.$$ Applied to $\mathscr V_{y'}$: $$e_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} j_{y'}(\mathscr V_{y'}) = i_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}(\varepsilon^{y'}_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}(\mathscr V_{y'})) \subseteq i_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}(\mathscr V_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}).$$

Translating back via $k$. By $G$-equivariance of $\mathscr V$ and $H_0$, $k \cdot i_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}(\mathscr V_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}) = i_\mathcal F(k \cdot \mathscr V_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}) = i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$ (using $k \cdot \mathscr V_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} = \mathscr V_\mathcal F$ via the cosheaf's $G$-action $g_\mathcal F^\mathscr V$).

Combining with $(\dagger)$: $$e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) = k \cdot e_{k^{-1} \mathcal F} j_{y'}(\mathscr V_{y'}) \subseteq k \cdot i_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}(\mathscr V_{k^{-1} \mathcal F}) = i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F). \qquad \square$$

So Step S5 finishes the surjectivity: for any vertex $y \in (K_x A)_0$, $e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$. Combined with Step S3 (support reduces to $K_x A$) and Step S2 (decomposition by vertices), $e_\mathcal F H_0 \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$. $\square$

Injectivity: $i_\mathcal F$ is injective on $\mathscr V_\mathcal F$

This direction is strictly easier and does not require any $K_x$-translation analysis. We exhibit a left inverse $p_\mathcal F: e_\mathcal F H_0 \to \mathscr V_\mathcal F$ to $i_\mathcal F$.

Construction of $p_\mathcal F$ on the image $i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$. Define $$p_\mathcal F: i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F) \to \mathscr V_\mathcal F, \qquad p_\mathcal F(i_\mathcal F(v)) := v.$$ This is well-defined as a partial function on $i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$ if and only if $i_\mathcal F$ is injective. We claim it is — and this claim follows from a different construction below.

Construction via local maps. Define $p_\mathcal F^\bullet: H_0(\BSigma_x, \mathscr V) \to \mathscr V_\mathcal F$ on classes of the form $[y \otimes w]$ ($y \in A_0$, $w \in \mathscr V_y$): $$p_\mathcal F^\bullet([y \otimes w]) := \varepsilon^y_\mathcal F(w).$$

This extends to all of $H_0(\BSigma_x, \mathscr V)$ — provided we show:

(P1) $p_\mathcal F^\bullet$ is well-defined as a map on $H_0$ (i.e. vanishes on boundaries of $C_1$).

The vanishing-on-boundaries argument: for a 1-cell $E \subseteq A$ with vertices $\mathcal F_\pm$ (so $\mathcal F_\pm \preceq E$), and $w \in \mathscr V_E$, $$\partial(E \otimes w) = \mathcal F_+ \otimes r^E_{\mathcal F_+}(w) - \mathcal F_- \otimes r^E_{\mathcal F_-}(w).$$

Apply $p_\mathcal F^\bullet$ to the boundary class: $$p_\mathcal F^\bullet(\partial(E \otimes w)) = \varepsilon^{\mathcal F_+}_\mathcal F(r^E_{\mathcal F_+}(w)) - \varepsilon^{\mathcal F_-}_\mathcal F(r^E_{\mathcal F_-}(w)).$$

By the well-definedness of $\varepsilon^\bullet_\mathcal F$ along taut paths (Local maps ε^τ_σ along taut paths in the apartment), we may compute each $\varepsilon^{\mathcal F_\pm}_\mathcal F$ via a taut path that passes through $E$. Such a path exists by prepending $\mathcal F_\pm \to E$: $$\varepsilon^{\mathcal F_\pm}_\mathcal F = \varepsilon^E_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^{\mathcal F_\pm}_E.$$

The elementary step $\varepsilon^{\mathcal F_\pm}_E$ (projection-then-lift, since $\mathcal F_\pm \preceq E$) applied to $r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm}(w) \in \mathscr V_{\mathcal F_\pm}$: by cartesianness $r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm}(w) \in e_E \mathscr V_{\mathcal F_\pm} = r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm}(\mathscr V_E)$, hence $\varepsilon^{\mathcal F_\pm}_E(r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm}(w)) = (r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm})^{-1}(r^E_{\mathcal F_\pm}(w)) = w$. So $$\varepsilon^{\mathcal F_+}_\mathcal F(r^E_{\mathcal F_+}(w)) = \varepsilon^E_\mathcal F(w) = \varepsilon^{\mathcal F_-}_\mathcal F(r^E_{\mathcal F_-}(w)).$$

Hence $p_\mathcal F^\bullet(\partial(E \otimes w)) = 0$, proving (P1) for 1-cells.

For general $d$-cells, the boundary $\partial$ is an alternating sum over (d-1)-faces; the same argument applied to each pair of codimension-1 faces (or, equivalently, to the equivalence relation on the boundary 0-chain) gives vanishing.

Composition $p_\mathcal F^\bullet \circ i_\mathcal F = \id$. For $v \in \mathscr V_\mathcal F$: $i_\mathcal F(v) = [x \otimes r^\mathcal F_x(v)]$, so $$p_\mathcal F^\bullet(i_\mathcal F(v)) = \varepsilon^x_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal F_x(v)).$$ Using the definition of $\varepsilon^x_\mathcal F$ (projection-then-lift, since $x \preceq \mathcal F$): $$\varepsilon^x_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal F_x(v)) = (r^\mathcal F_x)^{-1}(e_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal F_x(v))).$$ But $r^\mathcal F_x(v) \in r^\mathcal F_x(\mathscr V_\mathcal F) = e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_x$ already, so $e_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal F_x(v)) = r^\mathcal F_x(v)$. Hence $\varepsilon^x_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal F_x(v)) = (r^\mathcal F_x)^{-1}(r^\mathcal F_x(v)) = v$.

So $p_\mathcal F^\bullet \circ i_\mathcal F = \id_{\mathscr V_\mathcal F}$. ✓

This forces $i_\mathcal F$ to be injective: if $i_\mathcal F(v) = 0$, then $v = p_\mathcal F^\bullet(i_\mathcal F(v)) = p_\mathcal F^\bullet(0) = 0$. $\square$

Combining surjectivity + injectivity

By surjectivity (above), $e_\mathcal F H_0 \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$. By definition of $i_\mathcal F$ (image in $\phi_\mathcal F$-isotypic), $i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F) \subseteq e_\mathcal F H_0$. So $i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F) = e_\mathcal F H_0$.

By injectivity, $i_\mathcal F: \mathscr V_\mathcal F \to i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F) = e_\mathcal F H_0$ is a bijection. As an $R$-linear map between $R$-modules, this is an isomorphism. $\square$

Final form of the theorem (the expected equivalence)

Theorem (cartesian equivalence). Assume $(T1)$-$(T5)$ and Hypothesis $(S)$ ($\Rep_\Phi(G)$ is a Serre subcategory of $\Rep(G)$). Then $$\Loc_\phi : \Rep_\Phi(G) \;\xrightleftarrows[\sim]{\sim}\; \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi) : H_0 \circ C_\bullet$$ is a pair of quasi-inverse equivalences of $R$-linear categories.

Proof
  • $\Loc_\phi$ lands in $\Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G$: immediate from the definition of cartesian (any $V \in \Rep_\Phi(G)$ has $\Loc_\phi(V)_\mathcal F = V[\phi_\mathcal F]$ cartesian by (T2)).
  • $H_0 \circ C_\bullet$ lands in $\Rep_\Phi(G)$: by definition of $\Rep_\Phi(G)$ as generated by $\bigcup_x V[\phi_x]$, and the construction of $C_\bullet$ from $\phi$-isotypic stalks.
  • Counit iso: apply the unit-iso theorem above at $\Loc_\phi V$ (which is cartesian); the unit-iso $\Loc_\phi V \xrightarrow\sim \Loc_\phi(H_0 C_\bullet \Loc_\phi V)$ combined with the adjunction triangle identity gives the counit iso $H_0 C_\bullet \Loc_\phi V \xrightarrow\sim V$. (Under the Lanard cartesian framework this supersedes the older §7.1 contraction-based resolution argument.)
  • Unit iso: the theorem above. $\square$

This matches [Lan19 Thm 4.10/4.11] and [DF Prop 3.2.4] in form. The proof strategy mirrors Lanard's but specialized to our setting: - We work on the apartment $A$ (via §7.3 reduction) instead of the full building, so Wang-style taut paths suffice in place of Lanard's admissible paths. - Our single-character $\phi$ collapses the wild-inertia torsor sum that appears in DF, removing the orthogonality / equivalence-class bookkeeping of [Lan19 Lem 2.6.1] / [DF Lem 2.6.1].

Where the single-character simplification enters

In DF's setting, $e_{\phi,I,x} = \sum_{\iota \in I_x/\sim} e_{\iota,x}$ is a sum of "pure" idempotents indexed by a wild-inertia equivalence class. Lanard's iterated identity $e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$ then requires orthogonality between sub-idempotents to avoid double counting (Lemma 2.6.1 of DF).

In our setting, $e_\mathcal F = e_{\phi_\mathcal F}$ is a single idempotent. Steps S2-S4 above proceed directly without invoking any orthogonality lemma. The K_x-extension (Step S5) inherits the same simplification.

This is the substantive simplification that lets us omit the torsor / equivalence-class machinery of [Lan19 §2] / [DF §2.6].
