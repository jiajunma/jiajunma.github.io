Idempotent system extracted from (T1)–(T5)

This lemma packages the (T1)–(T5) axioms into a small Hecke-algebraic framework that drives the §7.2 proof. After it is established, the remainder of the cartesian-unit-iso argument depends only on the abstract properties (i)–(v), never returning to $\Phi$ itself.

Setting and notation

Assume $\Phi = (\mathscr K_+, \phi)$ is a compatible system of semisimple characters on a generalized building $\BSigma$, satisfying the relevant subset of (T1)–(T5) for each property below (see [[node:coefficient_systems.semisimple_character_T_axioms]]).

Bourbaki-style hypothesis pruning. This lemma is purely Hecke-algebraic; it uses no building-side hypothesis (K1)–(K3). The proofs of (i)–(v) consume only: - (v) Support: integral definition (no T-axiom); - (iv) $G$-equivariance: (T3); - (i) Face compatibility: (T2) + (T3); - (ii) Adjacent commutation: (T2) + (T5); - (iii) Geodesic telescoping (unconditional): (T4b) + (T5).

No (K1), (K2), (K3) appears in any proof. They were listed in earlier drafts as "ambient setting" but are not logically required.

For each facet $\mathcal F$ of $\BSigma$, the idempotent $e_\mathcal F$ is defined by $$e_\mathcal F \;:=\; \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{\mathcal F,+})} \int_{K_{\mathcal F,+}} \phi_\mathcal F^{-1}(k)\, k \, dk \;\in\; \mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+}).$$

For any point $x \in \BSigma$ we set $e_x := e_{\mathcal F_x}$, where $\mathcal F_x$ is the unique facet containing $x$ in its interior. By (T1) this matches the direct definition $e_x = e_{\phi_x}$.

For a smooth $R[G]$-module $V$, $e_\mathcal F \cdot V = V[\phi_\mathcal F]$, the $\phi_\mathcal F$-isotypic submodule for the $K_{\mathcal F,+}$-action.

The ring $\mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F)$ is the Hecke algebra of locally constant, compactly supported $R$-valued functions on $\hat K_\mathcal F := \Fix_G(\mathcal F)$, with convolution. The idempotent $e_\mathcal F$ is central in $\mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+})$ because $\phi_\mathcal F$ is a character.

Statement

Lemma. The family $\{e_\mathcal F\}_\mathcal F$ satisfies the following identities in the appropriate Hecke algebras:

(i) Face compatibility. For $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$ (i.e. $\mathcal E \subseteq \overline{\mathcal F}$, $\mathcal E$ smaller), $$e_\mathcal E \, e_\mathcal F \;=\; e_\mathcal F \, e_\mathcal E \;=\; e_\mathcal F$$ as elements of $\mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F)$.

(ii) Adjacent commutation. For two facets $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ both contained in $\overline{\mathcal G}$ for some facet $\mathcal G$, $$e_\mathcal F \, e_{\mathcal F'} \;=\; e_{\mathcal F'} \, e_\mathcal F$$ as elements of $\mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal G)$. In particular this holds whenever $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ are adjacent (share a common bigger facet).

(iii) Geodesic telescoping — unconditional. For any points $x, y \in A$ and any $z \in [x, y]$ (closed line segment), with no facet relation assumed between $\mathcal F_x, \mathcal F_y, \mathcal F_z$, $$e_x \, e_z \, e_y \;=\; e_x \, e_y$$ as operators on every smooth $R[G]$-module $V$.

(iv) $G$-equivariance. For $g \in G$ and any facet $\mathcal F$, $$e_{g \mathcal F} \;=\; g \, e_\mathcal F \, g^{-1}.$$

(v) Support. $e_\mathcal F \in \mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+}) \subseteq \mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F)$. In particular, $e_x \in \mathcal H_R(\hat K_x)$ for every $x \in \BSigma$, and (v) implies the "condition $(*)$" of [Lan19, §1.1].

Proof

(v) Support (warm-up)

Immediate from the definition: the integrand of $e_\mathcal F$ is supported on $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal F$. The "in particular" part follows since $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal F \subseteq \hat K_x$ for any $x \in \overline{\mathcal F}$ (the chain $\Fix_G(\overline{\mathcal F}) \subseteq \Fix_G(\overline{\mathcal F'})$ when $\mathcal F \supseteq \mathcal F'$).

(iv) $G$-equivariance

By (T3), $K_{g\mathcal F,+} = {}^g K_{\mathcal F,+} = g K_{\mathcal F,+} g^{-1}$ and $\phi_{g\mathcal F} = {}^g\phi_\mathcal F$, i.e.\ $\phi_{g\mathcal F}(g h g^{-1}) = \phi_\mathcal F(h)$. Substituting $k = ghg^{-1}$ in the integral defining $e_{g\mathcal F}$, $$e_{g\mathcal F} = \int_{gK_{\mathcal F,+}g^{-1}} \phi_{g\mathcal F}^{-1}(k)\, k\, dk = \int_{K_{\mathcal F,+}} \phi_\mathcal F^{-1}(h) \, g h g^{-1} \, dh = g \biggl(\int_{K_{\mathcal F,+}} \phi_\mathcal F^{-1}(h)\, h\, dh \biggr) g^{-1} = g\, e_\mathcal F\, g^{-1}. \quad\square$$

(i) Face compatibility

Let $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$. By (T2): $$K_{\mathcal E,+} \subseteq K_{\mathcal F,+}, \qquad \phi_\mathcal F\bigl|_{K_{\mathcal E,+}} = \phi_\mathcal E.$$

Claim 1. $K_{\mathcal F,+}$ normalizes $K_{\mathcal E,+}$ and fixes the character $\phi_\mathcal E$ under conjugation.

Proof. $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal F \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal E$, since pointwise-fixing $\overline{\mathcal F}$ implies pointwise-fixing $\overline{\mathcal E} \subseteq \overline{\mathcal F}$. Now $K_{\mathcal E,+}$ is normal in $\hat K_\mathcal E$ (standard, from the construction of $\mathscr K_+$ as a pro-$p$ sheaf normal in the fixator), so $K_{\mathcal F,+}$ normalizes $K_{\mathcal E,+}$. And $\hat K_\mathcal E$ fixes $\phi_\mathcal E$ by (T3) applied with $g \in \hat K_\mathcal E$ ($g\mathcal E = \mathcal E \Rightarrow {}^g \phi_\mathcal E = \phi_\mathcal E$), so $K_{\mathcal F,+}$ does too. $\square$

Claim 2. $e_\mathcal F V \subseteq e_\mathcal E V$ for any smooth $R[G]$-module $V$.

Proof. If $v \in V[\phi_\mathcal F]$ and $k \in K_{\mathcal E,+} \subseteq K_{\mathcal F,+}$, then $kv = \phi_\mathcal F(k) v = \phi_\mathcal E(k) v$ by (T2). So $v \in V[\phi_\mathcal E]$. $\square$

Claim 3. $e_\mathcal E \, e_\mathcal F = e_\mathcal F$ as operators on any $V$, hence as elements of $\mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F)$.

Proof. For any $v \in V$: $e_\mathcal F v \in V[\phi_\mathcal F] \subseteq V[\phi_\mathcal E]$ by Claim 2. Since $e_\mathcal E$ acts as identity on $V[\phi_\mathcal E]$, $e_\mathcal E(e_\mathcal F v) = e_\mathcal F v$. $\square$

Claim 4. $e_\mathcal F \, e_\mathcal E = e_\mathcal F$.

Proof. By Claim 1, $K_{\mathcal F,+}$ preserves $V[\phi_\mathcal E]$ (it normalizes $K_{\mathcal E,+}$ and fixes the character). Hence so does $e_\mathcal F$. Restricted to $V[\phi_\mathcal E]$, $e_\mathcal F$ remains the projection onto $V[\phi_\mathcal F]$. For any $v \in V$: $e_\mathcal E v \in V[\phi_\mathcal E]$, and then $e_\mathcal F(e_\mathcal E v) = $ projection of $e_\mathcal E v$ to $V[\phi_\mathcal F]$. On the other hand $e_\mathcal F v = $ projection of $v$ to $V[\phi_\mathcal F]$. Since $V[\phi_\mathcal F] \subseteq V[\phi_\mathcal E]$ and the projections (averaging operators) decompose the ambient $V$ into mutually orthogonal isotypic components, the two coincide. $\square$ $\square$

(ii) Adjacent commutation

Let $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$ be facets with $\mathcal F, \mathcal F' \preceq \mathcal G$ for some $\mathcal G$. Choose $x \in \mathcal F$, $y \in \mathcal F'$, and pick $z \in \mathcal G^\circ$ (interior). Then by (T5), for $z$ on the segment $[x, y]$ (which lies in $\overline{\mathcal G}$ by convexity since $A$ is Euclidean and $\overline{\mathcal G}$ is convex): $$K_{z,+} = \bigl(K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+}\bigr) \bigl(K_{y,+} \cap K_{z,+}\bigr).$$

But $\mathcal F, \mathcal F' \preceq \mathcal G \succeq \mathcal F_z$ implies (with some care about which facet $z$ actually lies in) $K_{x,+}, K_{y,+} \subseteq K_{z,+}$ when $\mathcal F_z$ is the maximal face among those containing the segment $[x,y]$. Hence $K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+} = K_{x,+}$ and similarly for $y$, so (T5) becomes: $$K_{z,+} \;=\; K_{x,+} \cdot K_{y,+}.$$

Claim 5. $\phi_z$ restricts to $\phi_x$ on $K_{x,+}$ and to $\phi_y$ on $K_{y,+}$, and to a common character on $K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}$.

Proof. The first two statements are (T2) applied to $\mathcal F_x \preceq \mathcal F_z$ and $\mathcal F_y \preceq \mathcal F_z$. The third is automatic by restriction of $\phi_z$. $\square$

Now we compute $e_x \, e_y$ in $\mathcal H_R(K_{z,+})$ using the factorization $K_{z,+} = K_{x,+} K_{y,+}$. The natural surjection $K_{x,+} \times K_{y,+} \twoheadrightarrow K_{z,+}$ has fibre $K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}$, so $$|K_{z,+}| = \frac{|K_{x,+}| \cdot |K_{y,+}|}{|K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}|}$$ (reading $|\cdot|$ as Haar volume).

For $k \in K_{z,+}$, factor $k = k_x k_y$. By Claim 5, $\phi_z(k_x k_y) = \phi_x(k_x) \phi_y(k_y)$ is well-defined on $K_{x,+} K_{y,+}$ (independent of the choice of factorization, modulo the intersection, because $\phi_x = \phi_y$ on $K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}$). Then $$e_z \;=\; \frac{1}{|K_{z,+}|} \int_{K_{z,+}} \phi_z^{-1}(k)\, k \, dk \;=\; \frac{|K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}|}{|K_{x,+}|\cdot|K_{y,+}|} \cdot \frac{1}{|K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}|} \int_{K_{x,+}} \int_{K_{y,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(k_x) \phi_y^{-1}(k_y) \, k_x k_y \, dk_y \, dk_x$$ $$= \frac{1}{|K_{x,+}|\cdot|K_{y,+}|} \int \int \phi_x^{-1}(k_x) \phi_y^{-1}(k_y) \, k_x k_y \;=\; e_x \, e_y.$$

So $e_x \, e_y = e_z$. By the symmetric calculation (parametrize $K_{z,+} = K_{y,+} K_{x,+}$), $e_y \, e_x = e_z$ as well. Hence $$e_x \, e_y \;=\; e_y \, e_x \;=\; e_z. \quad\square$$

(Here $x, y, z$ are points; the same identities hold facet-level by (T1) and the definition $e_x = e_{\mathcal F_x}$.)

(iii) Geodesic telescoping — unconditional

Setting: $x, y \in A$ arbitrary, $z \in [x, y]$. We make no facet relation hypothesis between $\mathcal F_x, \mathcal F_y, \mathcal F_z$.

Abbreviate $K_{x,z,+} := K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+}$ and $K_{y,z,+} := K_{y,+} \cap K_{z,+}$.

Step 1 — character agreement. By (T4b) (the $g = e$ consequence of (T4), see [[node:coefficient_systems.semisimple_character_T_axioms]] applied to the pairs $(x, z)$ and $(y, z)$): $$\phi_x = \phi_z \text{ on } K_{x,z,+}, \qquad \phi_y = \phi_z \text{ on } K_{y,z,+}.$$

Step 2 — geodesic factorization. By (T5) at $z$: $$K_{z,+} \;=\; K_{x,z,+} \cdot K_{y,z,+}.$$ The multiplication map $K_{x,z,+} \times K_{y,z,+} \twoheadrightarrow K_{z,+}$ has fibre $K_{x,z,+} \cap K_{y,z,+}$, so $$\mathrm{vol}(K_{z,+}) \cdot \mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+} \cap K_{y,z,+}) \;=\; \mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+}) \cdot \mathrm{vol}(K_{y,z,+}).$$

Step 3 — computation of $e_z$ on $V[\phi_y]$. Take $w \in V$ and let $v := e_y(w) \in V[\phi_y]$. For $b \in K_{y,z,+} \subseteq K_{y,+}$, $bv = \phi_y(b)\,v$, so $\phi_y^{-1}(b)\, bv = v$. Using Step 1 to write $\phi_z^{-1}(ab) = \phi_x^{-1}(a)\,\phi_y^{-1}(b)$ for $(a, b) \in K_{x,z,+} \times K_{y,z,+}$:

$$\begin{aligned} e_z(v) &= \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{z,+})} \int_{K_{z,+}} \phi_z^{-1}(k)\, kv\, dk \\ &= \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+})\,\mathrm{vol}(K_{y,z,+})} \int_{K_{x,z,+}} \int_{K_{y,z,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(a)\,\phi_y^{-1}(b)\, abv\, db\, da \\ &= \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+})\,\mathrm{vol}(K_{y,z,+})} \int_{K_{x,z,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(a)\, a \cdot \bigl[\mathrm{vol}(K_{y,z,+})\, v\bigr]\, da \\ &= \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+})} \int_{K_{x,z,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(a)\, av\, da \;=:\; \widetilde e_{x,z}(v), \end{aligned}$$

the "partial $e_x$" averaging only over $K_{x,z,+} \subseteq K_{x,+}$ against $\phi_x|_{K_{x,z,+}}$.

Step 4 — absorption $e_x \circ \widetilde e_{x,z} = e_x$. Change of variable $k = ha$ for $h \in K_{x,+}$, $a \in K_{x,z,+} \subseteq K_{x,+}$ (so $k$ ranges over $K_{x,+}$ for each fixed $a$): $$e_x\bigl(\widetilde e_{x,z}(u)\bigr) = \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,+})\,\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,z,+})} \int_{K_{x,z,+}} \int_{K_{x,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(k)\, ku\, dk\, da = \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{x,+})}\int_{K_{x,+}} \phi_x^{-1}(k)\, ku\, dk = e_x(u).$$

Conclusion. For any $w \in V$: $$e_x e_z e_y(w) = e_x(e_z(v)) = e_x(\widetilde e_{x,z}(v)) = e_x(v) = e_x e_y(w).$$

So $e_x \, e_z \, e_y = e_x \, e_y$ unconditionally. $\square$

Remark — comparison with Lanard

Lanard's "consistent system of idempotents" axioms [Lan19 Def 1.1] are:

  1. $e_x e_y = e_y e_x$ when $x, y$ are adjacent vertices — this is our (ii) restricted to vertices.
  2. $e_x e_z e_y = e_x e_y$ when $z \in H(x,y)$ is adjacent to $x$ — this is our (iii) for points on a simplicial hull, restricted to vertices.
  3. $e_{gx} = g e_x g^{-1}$ — this is our (iv).

Lanard's condition (*) "for every vertex $x$ and every polysimplex $\sigma$ containing $x$, $e_\sigma \in \mathcal H_R(G_x)$" is implied by our (v).

Our facet-level (T1)+(T2) gives (i) — Lanard derives the facet-level analogue from his vertex-level axioms via consistency; we get it directly from (T2).

So (i)–(v) is a slightly stronger / more direct axiomatization of the same Hecke-algebraic structure that Lanard / Meyer–Solleveld work with. The rest of the §7.2 proof uses only (i)–(v).

Conditional (ii) versus unconditional (iii)

Property (ii) (adjacent commutation) does require a common bigger facet $\mathcal G$ for the convolution computation to identify $K_{x,+}, K_{y,+}$ as subgroups of a common $K_{z,+}$ — the argument needs $K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+} = K_{x,+}$ which requires $\mathcal F_x \preceq \mathcal F_z$.

Property (iii) (geodesic telescoping) is unconditional, with no facet relation required, because it goes through a different route: the convolution proof handles only one factor at a time (one of $K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+}$, the other of $K_{y,+} \cap K_{z,+}$) and uses (T4b) to make $\phi_z$-restrictions agree with $\phi_x$ / $\phi_y$ on those intersections. The final step uses absorption of a "partial $e_x$" into the full $e_x$, which holds for any subgroup $A \subseteq K_{x,+}$.

For the §7.2 apartment-level proof, the strong form of (iii) is what is needed when iterating the Lanard 4.9 identity along a taut path that crosses different chambers — different cells visited need not share a common bigger facet.
