Lemma Loc_Φ: Rep_Φ(G) → Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) and H_0 adjunction
lemma staged
#

Uses

  • Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) with axioms (EC1)–(EC4)
  • The subcategory Rep_Φ(G)
  • Resolution theorem: C_•(Φ, V) → V (Thm 7.1.7)

$\Loc_\Phi$ and its left adjoint $H_0$

Define $$\Loc_\Phi : \Rep_\Phi(G) \to \Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi), \qquad (\Loc_\Phi V)_\mathcal{F} := V[\phi_\mathcal{F}],$$ with restrictions the natural inclusions (justified by (T2)) and the obvious $G$-action.

For $\mathscr{V} \in \Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$, define the chain complex $$C_d(\BSigma, \mathscr{V}) := \mathrm{span}\{\mathcal{F} \otimes v : \mathcal{F}\in [\![\BSigma]\!]_d, v \in \mathscr{V}_\mathcal{F}\}$$ with diagonal $G$-action and boundary $\partial(\mathcal{F} \otimes v) = \sum_{\mathcal{E} \subseteq \overline{\mathcal{F}}} \epsilon(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{E})\, \mathcal{E} \otimes r^\mathcal{F}_\mathcal{E}(v)$. Write $H_i(\mathscr{V}) := H_i(C_\bullet(\BSigma, \mathscr{V}))$.

When $\mathscr{V} = \Loc_\Phi(V)$, $C_d(\BSigma, \Loc_\Phi V) = C_d(\Phi, V)$, so the resolution theorem Resolution theorem: C_•(Φ, V) → V (Thm 7.1.7) gives:

Lemma (counit). $\mathrm{counit}_V : H_0(\Loc_\Phi V) \to V$ is a natural isomorphism.

The unit $(\mathrm{unit}\mathscr{V})\mathcal{F} := q_\mathscr{V} \circ r^\mathcal{F}x : \mathscr{V}\mathcal{F} \to H_0(\mathscr{V})[\phi_\mathcal{F}]$ is independent of the chosen vertex $x \in \overline{\mathcal{F}}$ (small lemma using path connectivity inside $\overline{\mathcal{F}}$).

Lemma. $H_0$ is left adjoint to $\Loc_\Phi$.

Proof

We verify both triangle identities of the adjunction.

Triangle 1. For $V \in \Rep_\Phi(G)$, must show $\Loc_\Phi(\mathrm{counit}_V) \circ \mathrm{unit}_{\Loc_\Phi V} = \id$.

The counit $\mathrm{counit}_V : H_0(\Loc_\Phi V) \to V$ is, in representation terms, the surjection $[\mathcal F \otimes v] \mapsto v$ (well-defined: $v \in (\Loc_\Phi V)_\mathcal F = V[\phi_\mathcal F] \subseteq V$); the resolution theorem (Resolution theorem: C_•(Φ, V) → V (Thm 7.1.7)) makes it an iso. Applying $\Loc_\Phi$ at facet $\mathcal F$ gives the map $\mathrm{counit}_V|_{V[\phi_\mathcal F]} : V[\phi_\mathcal F] \to V[\phi_\mathcal F]$, which is the identity.

The unit $(\mathrm{unit}{\Loc\Phi V})\mathcal F : V[\phi\mathcal F] \to H_0(\Loc_\Phi V)[\phi_\mathcal F]$ sends $v$ to $q([\mathcal F \otimes v])$, the 0-homology class. Composing with $\Loc_\Phi(\mathrm{counit}_V)$ yields $v$ back, so the composite is $\id_{V[\phi_\mathcal F]}$, as required.

Triangle 2. For $\mathscr V \in \Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$, must show $\mathrm{counit}_{H_0 \mathscr V} \circ H_0(\mathrm{unit}_\mathscr V) = \id$.

The unit $(\mathrm{unit}_\mathscr V)_\mathcal F$ is the map $q_\mathscr V \circ r^\mathcal F_x$, choosing any vertex $x \in \overline{\mathcal F}$; it factors as $\mathscr V_\mathcal F \xrightarrow{r^\mathcal F_x} \mathscr V_x \xrightarrow{q_\mathscr V} H_0(\mathscr V)[\phi_\mathcal F]$. Applying $H_0$ gives $$H_0(\mathrm{unit}_\mathscr V) : H_0(\mathscr V) \to H_0(\Loc_\Phi H_0(\mathscr V)), \qquad [\mathcal F \otimes v] \mapsto [\mathcal F \otimes q_\mathscr V(r^\mathcal F_x(v))].$$ By the cell-poset relation $\mathcal F \succeq x$, the class $[\mathcal F \otimes q_\mathscr V(r^\mathcal F_x(v))]$ equals $[x \otimes q_\mathscr V(r^\mathcal F_x(v))]$ in $H_0(\Loc_\Phi H_0(\mathscr V))$ (boundary computation).

Then $\mathrm{counit}_{H_0 \mathscr V}$ sends this back to $q_\mathscr V(r^\mathcal F_x(v)) \in H_0(\mathscr V)$, which by construction equals $[\mathcal F \otimes v]$. So the composite is $\id_{H_0(\mathscr V)}$.

Both triangle identities hold, so $H_0 \dashv \Loc_\Phi$. $\square$

Why this matters

The adjunction $H_0 \dashv \Loc_\Phi$ is the engine of §7.4: combined with Gabriel–Zisman localization, the counit-iso half (Triangle 1) upgrades to a full localized equivalence $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)[E_\Phi^{-1}] \simeq \Rep_\Phi(G)$ (Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ)[E_Φ⁻¹] ≃ Rep_Φ(G) (§7.4 Thm)). Triangle 2 by itself does not need cartesianness — it holds for all coefficient systems in $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$; cartesianness enters only when demanding the unit itself (not just $H_0$-applied) to be an iso, which is the §7.2 unit-iso theorem (Cartesian cosheaves Cosh^cart_G(X,R,φ) and unit isomorphism).
