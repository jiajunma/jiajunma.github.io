Local maps $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ along taut paths in $A$

This node delivers Steps 3 and 4 of Proof plan: Cartesian unit-iso via apartment-level system of idempotents: the construction of local maps $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ between facet values of a cartesian cosheaf, indexed by taut paths in the apartment $A$, together with the proof that they depend only on the endpoints.

Setting

Throughout: $\mathscr V \in \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi)$ (cartesian cosheaf), $A \subseteq \BSigma$ is the apartment, equipped with the polysimplicial structure from (F1)–(F4). Idempotents $e_\mathcal F$ (for facets $\mathcal F$ of $A$) and their abstract properties (i)–(v) come from [[node:coefficient_systems.idempotent_system_from_T_axioms]].

For $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$ (i.e. $\mathcal E \subseteq \overline{\mathcal F}$, $\mathcal E$ smaller), the cosheaf restriction is $$r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E \;:\; \mathscr V_\mathcal F \longrightarrow \mathscr V_\mathcal E.$$ The cartesian condition says: $r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E$ identifies $\mathscr V_\mathcal F$ with $e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_\mathcal E \subseteq \mathscr V_\mathcal E$.

Elementary local maps

For an adjacent pair of facets $\sigma, \tau$ in $A$ (i.e. $\sigma \preceq \tau$ or $\tau \preceq \sigma$), define the elementary local map $\varepsilon^\sigma_\tau : \mathscr V_\sigma \to \mathscr V_\tau$ as follows:

  • Inclusion case ($\tau \preceq \sigma$, target smaller): set $$\varepsilon^\sigma_\tau \;:=\; r^\sigma_\tau \;:\; \mathscr V_\sigma \longrightarrow \mathscr V_\tau.$$ This is just the cosheaf restriction, which by cartesianness identifies $\mathscr V_\sigma$ with $e_\sigma \mathscr V_\tau$ inside $\mathscr V_\tau$.
  • Projection case ($\sigma \preceq \tau$, source smaller): set $$\varepsilon^\sigma_\tau \;:=\; (r^\tau_\sigma)^{-1} \circ e_\tau \;:\; \mathscr V_\sigma \twoheadrightarrow e_\tau \mathscr V_\sigma \xrightarrow{(r^\tau_\sigma)^{-1}} \mathscr V_\tau.$$ Here $e_\tau$ projects $\mathscr V_\sigma$ onto its $\phi_\tau$-isotypic part, which by cartesianness is the image $r^\tau_\sigma(\mathscr V_\tau)$; the inverse of $r^\tau_\sigma$ on this image lifts back to $\mathscr V_\tau$.

These elementary maps satisfy:

  • $\varepsilon^\sigma_\sigma = \id_{\mathscr V_\sigma}$.
  • If $\sigma \preceq \tau \preceq \omega$, then $\varepsilon^\tau_\omega \circ \varepsilon^\sigma_\tau = \varepsilon^\sigma_\omega$ (both are projections then inclusions through the chain).
  • If $\omega \preceq \tau \preceq \sigma$, then $\varepsilon^\tau_\omega \circ \varepsilon^\sigma_\tau = \varepsilon^\sigma_\omega$ (cosheaf composition: $r^\tau_\omega \circ r^\sigma_\tau = r^\sigma_\omega$).

These are immediate from the definitions and the cosheaf axioms; the verification uses property (i) of $\{e_\mathcal F\}$ at each step (to identify $\phi$-isotypic parts across face inclusions).

Taut paths

Rigorous definition

Throughout, $A$ is a finite-dimensional Euclidean polysimplicial complex satisfying (F1)–(F4); $\mathfrak F(A)$ denotes its set of facets, partially ordered by $\sigma \preceq \tau \iff \sigma \subseteq \overline\tau$, and $A = \coprod_{\sigma \in \mathfrak F(A)} \sigma^\circ$ is the open cell decomposition.

Definition (Taut path). Let $\sigma, \tau \in \mathfrak F(A)$. A taut path from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ in $A$ is a tuple $$\mathcal P \;=\; \bigl(\, x,\, y\, ;\; 0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n = 1\, ;\; \sigma_0, \sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n \,\bigr)$$ satisfying:

(T-pts) $x \in \sigma^\circ$ and $y \in \tau^\circ$. Define $\gamma : [0,1] \to A$ by $\gamma(t) := (1-t)x + ty$. Convexity of $A$ (Euclidean apartment) gives $[x,y] = \gamma([0,1]) \subseteq A$.

(T-cells) $\sigma_i \in \mathfrak F(A)$, $\sigma_0 = \sigma$, $\sigma_n = \tau$.

(T-realize) For each $i = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$, $\gamma\bigl((t_i, t_{i+1})\bigr) \subseteq \sigma_i^\circ$, and the endpoints $\gamma(0) = x$, $\gamma(1) = y$ lie in $\sigma_0^\circ$ and $\sigma_n^\circ$ respectively (this is just (T-pts) restated).

(T-adjacent) For each $i = 0, \dots, n-1$, $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma_{i+1}$ are face-comparable: $\sigma_i \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$ or $\sigma_{i+1} \preceq \sigma_i$.

(T-minimal) $\sigma_i \ne \sigma_{i+1}$ for all $i$ (no stuttering; the $t_i$ are genuine cell crossings).

Two taut paths are equivalent iff they share both $(x, y)$ and the facet sequence $(\sigma_0, \dots, \sigma_n)$; the transition times $t_i$ are then forced by $\gamma$ and the cell decomposition. The content of the well-definedness Proposition below is that the further dependence on $(x, y)$ drops out at the level of $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$.

Immediate corollaries of the definition

(C1) Transition points lie on common faces. At each $t_i$ ($1 \le i \le n-1$), $\gamma(t_i) \in \overline{\sigma_{i-1}} \cap \overline{\sigma_i}$. By (F2), this intersection is the closure of a common face $\rho_i \preceq \sigma_{i-1}, \sigma_i$. Combined with (T-adjacent), $\rho_i$ is whichever of $\sigma_{i-1}, \sigma_i$ is the smaller one.

(C2) No imposed dimension monotonicity. Unlike Coxeter galleries, $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma_{i+1}$ may differ in dimension by any amount; the only constraint is face-comparability.

(C3) Existence. For any $\sigma, \tau \in \mathfrak F(A)$ and any $x \in \sigma^\circ, y \in \tau^\circ$, the segment $[x,y]$ meets only finitely many cells (local finiteness of $A$). Indexing them in order along $\gamma$ yields a taut path.

(C4) Non-uniqueness. Different choices of $(x, y)$ may yield different facet sequences. Typical failure: $\gamma$ travels along the relative interior of a lower-dimensional face $\rho$ of some cell; (T-realize) then forces $\sigma_i = \rho$, but a small perturbation of $(x, y)$ makes $\gamma$ skirt $\rho$ through higher-dimensional cells, producing a different taut path with the same endpoint facets $(\sigma, \tau)$. The well-definedness Proposition handles exactly this.

Equivalent formulations

The following are equivalent to the rigorous definition above:

  • (Gallery form) A minimal-length walk in the face-comparability graph (vertices = facets, edges = face-comparable pairs) from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ that is realized by some straight segment $[x, y]$ with $x \in \sigma^\circ, y \in \tau^\circ$.
  • (Piecewise-affine form) A continuous injection $\gamma : [0,1] \to A$, piecewise affine with all pieces sharing one common direction (hence globally affine = a straight segment), with $\gamma(0) \in \sigma^\circ, \gamma(1) \in \tau^\circ$, together with the induced facet sequence.

Contrast with Lanard's admissible paths

Caution on terminology. The phrase "taut path" originates with Wang [Wan17 §2.3] (unpublished manuscript) (unpublished manuscript) in the $\mathrm{GL}_n$ setting. Lanard [Lan19, §2] does not use taut paths; he introduces a different, more general notion called admissible paths, designed to cope with arbitrary reductive $G$ where the simplicial hull of two polysimplices need not be a simplex.

Our setting can use Wang taut paths in place of Lanard admissible paths because we first reduce to a single apartment $A$ (Reduction lemma: cartesian unit-iso check transfers to apartment) which is convex Euclidean. Lanard cannot reduce to a single apartment because his hulls $H(\sigma, \tau)$ generically do not lie in one.

Consequence for terminology. Throughout this node and the rest of the §7.2 proof, "taut path" always means the Definition above — Wang-style, in an apartment, realized by a straight segment. The phrase never refers to Lanard's admissible paths, and we never invoke Lanard's path-equivalence machinery as a black box.

Composed local map along a taut path

Given a taut path $\sigma_0, \dots, \sigma_n$ from $\sigma_0 = \tau$ to $\sigma_n = \sigma$ (note the source-target convention: $\tau$ is the source, $\sigma$ the target), define $$\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma \;:=\; \varepsilon^{\sigma_{n-1}}_{\sigma_n} \circ \cdots \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_0}_{\sigma_1} : \mathscr V_\tau \longrightarrow \mathscr V_\sigma.$$

Well-definedness (Step 4)

Proposition (Well-definedness). $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ depends only on $\tau$ and $\sigma$, not on the chosen taut path.

The proof follows Wang [Wan17 §2.3] (Chuijia Wang, unpublished manuscript by Sheaf7 co-author) (Chuijia Wang, unpublished manuscript by Sheaf7 co-author) adapted to facet-level idempotents. The key observation, which makes the apartment case simpler than Lanard's, is that in the Euclidean apartment $A$ two taut paths between the same endpoints differ by a finite sequence of "elementary moves" that can be analyzed using properties (ii) and (iii) of $\{e_\mathcal F\}$ directly.

Strategy

The proof proceeds in three reductions:

  1. Local independence at one cell crossing. Show that if two paths differ by a single "detour" through one adjacent facet (an elementary move of the form $\sigma_i, \sigma_{i+1} \rightsquigarrow \sigma_i, \sigma', \sigma_{i+1}$ where $\sigma' \in \overline{\sigma_i} \cap \overline{\sigma_{i+1}}$ or vice versa), the composed map is unchanged.
  2. Adjacent transposition. Any two taut paths from $\tau$ to $\sigma$ differ by a finite sequence of moves of type (1), via the standard apartment-geometry argument.
  3. Combine.

Proof of (1) — single-detour invariance

A taut path is induced by a straight line segment in $A$. The elementary moves that can change a taut path without changing its endpoints are exactly:

Insert (or delete) one intermediate facet $\sigma'$ between consecutive $\sigma_i, \sigma_{i+1}$ such that $\sigma_i \preceq \sigma' \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$ (or with all face relations reversed).

(Note: it is NOT a valid elementary move to insert a $\sigma'$ that is bigger than both $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma_{i+1}$ — the line segment that defines the taut path does not detour into a bigger cell, by (T-realize). Similarly, inserting a $\sigma'$ that is smaller than both, where neither $\sigma_i$ nor $\sigma_{i+1}$ contains the other, does not arise either — that would require $\sigma_i, \sigma_{i+1}$ to be non-face-comparable in the original path, contradicting (T-adjacent).)

So there are essentially two cases:

  • Case C (intermediate, ascending): $\sigma_i \preceq \sigma' \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$.
  • Case D (intermediate, descending): $\sigma_i \succeq \sigma' \succeq \sigma_{i+1}$.

Both are about refining the face inclusion $\sigma_i \leftrightarrow \sigma_{i+1}$ by an intermediate cell.

Case C ($\sigma_i \preceq \sigma' \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$).

We need $\varepsilon^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}} \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'} = \varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}}$. All three maps are "projection-then-lift" type ($\sigma_i$ smaller in each pair): - $\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}}(v) = (r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i})^{-1}(e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(v))$ - $\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v) = (r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i})^{-1}(e_{\sigma'}(v))$ - $\varepsilon^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}}(u) = (r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma'})^{-1}(e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(u))$

Both target $\mathscr V_{\sigma_{i+1}}$; we can compare them by applying the (injective) cartesian iso $r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}: \mathscr V_{\sigma_{i+1}} \xrightarrow{\sim} e_{\sigma_{i+1}} \mathscr V_{\sigma_i}$.

For the direct path: $$r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}\bigl(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}}(v)\bigr) \;=\; e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(v).$$

For the detour, factor $r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i} = r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i} \circ r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma'}$ (cosheaf composition via $\sigma_i \preceq \sigma' \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$): $$r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}\bigl(\varepsilon^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v))\bigr) = r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i}\Bigl( r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma'} \bigl((r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma'})^{-1}(e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v)))\bigr) \Bigr)$$ $$= r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i}\bigl(e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v))\bigr).$$

Key step: $r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i}$ is $\hat K_{\sigma'}$-equivariant (by cartesianness and the definition of restriction maps), and since $\sigma_{i+1} \succeq \sigma'$ implies $K_{\sigma_{i+1},+} \subseteq \hat K_{\sigma'}$ (because $\hat K_{\sigma_{i+1}} \subseteq \hat K_{\sigma'}$ as $\overline{\sigma_{i+1}} \supseteq \overline{\sigma'}$), it commutes with the action of every element of $K_{\sigma_{i+1},+}$ and hence with their averaging $e_{\sigma_{i+1}}$. So $$r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i}\bigl(e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v))\bigr) = e_{\sigma_{i+1}}\bigl(r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_i}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}(v))\bigr).$$

But $r^{\sigma'}{\sigma_i}(\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}{\sigma'}(v)) = r^{\sigma'}{\sigma_i}((r^{\sigma'}{\sigma_i})^{-1}(e_{\sigma'}(v))) = e_{\sigma'}(v)$.

So the detour reduces to $e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(e_{\sigma'}(v))$. By property (i) of [[node:coefficient_systems.idempotent_system_from_T_axioms]], $e_{\sigma_{i+1}} e_{\sigma'} = e_{\sigma_{i+1}}$ (since $\sigma' \preceq \sigma_{i+1}$). Therefore $$r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}(\text{detour}) = e_{\sigma_{i+1}}(v) = r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}(\text{direct}).$$

Since $r^{\sigma_{i+1}}_{\sigma_i}$ is injective (cartesian iso onto $e_{\sigma_{i+1}} \mathscr V_{\sigma_i}$), the detour and direct maps agree. $\square$

Case D ($\sigma_i \succeq \sigma' \succeq \sigma_{i+1}$).

All three local maps are now of "inclusion" type (cosheaf restrictions): - $\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}} = r^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}}$. - $\varepsilon^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'} = r^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'}$. - $\varepsilon^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}} = r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}}$.

The detour equals $r^{\sigma'}_{\sigma_{i+1}} \circ r^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma'} = r^{\sigma_i}_{\sigma_{i+1}}$ by cosheaf composition. $\square$

Proof of (2) — any two taut paths differ by intermediate detours

In Euclidean apartment $A$, given two taut paths $P_1, P_2$ from $\sigma_0$ to $\sigma_n$ with witnessing line segments $[x_1, y_1]$, $[x_2, y_2]$ (where $x_i \in \sigma_0^\circ$, $y_i \in \sigma_n^\circ$), we can homotope $P_1$ to $P_2$ by continuously deforming the segment. Each generic continuous deformation introduces or removes one "intermediate face crossing" — exactly the Case C/D elementary moves above. Special (non-generic) deformations passing through codimension-2 or higher faces decompose into finite sequences of generic ones.

This is the standard combinatorial-topology argument on a polysimplicial Euclidean apartment; it requires only the cellular structure (F1)–(F4) and convexity of $\overline{\mathcal F}$ (a standing CW assumption).

Conclusion

By (1) [Cases C and D] and (2), any two taut paths from $\tau$ to $\sigma$ give the same composite map $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$. $\square$
