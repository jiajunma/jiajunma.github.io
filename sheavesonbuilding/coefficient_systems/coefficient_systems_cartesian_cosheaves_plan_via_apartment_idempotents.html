Proof plan for cartesian_cosheaves (unit is iso)

The expected theorem

The §7.2 proposition that unit-is-iso, combined with §7.1 resolution, upgrades to a categorical equivalence of the same form as [Lan19 Thm 4.10/4.11] and [DF Prop 3.2.4]:

Theorem (expected). Assume (T1)–(T5) and Hypothesis (S) ($\Rep_\Phi(G)$ is a Serre subcategory of $\Rep(G)$). Then $$\Loc_\phi : \Rep_\Phi(G) \;\xrightleftarrows[\sim]{\sim}\; \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi) : H_0 \circ C_\bullet$$ is a pair of quasi-inverse equivalences of $R$-linear categories.

  • Counit iso ($H_0(C_\bullet(\Loc_\phi V)) \xrightarrow{\sim} V$): the §7.1 resolution theorem Resolution theorem: C_•(Φ, V) → V (Thm 7.1.7) — already done.
  • Unit iso ($\mathscr V \xrightarrow{\sim} \Loc_\phi(H_0 \mathscr V)$): the §7.2 proposition Cartesian cosheaves Cosh^cart_G(X,R,φ) and unit isomorphism — what the rest of this plan delivers.

This is the same theorem as [Lan19 Thm 4.10/4.11] and [DF Prop 3.2.4], specialized to our (Φ, B(Σ))-setting. We prove it from scratch (per user decision Q1), borrowing only the proof structure from Lanard.

Target. Cartesian cosheaves Cosh^cart_G(X,R,φ) and unit isomorphism (the unit-iso half).

This route is inspired by [Lanard 2019] (and the structurally identical [Dat–Fintzen 2026 Prop 3.2.4]) but does not invoke their main theorem as a black box. The result will be self-contained, exploiting one simplification: we work on the apartment $A$, not the building.

Notation clarification

Throughout this plan, $x$ denotes an arbitrary point of $\BSigma$, not necessarily a vertex. Let $\mathcal F_x$ be the unique facet containing $x$ in its interior. By (T1): $$K_{x,+} = K_{\mathcal F_x, +}, \quad \phi_x = \phi_{\mathcal F_x}, \quad \text{hence} \quad e_x \;=\; e_{\mathcal F_x}.$$ So $e_x$ depends only on $\mathcal F_x$; we use $e_\mathcal F$ as the primary symbol and $e_x$ as point-level shorthand.

The "vertex" role only surfaces in the existing Contraction map c: C_d(A) → C_{d+1}(A) (Bestvina–Savin) / Bestvina–Savin contraction (which is set up from a fixed vertex). The new proof material below is written facet-level; specializing to vertices is unnecessary.

1. Reduction lemma (§7.2.1 of planned write-up)

Lemma (Reduction). Let $\mathscr V \in \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi)$. The unit map $\mathrm{unit}_\mathscr V : \mathscr V \to \Loc_\phi(H_0 \mathscr V)$ is an isomorphism in $\Coeff_G(\BSigma, \Phi)$ iff for every facet $\mathcal F \subseteq A$, $$(\mathrm{unit}_\mathscr V)_\mathcal F : \mathscr V_\mathcal F \;\longrightarrow\; e_\mathcal F \cdot H_0\bigl(C_\bullet(\BSigma, \mathscr V)\bigr)$$ is an $R$-module isomorphism. Equivalently (by (T1)), for every point $x \in A$, $(\mathrm{unit})_x : \mathscr V_x \to e_x \cdot H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$ is iso.

Reduction steps

(a) $G$-orbit reduction. By $G$-equivariance (EC3) and (T3), the unit being an iso at $\mathcal F$ is equivalent to being an iso at $g \mathcal F$ for any $g \in G$. So it suffices to check one $\mathcal F$ per $G$-orbit.

(b) Apartment reduction. By [[node:coefficient_systems.A_over_K_equivalence]], every $G$-orbit of facets in $\BSigma$ contains a representative in $A$. So we may restrict checking to facets $\mathcal F \subseteq A$.

(c) Optional face-shrinking via cartesian. If desired, the check at facet $\mathcal F$ transfers to any face $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$ (vertex or otherwise): the cartesian condition + (T2) gives an iso $r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E : \mathscr V_\mathcal F \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathscr V_\mathcal E[\phi_\mathcal F] = e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_\mathcal E$. This step is not strictly needed for the apartment-level proof — it is only useful when we want to localize chain-complex computations near a chosen base point (next step).

(d) $\BSigma_x$-localization. By [[node:coefficient_systems.support_in_BSx]], for any point $x \in \BSigma$ (and writing $\mathcal F = \mathcal F_x$), $$e_\mathcal F \cdot C_d(\BSigma, \mathscr V) \subseteq C_d(\BSigma_x, \mathscr V), \qquad \BSigma_x := K_x \cdot A = \hat K_x \cdot A$$ (see The K_x-orbit subset B(Σ)_x := K_x · A). So $$e_\mathcal F \cdot H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V) = e_\mathcal F \cdot H_0(\BSigma_x, \mathscr V|_{\BSigma_x})$$ and the right side is computed from data on $\BSigma_x$ alone. Caveat: although the support lemma works for any $x$, the existing Bestvina–Savin contraction in Contraction map c: C_d(A) → C_{d+1}(A) (Bestvina–Savin) is set up from a fixed vertex. For the present reduction step any $x$ works; vertices only enter if we later need to invoke that contraction.

Net result: the question is reduced to comparing two $R$-modules built from the apartment-local data $\{\mathscr V_\mathcal F\}_{\mathcal F \subseteq A}$ together with the $\hat K_x$-action — exactly the setting in which Lanard's techniques apply, but on the much simpler geometric object $A$ (Euclidean, finite-dimensional, polysimplicial).

2. Abstract idempotent framework (§7.2.2 of planned write-up)

We extract from (T1)–(T5) a small, purely Hecke-algebraic package about the idempotents $e_\mathcal F$ (resp. $e_x$ for vertices) so that the remainder of the proof depends only on this package.

Translation dictionary

For each facet $\mathcal F$ of $A$ define $$e_\mathcal F := \frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{\mathcal F,+})} \int_{K_{\mathcal F,+}} \phi_\mathcal F^{-1}(k)\, k\, dk \;\in\; \mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+}) \;\subseteq\; \mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F).$$

For any point $x \in \BSigma$ (not necessarily a vertex): $$e_x \;:=\; e_{\mathcal F_x}$$ where $\mathcal F_x$ is the unique facet containing $x$ in its interior. By (T1), $K_{x,+} = K_{\mathcal F_x, +}$ and $\phi_x = \phi_{\mathcal F_x}$, so this matches the direct definition $e_x = e_{\phi_x}$. When $x$ happens to be a vertex, $\mathcal F_x = \{x\}$ as a 0-cell, and $e_x$ agrees with Lanard's vertex-level idempotent.

Properties extracted from (T1)–(T5)

The full proof depends only on the following abstract package.

Lemma (idempotent system on $[A/\mathscr K]$).

(i) Restriction compatibility [from (T2)]. For $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$, $e_\mathcal E e_\mathcal F = e_\mathcal F e_\mathcal E = e_\mathcal F$. (Equivalently, $\phi_\mathcal F$-isotypic implies $\phi_\mathcal E$-isotypic after restriction; centrality of $e_\mathcal F$ inside the ambient algebra needs (T5) or normality.)

(ii) Adjacent-point commutation [from (T5)]. For two points $x, y \in A$ whose facets $\mathcal F_x, \mathcal F_y$ are adjacent (i.e. share some common facet whose closure contains both), $e_x e_y = e_y e_x$ in $\mathcal H_R$. Proof sketch. For any $z \in [x, y]$, (T5) gives $K_{z,+} = (K_{x,+} \cap K_{z,+})(K_{y,+} \cap K_{z,+})$. Take $z$ in a witnessing facet of adjacency; the factorization identity, combined with the character compatibility on $K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}$ (forced by (T2)+(T4)), shows that $e_x e_y$ and $e_y e_x$ both equal the projection associated with $\phi_x \cdot \phi_y$ restricted appropriately.

(iii) Geodesic telescoping [from (T5)]. For points $x, y, z$ with $z \in [x, y]$ (i.e. $z$ on the line segment from $x$ to $y$), $e_x e_z e_y = e_x e_y$ in $\mathcal H_R$. Proof sketch. Directly from (T5)'s factorization at $z$.

(iv) $G$-equivariance [from (T3)]. $e_{g \mathcal F} = g e_\mathcal F g^{-1}$ for $g \in G$. Point-level: $e_{gx} = g e_x g^{-1}$.

(v) Support [from definition]. $e_\mathcal F \in \mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+}) \subseteq \mathcal H_R(\hat K_\mathcal F)$; in particular, $e_x \in \mathcal H_R(\hat K_x)$ for any $x \in \BSigma$.

Status of (ii): still needs verification of one technical commutation on the overlap. The user's instinct (T5 ⇒ vertex commutation) is correct in spirit, but the precise statement may need (T4)'s intertwiner clause to pin down character agreement on $K_{x,+} \cap K_{y,+}$. Flagged for the final write-up.

After this lemma, the rest of the proof uses only (i)–(v) and never returns to $\Phi$ itself.

3. Local maps along taut paths (§7.2.3)

For $\mathcal F, \mathcal E$ facets of $A$ adjacent (sharing a face), define elementary local maps:

  • If $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$ (E smaller face of F): $$\varepsilon^\mathcal F_\mathcal E := p^\mathcal F_\mathcal E : \mathscr V_\mathcal F \twoheadrightarrow e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_\mathcal E \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathscr V_\mathcal F$$ (the cartesian iso composed with the projection).
  • If $\mathcal F \preceq \mathcal E$ (F smaller): $$\varepsilon^\mathcal F_\mathcal E := \varphi^\mathcal E_\mathcal F : \mathscr V_\mathcal F \hookrightarrow \mathscr V_\mathcal E$$ (the cartesian inclusion).

For a taut path $\sigma_0 = \mathcal F, \dots, \sigma_n = \mathcal E$ (a sequence of facets traversed by a generic line segment from a chosen interior point of $\mathcal F$ to one of $\mathcal E$, with each $\sigma_i, \sigma_{i+1}$ adjacent), set $$\varepsilon^\mathcal F_\mathcal E := \varepsilon^{\sigma_{n-1}}_{\sigma_n} \circ \cdots \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_0}_{\sigma_1}.$$

Why apartment lets us use taut paths (not admissible paths)

Lanard introduced "admissible paths" because the simplicial hull of two vertices in a general Bruhat–Tits building need not be a simplex — Wang's taut-path notion (designed for $\mathrm{GL}_n$) breaks down.

But our reduction (§1) places us inside the apartment $A$, which is: - a (closed, full-dimensional) subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean affine space, - with a polysimplicial structure (from (F1)–(F4)) that is locally finite, - where the convex hull (= cellular convex hull) of two points is a genuine convex polyhedron, in turn a finite union of facets.

Inside this Euclidean object, taut paths along straight-line segments behave well; the obstructions Lanard's admissible-path mechanism removes do not arise. We expect to be able to adapt Wang's [Wan17 §2.3] (unpublished manuscript) (unpublished manuscript) proof of well-definedness of $\varepsilon$ in just a couple of pages.

4. Well-definedness of $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ (§7.2.4)

Proposition. $\varepsilon^\tau_\sigma$ depends only on the source $\tau$ and target $\sigma$, not on the chosen taut path.

The proof uses: - (i), (ii), (iii) of §2 to reorder consecutive elementary steps in a path, - the geometry of taut paths in $A$ (Wang-style): any two taut paths between the same endpoints differ by a finite sequence of "elementary moves" of the form covered by (ii) and (iii).

This is structurally Lanard §3, but stripped of the admissible-path overhead.

5. Unit-iso from the idempotent system (§7.2.5)

Following Meyer–Solleveld [MS10 §2.5] (Meyer–Solleveld 2010, J. Reine Angew. Math. 647, arXiv:1004.4361 — PDF not in our local folder, citation not independently verified) + Wang [Wan17 §2.4] (unpublished manuscript), the local maps $\varepsilon^{\Sigma}_\sigma$ assemble into a system of idempotents $\{e_\mathcal F^\Sigma\}_\mathcal F$ acting on $H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$ via $$e_\mathcal F^\Sigma := i_\mathcal F^\Sigma \circ p_\mathcal F^\Sigma : H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V) \to \mathscr V_\mathcal F \to H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V).$$ The properties (i)–(v) of §2 imply this system is consistent in the Meyer–Solleveld sense, hence [MS10 Thm 2.12] (Meyer–Solleveld 2010 — PDF not in local folder, unverified) yields a decomposition of $H_0$ indexed by facets.

Special simplification in our setting. Because our idempotents come from a single character $\phi$ (no torsor sum over wild-inertia classes as in DF), the relevant decomposition collapses: after applying $e_\mathcal F$, only facets $\mathcal F'$ with $\phi_{\mathcal F'} \equiv \phi_\mathcal F$ in the appropriate sense contribute, and (T4) pins this down to a single class. The unit map $$\mathscr V_\mathcal F \xrightarrow{(\mathrm{unit})_\mathcal F} e_\mathcal F H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$$ becomes the identity on a single summand, with inverse the local projection $p_\mathcal F^\Sigma$. Iso for every facet, which by §1 finishes the proof.

9. What NOT to do

  • Do not invoke [Lan19, Thm 4.10/4.11] or [DF Prop 3.2.4] as a black box. Per user decision (Q1).
  • Do not introduce Heisenberg–Weil coefficient systems at this point. Per user decision (Q4). Those belong to §8.
  • Do not descend to the alcove $\mathcal A$ unless geometry forces it. The apartment $A$ is the working level. (F5)–(F6) stay out of the picture for §7.2.
  • Do not introduce e-subdivision (à la [DF §2.7.3–§2.7.4]). DF needs this to manufacture facet-constancy of their point-level $e_{\phi,I,x}$; our (T1) gives facet-constancy as an axiom, and (F1)–(F6) supply the cellular fineness DF buys via subdivision. Adding subdivision would (a) require choosing a tame extension $E/F$ we don't otherwise need, (b) force re-verifying (F1)–(F4)-analogues on the subdivided structure, (c) break our clean §7.3 chain $[\mathcal A/\mathscr K] \simeq [A/\mathscr K] \simeq [\BSigma/G]$. Stay with the original cellular structure.
