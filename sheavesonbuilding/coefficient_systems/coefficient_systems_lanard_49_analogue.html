Lanard 4.9 analogue (facet-level)

This lemma is the basic intertwining identity that drives Step 5 of the §7.2 cartesian unit-iso proof. It is the facet-level analogue of [Lanard 2019, Lemma 4.9].

Setting

Let $\mathscr V \in \Cosh^{\mathrm{cart}}_G(\BSigma, R, \phi)$ (cartesian cosheaf), $A$ the apartment, with idempotents $\{e_\mathcal F\}$ and local maps $\{\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F\}$ from [[node:coefficient_systems.idempotent_system_from_T_axioms]] and Local maps ε^τ_σ along taut paths in the apartment respectively.

For a facet $\mathcal E$ of $A$, define the canonical inclusion $$i_\mathcal E \;:\; \mathscr V_\mathcal E \;\longrightarrow\; H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V), \qquad i_\mathcal E(v) \;:=\; \bigl[x \otimes r^\mathcal E_x(v)\bigr]$$ where $x$ is any vertex of $\overline{\mathcal E}$. This is independent of the choice of $x$ by Loc_Φ: Rep_Φ(G) → Coeff_G(B(Σ), Φ) and H_0 adjunction — the same well-definedness used in the §7.4 unit construction.

Statement

Lemma (Lanard 4.9 analogue). Let $\mathcal E, \mathcal F$ be two facets of $A$ that share a common vertex $x \in \overline{\mathcal E} \cap \overline{\mathcal F}$ AND are adjacent (one is a face of the other). Then $$i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F \;=\; e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E$$ as $R$-linear maps $\mathscr V_\mathcal E \to H_0(\BSigma, \mathscr V)$.

(Both sides act on $\mathscr V_\mathcal E$; $e_\mathcal F$ acts on $H_0$ via $\mathcal H_R(K_{\mathcal F,+})$, well-defined because $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal F \subseteq \hat K_x$ and $\mathscr V_x$ is a $\hat K_x$-module.)

Proof

Take any $v \in \mathscr V_\mathcal E$. Using the common vertex $x$ for both $i_\mathcal E$ and $i_\mathcal F$: $$i_\mathcal E(v) = \bigl[x \otimes r^\mathcal E_x(v)\bigr], \qquad i_\mathcal F(\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v)) = \bigl[x \otimes r^\mathcal F_x(\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v))\bigr].$$

For the RHS of the lemma: since $e_\mathcal F$ acts at the $\mathscr V_x$-stalk level, $$e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E(v) = e_\mathcal F \bigl[x \otimes r^\mathcal E_x(v)\bigr] = \bigl[x \otimes e_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal E_x(v))\bigr].$$

So it suffices to show, at the $\mathscr V_x$-stalk level, $$r^\mathcal F_x\bigl(\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v)\bigr) \;=\; e_\mathcal F\bigl(r^\mathcal E_x(v)\bigr) \quad \text{in } \mathscr V_x. \qquad (\ast)$$

Two sub-cases by face-order.

Sub-case (a): $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$ ($\mathcal E$ smaller, $\mathcal F$ bigger)

Then $\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F$ is the "projection-then-lift" map: $$\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v) = (r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E)^{-1}(e_\mathcal F(v))$$ where $(r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E)^{-1}$ is the inverse of the cartesian iso $r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E : \mathscr V_\mathcal F \xrightarrow{\sim} e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_\mathcal E$.

We have $x \in \overline{\mathcal E}$, so $x \preceq \mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$, and the cosheaf composition gives $$r^\mathcal F_x = r^\mathcal E_x \circ r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E.$$

Hence $$r^\mathcal F_x\bigl(\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v)\bigr) = r^\mathcal E_x \circ r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E\bigl((r^\mathcal F_\mathcal E)^{-1}(e_\mathcal F(v))\bigr) = r^\mathcal E_x\bigl(e_\mathcal F(v)\bigr).$$

To get $(\ast)$ we need $r^\mathcal E_x(e_\mathcal F(v)) = e_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal E_x(v))$, i.e.\ $r^\mathcal E_x$ commutes with $e_\mathcal F$.

Commutation check. $e_\mathcal F$ is an integral over $K_{\mathcal F,+}$. The restriction $r^\mathcal E_x$ is equivariant for the action of any group fixing both $\mathcal E$ and $x$ pointwise — i.e., for $\Fix_G(\mathcal E) \cap \Fix_G(x) = \hat K_\mathcal E$ (since $x \in \overline{\mathcal E}$ implies $\hat K_\mathcal E \subseteq \hat K_x$). Since $\mathcal E \preceq \mathcal F$, $\hat K_\mathcal F \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal E$ (fixing the bigger $\overline{\mathcal F}$ implies fixing the smaller $\overline{\mathcal E}$), so $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal F \subseteq \hat K_\mathcal E$. Thus $r^\mathcal E_x$ commutes with each element of $K_{\mathcal F,+}$, hence with their averaging $e_\mathcal F$. $\square$ for sub-case (a).

Sub-case (b): $\mathcal F \preceq \mathcal E$ ($\mathcal F$ smaller, $\mathcal E$ bigger)

Then $\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F$ is the "inclusion" map: $$\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v) = r^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v).$$

We have $x \preceq \mathcal F \preceq \mathcal E$, and cosheaf composition gives $$r^\mathcal F_x \circ r^\mathcal E_\mathcal F = r^\mathcal E_x.$$

Hence $$r^\mathcal F_x\bigl(\varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v)\bigr) = r^\mathcal F_x(r^\mathcal E_\mathcal F(v)) = r^\mathcal E_x(v).$$

To get $(\ast)$ we need $r^\mathcal E_x(v) = e_\mathcal F(r^\mathcal E_x(v))$, i.e.\ $r^\mathcal E_x(v) \in e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_x$.

Containment check. By cartesianness, $r^\mathcal E_x(\mathscr V_\mathcal E) = e_\mathcal E \mathscr V_x = V[\phi_\mathcal E]$. Since $\mathcal F \preceq \mathcal E$, (T2) gives $K_{\mathcal F,+} \subseteq K_{\mathcal E,+}$ and $\phi_\mathcal F = \phi_\mathcal E|_{K_{\mathcal F,+}}$. A vector $\phi_\mathcal E$-isotypic under $K_{\mathcal E,+}$ is therefore $\phi_\mathcal F$-isotypic under the smaller $K_{\mathcal F,+}$, so $V[\phi_\mathcal E] \subseteq V[\phi_\mathcal F] = e_\mathcal F \mathscr V_x$. Hence $r^\mathcal E_x(v) \in V[\phi_\mathcal F]$, on which $e_\mathcal F$ is the identity. $\square$ for sub-case (b).

Combining (a) and (b), $(\ast)$ holds; the lemma follows. $\square$

Iteration along a taut path

Corollary. Let $\mathcal E, \mathcal F$ be any facets of $A$, and let $\mathcal E = \sigma_0, \sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_n = \mathcal F$ be a taut path in $A$ from $\mathcal E$ to $\mathcal F$ (with consecutive cells adjacent and sharing a common vertex with the next). Then $$i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F \;=\; e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E.$$

Proof. Iterating the lemma at each step: $$i_{\sigma_{j+1}} \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_j}_{\sigma_{j+1}} = e_{\sigma_{j+1}} \circ i_{\sigma_j} \quad (j = 0, \dots, n-1).$$

Hence $$\begin{aligned} i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F &= i_{\sigma_n} \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_{n-1}}_{\sigma_n} \circ \cdots \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_0}_{\sigma_1} \\ &= e_{\sigma_n} \circ i_{\sigma_{n-1}} \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_{n-2}}_{\sigma_{n-1}} \circ \cdots \circ \varepsilon^{\sigma_0}_{\sigma_1} \\ &= e_{\sigma_n} \circ e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \circ i_{\sigma_{n-2}} \circ \cdots \\ &\;\;\vdots \\ &= e_{\sigma_n} \circ e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \circ \cdots \circ e_{\sigma_1} \circ i_\mathcal E. \end{aligned}$$

So $i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F = e_\mathcal F e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \cdots e_{\sigma_1} \circ i_\mathcal E$.

Collapse via property (iii). Apply both sides to $v \in \mathscr V_\mathcal E$. The image $i_\mathcal E(v) = [x_\mathcal E \otimes r^\mathcal E_{x_\mathcal E}(v)]$ lies in $e_\mathcal E H_0$ because $r^\mathcal E_{x_\mathcal E}(v) \in r^\mathcal E_{x_\mathcal E}(\mathscr V_\mathcal E) = e_\mathcal E \mathscr V_{x_\mathcal E}$. Hence $e_\mathcal E$ acts as identity on $i_\mathcal E(v)$, and we may insert $e_\mathcal E$ at the end of the chain of $e$'s without changing the value: $$e_\mathcal F e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \cdots e_{\sigma_1} i_\mathcal E(v) = e_\mathcal F e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \cdots e_{\sigma_1} e_\mathcal E i_\mathcal E(v).$$

Pick interior points $z_j \in \sigma_j^\circ$ for $0 \leq j \leq n$; because the taut path is realized by a straight-line segment, the points $z_0, z_1, \dots, z_n$ are collinear in $A$, with $z_j \in [z_0, z_n]$ for every $1 \leq j \leq n-1$. By (T1), $e_{\sigma_j} = e_{z_j}$. So we must show $$e_{z_n} e_{z_{n-1}} \cdots e_{z_1} e_{z_0} \;=\; e_{z_n} e_{z_0}.$$

Induction on $n$ using unconditional (iii). Property (iii) of [[node:coefficient_systems.idempotent_system_from_T_axioms|idempotent_system_from_T_axioms]] holds unconditionally for any $x, y, z \in A$ with $z \in [x, y]$ (no facet relation required).

  • Base $n = 2$: (iii) at $(z_2, z_1, z_0)$ gives $e_{z_2} e_{z_1} e_{z_0} = e_{z_2} e_{z_0}$.
  • Inductive step: The sub-path $z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n$ is itself a collinear chain of length $n-1$, so by IH $e_{z_n} e_{z_{n-1}} \cdots e_{z_1} = e_{z_n} e_{z_1}$. Then $e_{z_n} e_{z_{n-1}} \cdots e_{z_1} e_{z_0} = (e_{z_n} e_{z_1}) e_{z_0} = e_{z_n} e_{z_0}$ by (iii) at $(z_n, z_1, z_0)$ — valid because $z_1 \in [z_n, z_0]$.

Thus $e_\mathcal F e_{\sigma_{n-1}} \cdots e_{\sigma_1} e_\mathcal E = e_\mathcal F e_\mathcal E$.

Combined with $e_\mathcal E i_\mathcal E(v) = i_\mathcal E(v)$: $$i_\mathcal F \circ \varepsilon^\mathcal E_\mathcal F (v) = e_\mathcal F \circ i_\mathcal E(v). \qquad \square$$

Use in the unit-iso proof

This iterated identity is the key tool used in Unit-iso assembly: V_F ≅ e_F H_0 via surjectivity + injectivity to show the inverse identity $\text{unit}_\mathcal F \circ p_\mathcal F = \id$ on $e_\mathcal F H_0$.

The key consequence: for every $y \in A_0$, $e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F)$, because choosing a taut path from $y$ to $\mathcal F$ and applying the iteration above to $i_y$ (which is just $j_y$ when $y$ is a vertex viewed as a 0-facet) gives: $$e_\mathcal F j_y(\mathscr V_y) = e_\mathcal F i_y(\mathscr V_y) = i_\mathcal F\bigl(\varepsilon^y_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_y)\bigr) \subseteq i_\mathcal F(\mathscr V_\mathcal F).$$ This is exactly Lanard's proof of [Lan19 Thm 4.10] adapted to our facet-level setting; it gives the surjectivity half of unit-iso.

The injectivity half of unit-iso comes via a different argument (Meyer–Solleveld decomposition + counit information); see the assembly node.
