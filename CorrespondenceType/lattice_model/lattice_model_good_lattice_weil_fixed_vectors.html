Pro-Unipotent Fixed Vectors of the Weil Representation at a Good Lattice

This node localizes the Weil representation of \(\operatorname{Sp}(W)\) at a good lattice \(L\): it identifies the space of vectors fixed by the pro-unipotent radical \(G_{L,0^+}\) with the finite Weil representation \(S(\mathsf b_x)\) of the level-\(0\) symplectic factor of the parahoric reductive quotient, computed inside the generalized lattice model. This is the building-localization statement: the assignment \(L\mapsto\omega_\psi^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\) attaches to each good lattice (equivalently, to each vertex of \(\mathcal B(\operatorname{Sp}(W))\)) a finite Weil representation of a finite reductive group.

Setup

Let \(F\) be a non-archimedean local field with ring of integers \(\mathfrak o\), maximal ideal \(\mathfrak p\), residue field \(k=\mathfrak o/\mathfrak p\) of odd cardinality \(q\), and uniformizer \(\varpi\). Fix an additive character \(\psi\) of \(F\) of conductor \(\mathfrak p\) (trivial on \(\mathfrak p\), nontrivial on \(\mathfrak o\)); since \(q\) is odd, \(\tfrac12\in\mathfrak o^\times\). Let \((W,\langle\,,\rangle)\) be a symplectic \(F\)-space of dimension \(2n\), \(G=\operatorname{Sp}(W)\), and \(\omega_\psi\) the Weil representation of \(G\) attached to \(\psi\). For an \(\mathfrak o\)-lattice \(L\subseteq W\) write

$$ L^{\#}=\{w\in W:\langle w,L\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak p\}. $$

A good lattice is an \(\mathfrak o\)-lattice \(L\subseteq W\) with

$$ L\ \supseteq\ L^{\#}\ \supseteq\ \mathfrak p L \qquad(\text{equivalently } L^{\#}\subseteq L\subseteq\varpi^{-1}L^{\#}). $$

A self-dual lattice (\(L=L^{\#}\)) is good; good lattices are strictly more general than self-dual ones. Good lattices are exactly the level-\(0\) lattices \(L=\mathscr L_{x,0}\) of the vertices \(x\) of the Bruhat–Tits building \(\mathcal B(\operatorname{Sp}(W))\) whose stabilizer is a maximal parahoric; the associated self-dual lattice function is constant equal to \(L\) on a neighborhood of level \(0\) and jumps only at the self-paired levels \(0\) and \(\tfrac12\pmod 1\) (see Self-Dual Lattice Function, Apartment Model for Sp(W) via Self-Dual Lattice Functions).

The symplectic form induces nondegenerate symplectic \(k\)-forms on the two graded pieces

$$ \mathsf b_x:=L/L^{\#}\ (\dim_k\mathsf b_x=2a),\qquad \mathsf b_x':=L^{\#}/\mathfrak p L\ (\dim_k\mathsf b_x'=2c),\qquad a+c=n. $$

For \(w\in L\) write \(\bar w\in\mathsf b_x\) for its image in \(L/L^{\#}\). The parahoric is the lattice stabilizer \(G_{L,0}=\operatorname{Sp}(L)=\{g\in G:gL=L\}\) (such a \(g\) automatically fixes \(L^{\#}\) and \(\mathfrak p L\)), and its pro-unipotent radical is

$$ G_{L,0^+}=\{g\in\operatorname{Sp}(L):(g-1)L\subseteq L^{\#}\ \text{ and }\ (g-1)L^{\#}\subseteq\mathfrak p L\}. $$

Then \(G_{L,0^+}\) is normal in \(G_{L,0}\), and reduction induces an isomorphism of finite reductive groups over \(k\)

$$ \mathsf G_x:=G_{L,0}/G_{L,0^+}\ \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\ \operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\times\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x') =\operatorname{Sp}_{2a}(k)\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2c}(k),\qquad g\mapsto(\bar g,\bar g'), $$

where \(\bar g\) (resp. \(\bar g'\)) is the action of \(g\) on \(\mathsf b_x=L/L^{\#}\) (resp. \(\mathsf b_x'=L^{\#}/\mathfrak p L\)) (Parahoric Reductive Quotient for Sp(W)). The level-\(0\) factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) is the one that enters the generalized lattice model.

Generalized lattice model (Generalized Lattice Model, Parahoric Action on the Generalized Lattice Model). Let \(\Omega_x=S(\mathscr L_{x,0})\) be the space of locally constant, compactly supported maps \(f:W\to S(\mathsf b_x)\) satisfying the covariance rule

$$ f(a+w)=\psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle w,a\rangle\big)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar a)\,f(w), \qquad a\in L,\ w\in W, $$

where \(\bar a\in\mathsf b_x=L/L^{\#}\) is the reduction of \(a\in L\) (so for \(a\in L^{\#}\), \(\bar a=0\) and \(f(a+w)=\psi(\tfrac12\langle w,a\rangle)f(w)\)), and \(\overline\omega_x\) is the finite oscillator representation of the finite Heisenberg group \(H(\mathsf b_x)\) and of \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) (Symplectic Weil Representation over a Finite Field). The relevant Heisenberg cocycle is

$$ \overline\omega_x(\bar u)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar v) =\psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle\bar u,\bar v\rangle\big)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar u+\bar v), \qquad \bar u,\bar v\in\mathsf b_x, $$

with \(\psi\) read on \(k\) via \(\bar t\mapsto\psi(t)\) (well defined as \(\psi|_{\mathfrak p}=1\)). The space \(\Omega_x\) realizes \(\omega_\psi\) over \(G_{L,0}\) through the lattice-model splitting, the action being

$$ (\omega_x(g)f)(w)=\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\,f(g^{-1}w),\qquad g\in G_{L,0}, $$

where \(\bar g\in\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) is the first-factor reduction; the second factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x')\) enters only through the geometric translation \(f\mapsto f(g^{-1}\cdot)\) on the \(W\)-support, not through the \(S(\mathsf b_x)\)-values. On \(G_{L,0^+}\) the first-factor reduction is trivial (\(\bar g=1\)), so the action is the bare geometric translation \((\omega_x(g)f)(w)=f(g^{-1}w)\).

Statement

Let \(L\) be a good lattice with vertex \(x\), and let \(\Omega_x\) be its generalized lattice model with the action \(\omega_x\) above. Define

$$ \iota:S(\mathsf b_x)\longrightarrow\Omega_x,\qquad (\iota\phi)(w)=f_\phi(w)= \begin{cases}\overline\omega_x(\bar w)\,\phi,& w\in L,\\ 0,& w\notin L,\end{cases} $$

where \(\bar w\in\mathsf b_x=L/L^{\#}\) is the reduction of \(w\in L\). Then:

  1. \(\iota\) is a well-defined linear injection whose image lies in the fixed space \(\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\).

  2. \(\iota\) intertwines the finite Weil representation \(\overline\omega_x\) of \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) on \(S(\mathsf b_x)\) with the residual \(\mathsf G_x\)-action induced by \(\omega_x\): for all \(g\in G_{L,0}\), \(\ \omega_x(g)\,\iota\phi=\iota\big(\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\phi\big)\), where \(\bar g\in\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) is the first-factor reduction. In particular the second reductive-quotient factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x')=\operatorname{Sp}(L^{\#}/\mathfrak p L)\) acts trivially on \(\operatorname{im}\iota\).

  3. \(\iota\) is surjective onto \(\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\); equivalently every \(G_{L,0^+}\)-fixed vector is supported on \(L\). Consequently, using \(\Omega_x\cong\omega_\psi|_{G_{L,0}}\),

$$ \boxed{\;\omega_\psi^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\;=\;\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\;\cong\;S(\mathsf b_x) \quad\text{as }\mathsf G_x\text{-representations},\;} $$

finite-dimensional of dimension \(\dim_{\mathbb C}S(\mathsf b_x)=q^{a}\), where \(2a=\dim_k(L/L^{\#})\); the first factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) acts by the finite Weil representation \(\overline\omega_x\) and the second factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x')\) acts trivially.

Proof

Throughout, \(\bar{(\cdot)}\) denotes reduction modulo \(L^{\#}\) (for vectors of \(L\), giving elements of \(\mathsf b_x=L/L^{\#}\)) or the first-factor reduction modulo \(G_{L,0^+}\) (for elements of \(G_{L,0}\), giving elements of \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\)).

(A) \(\iota\) is well defined and lands in \(\Omega_x\)

Fix \(\phi\in S(\mathsf b_x)\). The function \(f_\phi\) is supported on the compact open set \(L\), and on \(L\) it depends only on \(\bar w\in\mathsf b_x\) (a finite set), hence is locally constant and compactly supported. It remains to verify the covariance rule for all \(a\in L\), \(w\in W\). If \(w\notin L\) then \(a+w\notin L\) (as \(a\in L\)), so both sides vanish. If \(w\in L\) then \(a+w\in L\) and, by the Heisenberg cocycle,

$$ \psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle w,a\rangle\big)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar a)\,f_\phi(w) =\psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle w,a\rangle\big)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar a)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar w)\,\phi =\psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle w,a\rangle\big)\,\psi\!\big(\tfrac12\langle\bar a,\bar w\rangle\big)\, \overline\omega_x(\bar a+\bar w)\,\phi . $$

Now \(\langle\bar a,\bar w\rangle\) is \(\langle a,w\rangle\bmod\mathfrak p\) and \(\langle a,w\rangle=-\langle w,a\rangle\), so the two phases cancel and the right-hand side equals \(\overline\omega_x(\overline{a+w})\phi=f_\phi(a+w)\). Hence \(f_\phi\in\Omega_x\). Linearity is clear, and injectivity follows from \(f_\phi(0)=\phi\).

(B) \(\iota\) lands in the fixed space; the intertwining; the second factor is trivial

Let \(h\in G_{L,0^+}\). Then \((h-1)L\subseteq L^{\#}\), so \(h\) preserves \(L\) and acts trivially on \(L/L^{\#}\); in particular \(\overline{h^{-1}w}=\bar w\) for \(w\in L\). Since the \(G_{L,0^+}\)-action is bare translation,

$$ (\omega_x(h)f_\phi)(w)=f_\phi(h^{-1}w)=\overline\omega_x(\overline{h^{-1}w})\phi=\overline\omega_x(\bar w)\phi=f_\phi(w) $$

for \(w\in L\), and both sides vanish for \(w\notin L\). Hence \(\operatorname{im}\iota\subseteq\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\).

Now let \(g\in G_{L,0}\). For \(w\notin L\) both sides of the asserted identity vanish at \(w\). For \(w\in L\), the action formula and \(\overline{g^{-1}w}=\bar g^{-1}\bar w\) give

$$ (\omega_x(g)f_\phi)(w)=\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar g^{-1}\bar w)\,\phi . $$

The conjugation identity \(\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar v)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar g)^{-1} =\overline\omega_x(\bar g\bar v)\) (Parahoric Action on the Generalized Lattice Model, Symplectic Weil Representation over a Finite Field) gives

$$ \overline\omega_x(\bar g)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar g^{-1}\bar w) =\overline\omega_x(\bar w)\,\overline\omega_x(\bar g), $$

so \((\omega_x(g)f_\phi)(w)=\overline\omega_x(\bar w)\big(\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\phi\big) =f_{\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\phi}(w)\). Thus \(\omega_x(g)\iota\phi=\iota(\overline\omega_x(\bar g)\phi)\) for all \(g\in G_{L,0}\). Because only the first-factor reduction \(\bar g\) appears, any \(g\) whose image in \(\mathsf G_x\) has first component \(1\) acts trivially on \(\operatorname{im}\iota\); hence the second factor \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x')\) acts trivially. This proves (1) and (2). (Since \(G_{L,0^+}\) is normal in \(G_{L,0}\), the space \(\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\) is \(G_{L,0}\)-stable and carries the displayed \(\mathsf G_x\)-action.)

(C) Surjectivity: every \(G_{L,0^+}\)-fixed vector is supported on \(L\)

The argument rests on two detection lemmas for points outside the good lattice.

(C1) Lattice duality detects outside points. If \(x\in W\setminus L\), then there is \(v\in L^{\#}\) with \(\langle x,v\rangle\notin\mathfrak p\). Indeed the symplectic form identifies \(W\) with its \(F\)-linear dual, and for a full \(\mathfrak o\)-lattice the \(\mathfrak p\)-dual operation satisfies the double-dual identity \((L^{\#})^{\#}=L\) (choose an \(\mathfrak o\)-basis of \(L\); this is the usual double-dual identity for a perfect pairing, with the same \(\mathfrak p\)-normalization on both duals). Hence \(\langle x,L^{\#}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak p\) is equivalent to \(x\in(L^{\#})^{\#}=L\); so \(x\notin L\) forces some \(v\in L^{\#}\) with \(\langle x,v\rangle\notin\mathfrak p\).

(C2) A pro-unipotent transvection shifts \(x\) by a phase-detecting vector. For every \(x\in W\setminus L\) there exist \(h\in G_{L,0^+}\) and \(a\in L^{\#}\) with \(h^{-1}x=x+a\) and \(\psi(\tfrac12\langle x,a\rangle)\ne 1\). By (C1) choose \(v\in L^{\#}\) with \(\alpha:=\langle x,v\rangle\notin\mathfrak p\); let \(r=v_F(\alpha)\le 0\). Since the character induced by \(\psi\) on \(k\) is nontrivial and \(q\) is odd, choose \(u\in\mathfrak o^\times\) with \(\psi(-\tfrac12 u(\varpi^{-r}\alpha)^2)\ne 1\), and put \(c=\varpi^{-2r}u\in\mathfrak o\). Define the symplectic transvection \(h(y)=y+c\langle y,v\rangle v\) (symplectic because \(\langle v,v\rangle=0\) and the linear cross terms cancel). If \(y\in L\) then \(\langle y,v\rangle\in\mathfrak p\) (as \(v\in L^{\#}\)), so \((h-1)y=c\langle y,v\rangle v\in\mathfrak p L^{\#}\subseteq L^{\#}\); if \(y\in L^{\#}\) then \(y\in L\) (goodness of \(L\)) and likewise \((h-1)y\in\mathfrak p L^{\#}\subseteq\mathfrak p L\). Thus \(h\in G_{L,0^+}\). With \(h^{-1}(y)=y-c\langle y,v\rangle v\) and \(a:=-c\alpha v\), the coefficient \(c\alpha\) has valuation \(-r\ge 0\), so \(a\in L^{\#}\), \(h^{-1}x=x+a\), and

$$ \tfrac12\langle x,a\rangle=-\tfrac12 c\alpha^2=-\tfrac12 u(\varpi^{-r}\alpha)^2, $$

so by the choice of \(u\), \(\psi(\tfrac12\langle x,a\rangle)\ne 1\).

Conclusion of (C). Let \(f\in\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\) and \(x\in W\setminus L\). Choose \(h\in G_{L,0^+}\) and \(a\in L^{\#}\) as in (C2). Fixedness gives \(f(x)=f(h^{-1}x)=f(x+a)\); and since \(a\in L^{\#}\subseteq L\) has \(\bar a=0\), the covariance rule gives \(f(x+a)=\psi(\tfrac12\langle x,a\rangle)f(x)\). As the scalar is \(\ne 1\), \(f(x)=0\). Hence \(\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq L\). On \(L\), covariance with base point \(0\) gives \(f(w)=\overline\omega_x(\bar w)f(0)\), i.e. \(f=\iota\phi\) with \(\phi:=f(0)\in S(\mathsf b_x)\). So \(\iota\) is onto \(\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\).

Conclusion

\(\iota:S(\mathsf b_x)\to\Omega_x^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\) is an isomorphism, and by (2) the induced \(\mathsf G_x\)-action is \(\overline\omega_x\) on the first factor and trivial on the second. Through the lattice-model splitting \(\Omega_x\cong\omega_\psi|_{G_{L,0}}\), so \(\omega_\psi^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\cong S(\mathsf b_x)\), of dimension \(\dim_{\mathbb C}S(\mathsf b_x)=q^{a}\) with \(2a=\dim_k(L/L^{\#})\). \(\square\)

Remarks

  • Two extreme good lattices. When \(L^{\#}=\mathfrak p L\) (the \(\alpha=0\) vertex) one has \(\mathsf b_x=L/\mathfrak p L\cong k^{2n}\), \(\mathsf b_x'=0\), and \(\mathsf G_x=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(k)\), so \(\omega_\psi^{\,G_{L,0^+}}\cong S(\mathsf b_x)\) has dimension \(q^{n}\). The other extreme is the self-dual case \(L=L^{\#}\) (the hyperspecial \(\alpha=\tfrac12\) vertex), where \(\mathsf b_x=0\), the fixed space is one-dimensional (\(q^{0}=1\)), and the surviving symplectic factor is \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x')\). The general good lattice interpolates between these.

  • Building localization. Letting \(L\) (equivalently the vertex \(x\)) vary, the assignment \(x\mapsto\omega_\psi^{\,G_{x,0^+}}\cong S(\mathsf b_x)\) is the stalk of a \(G\)-equivariant coefficient system on \(\mathcal B(\operatorname{Sp}(W))\): each stalk is a finite Weil representation of the level-\(0\) factor of the finite reductive quotient \(\mathsf G_x\). The transition maps for an incidence \(y\le x\) are governed by the symplectic subquotient \(\mathsf b_x=U^{\perp}/U\subseteq\mathsf b_y\) of Level-0 Lattice Under Specialization to a Face, i.e. by parabolic restriction of the finite Weil representations.

  • Non-good parahorics. For a non-maximal parahoric (interior jumps present), the reductive quotient acquires \(\operatorname{GL}\) factors, \(\mathsf G_x=\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{gr}_0)\times\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{gr}_{1/2})\times\prod_{0<s<1/2}\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{gr}_s)\) (Parahoric Reductive Quotient for Sp(W)); the \(\operatorname{GL}\) factors act on functions on the Lagrangian \(\operatorname{gr}_s\). The good-lattice (vertex) case isolates the level-\(0\) symplectic factor and is the content above.

  • Oddness of \(q\). Used twice: \(\tfrac12\in\mathfrak o^\times\) (so the phase computation and the model make sense, and the transvection of (C2) can hit a nontrivial character), and \(\overline\omega_x\) is a genuine representation of \(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathsf b_x)\) over \(k\), so no metaplectic cover intervenes (Parahoric Action on the Generalized Lattice Model).

  • Provenance. This corrected general-good-lattice statement and proof were re-derived and machine-checked with the Rethlas generator/verifier loop; the two detection lemmas (C1)–(C2) were produced autonomously by the generator. An earlier draft mistakenly defined a good lattice as \(\psi\)-self-dual (\(L^{\#}=\mathfrak p L\)), which is only the single extreme \(\alpha=0\) vertex; the correct notion is \(L\supseteq L^{\#}\supseteq\mathfrak p L\), corresponding to all vertices.
