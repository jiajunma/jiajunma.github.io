Base-Lagrangian Trivialization of the Finite Weil-Index Cocycle

Use the finite Weil-index cocycle \(\gamma(L,L',L'')\) of Finite Weil-Index Cocycle for Lagrangian Transitions.

This node proves only the basepoint trivialization of the Lagrangian transition cocycle. Gérardin's stronger finite-field linearization theorem is recorded separately in Gérardin Linearization of the Finite Symplectic Weil Cocycle.

Statement

Choose a base-cochain datum \(C\), namely a base Lagrangian \(L_0\subset E\) together with the derived two-variable scalar

$$ c_{L,L'}:=\gamma(L,L',L_0)\in R^\times. $$

Then

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'') = c_{L,L'}c_{L',L''}c_{L,L''}^{-1}. $$

Also

$$ c_{L,L}=1. $$

Consequently the normalized transition maps

$$ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}:=c_{L,L'}^{-1}\mathcal F_{L,L'} $$

compose strictly:

$$ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}\circ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L',L''} = \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L''}. $$

Thus the finite Weil-index cocycle is a coboundary once a base Lagrangian is chosen.

Proof

The point is that the cocycle identity becomes a coboundary identity after one freezes the last variable to \(L_0\). We spell this out because the order of the indices matters with the convention \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}:\mathcal S_{L'}\to\mathcal S_L\).

The formal transition-map algebra in this proof is Lean-checked in lean/FiniteWeilCocycle.lean using the Lean 4.30/mathlib project /Users/hoxide/mycodes/lean-shared. The verification command is recorded in the YAML front matter.

The Lean file first models an abstract system of transition maps: a line \(\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\), a composition law, scalar multiplication, and chosen nonzero transitions \(F_{A,B}\) with

$$ F_{A,B}F_{B,C} = \gamma(A,B,C)F_{A,C}. $$

It then proves from associativity of composition that \(\gamma\) satisfies the cocycle identity. This is the formal version of the usual Schur-line argument: both ways of composing \(F_{L,L'}\), \(F_{L',L''}\), and \(F_{L'',L'''}\) give scalar multiples of the same nonzero \(F_{L,L'''}\), so the scalars are equal.

The chosen base \(L_0\) is also represented in Lean as data, not as a loose argument passed separately to each theorem. The structure BaseCochain gamma contains the base object, and its value on a pair is \(c_{L,L'}=\gamma(L,L',L_0)\).

Concretely, associativity gives

$$ F_{L,L'}(F_{L',L''}F_{L'',L'''}) = (F_{L,L'}F_{L',L''})F_{L'',L'''}. $$

Expanding the left-hand side gives

$$ \gamma(L',L'',L''')\gamma(L,L',L''')F_{L,L'''}, $$

and expanding the right-hand side gives

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')\gamma(L,L'',L''')F_{L,L'''}. $$

Cancelling the same nonzero transition line gives

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')\gamma(L,L'',L_0) = \gamma(L',L'',L_0)\gamma(L,L',L_0) $$

after setting \(L'''=L_0\). This is the specialization needed for the basepoint computation.

By definition of \(c\), the three terms with final argument \(L_0\) are

$$ \gamma(L,L'',L_0)=c_{L,L''},\qquad \gamma(L',L'',L_0)=c_{L',L''},\qquad \gamma(L,L',L_0)=c_{L,L'}. $$

Therefore the cocycle identity becomes

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')c_{L,L''} = c_{L',L''}c_{L,L'}, $$

and since \(c_{L,L''}\in R^\times\), this is equivalent to

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'') = c_{L,L'}c_{L',L''}c_{L,L''}^{-1}. $$

Next prove \(c_{L,L}=1\). The defining relation for \(\gamma(L,L,L_0)\) is

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L}\circ \mathcal F_{L,L_0} = \gamma(L,L,L_0)\mathcal F_{L,L_0}. $$

The formula for \(\mathcal F_{L,L}\) is the identity map, since the sum is over \(L/L\). Since \(\mathcal F_{L,L_0}\neq0\), this gives \(\gamma(L,L,L_0)=1\), hence \(c_{L,L}=1\).

Now compute the normalized composition. Starting from

$$ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{A,B}=c_{A,B}^{-1}\mathcal F_{A,B}, $$

we have

$$ \begin{aligned} \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}\circ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L',L''} &= c_{L',L''}^{-1}c_{L,L'}^{-1} (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ \mathcal F_{L',L''})\\ &= c_{L',L''}^{-1}c_{L,L'}^{-1} \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F_{L,L''}\\ &= c_{L,L''}^{-1}\mathcal F_{L,L''}\\ &= \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L''}. \end{aligned} $$

The third equality is exactly the coboundary formula just proved:

$$ c_{L',L''}^{-1}c_{L,L'}^{-1}\gamma(L,L',L'') = c_{L,L''}^{-1}. $$

This proves strict compatibility. \(\square\)

Explicit Formula for the Normalizing Scalar

Let \(\mathbf 1_{L_0}\in\mathcal S_{L_0}\) be the characteristic function of \(L_0\). Then

$$ c_{L,L'} = (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(0). $$

Indeed, the defining relation for \(\gamma(L,L',L_0)=c_{L,L'}\) is

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L_0} = c_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L,L_0}. $$

Evaluating both sides on \(\mathbf 1_{L_0}\) at \(0\), and using

$$ (\mathcal F_{L,L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(0)=1, $$

gives the formula.

The last displayed value is the same normalization used in Finite Weil-Index Cocycle for Lagrangian Transitions: expanding the definition of \(\mathcal F_{L,L_0}\) at \(0\) gives

$$ (\mathcal F_{L,L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(0) = \sum_{b\in L/(L\cap L_0)}\mathbf 1_{L_0}(b), $$

and only the zero coset contributes.

Expanding the two transition sums gives a denominator-free expression:

$$ c_{L,L'} = \sum_{\substack{ a\in L'/(L'\cap L_0)\\ b\in L/(L\cap L')\\ a+b\in L_0 }} \xi(i(a,b)). $$

Here is the expansion explicitly. First apply \(\mathcal F_{L',L_0}\) to \(\mathbf 1_{L_0}\):

$$ (\mathcal F_{L',L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(v) = \sum_{a\in L'/(L'\cap L_0)} \xi(i(a,v))\mathbf 1_{L_0}(v+a). $$

Then apply \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\) and evaluate at \(0\):

$$ \begin{aligned} (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(0) &= \sum_{b\in L/(L\cap L')} (\mathcal F_{L',L_0}\mathbf 1_{L_0})(b)\\ &= \sum_{\substack{ b\in L/(L\cap L')\\ a\in L'/(L'\cap L_0) }} \xi(i(a,b))\mathbf 1_{L_0}(a+b). \end{aligned} $$

The factor \(\mathbf 1_{L_0}(a+b)\) is \(1\) exactly when \(a+b\in L_0\) and otherwise is \(0\), giving the displayed sum.

This finite sum lies in the subring of \(R\) generated by \(\operatorname{im}\xi\), and it is a unit because it equals \(\gamma(L,L',L_0)\).
