Finite Weil-Index Cocycle for Lagrangian Transitions

Use the Lagrangian transition intertwiners \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}:\mathcal S_{L'}\to\mathcal S_L\) of Change-of-Lagrangian Intertwiner for Finite Schrödinger Models. By Orthogonality of Change-of-Lagrangian Intertwiners, each \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\) is an isomorphism.

Definition

For three Lagrangians \(L,L',L''\subset E\), define

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')\in R^\times $$

by

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ \mathcal F_{L',L''} = \gamma(L,L',L'')\,\mathcal F_{L,L''}. $$

This scalar is the finite Weil-index cocycle attached to the raw change-of-Lagrangian intertwiners \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\). It is not the primary definition of the finite quadratic Weil index; that normalized Gauss-sum definition is recorded in Finite Quadratic Weil Index. The name here means that this transition cocycle is the scalar obstruction which, after choosing explicit Fourier-Gaussian normalizations, is computed by finite Weil-index constants.

Why the Scalar Exists

Both sides are \(H(E,j)\)-homomorphisms

$$ \mathcal S_{L''}\longrightarrow \mathcal S_L. $$

By One-Dimensional Hom Line for Finite Schrödinger Models,

$$ \operatorname{Hom}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal S_{L''},\mathcal S_L) =R\cdot \mathcal F_{L,L''}. $$

Thus there is a unique scalar \(\gamma(L,L',L'')\in R\) with

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''} = \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F_{L,L''}. $$

The scalar is nonzero because both \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''}\) and \(\mathcal F_{L,L''}\) are isomorphisms. Since \(R\) is a field, this means \(\gamma(L,L',L'')\in R^\times\).

This is the only input from Schur's lemma: it identifies the line in which the composition lives. The next calculation identifies the scalar in that line.

Extracting the Scalar

Let \(\mathbf 1_{L''}\in\mathcal S_{L''}\) be the characteristic function of \(L''\). It belongs to \(\mathcal S_{L''}\): if \(c\in L''\), then both \(\mathbf 1_{L''}(v+c)\) and \(\mathbf 1_{L''}(v)\) vanish unless \(v\in L''\), and if \(v\in L''\) then \(i(v,c)=0\). Hence the covariance condition is satisfied.

The normalizing value of \(\mathcal F_{L,L''}\) on this vector is

$$ (\mathcal F_{L,L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(0)=1, $$

because

$$ (\mathcal F_{L,L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(0) = \sum_{b\in L/(L\cap L'')} \mathbf 1_{L''}(b), $$

and a representative \(b\in L\) lies in \(L''\) exactly for the zero coset of \(L/(L\cap L'')\).

Therefore evaluating

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''} = \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F_{L,L''} $$

on \(\mathbf 1_{L''}\) at \(0\) gives the scalar itself:

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'') = (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(0). $$

Now expand the two transition sums. The outer transition gives

$$ (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(0) = \sum_{b\in L/(L\cap L')} (\mathcal F_{L',L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(b), $$

because \(i(b,0)=0\). For each such \(b\), the inner transition gives

$$ (\mathcal F_{L',L''}\mathbf 1_{L''})(b) = \sum_{a\in L'/(L'\cap L'')} \xi(i(a,b))\,\mathbf 1_{L''}(b+a). $$

Combining the two formulas gives the explicit finite sum

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'') = \sum_{\substack{ b\in L/(L\cap L')\\ a\in L'/(L'\cap L'')\\ a+b\in L'' }} \xi(i(a,b)). $$

The condition \(a+b\in L''\) is exactly the condition that \(\mathbf 1_{L''}(b+a)\) be nonzero. No division or square root enters this formula.

Cocycle Identity

For four Lagrangians \(L,L',L'',L'''\), the scalars satisfy

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')\,\gamma(L,L'',L''') = \gamma(L',L'',L''')\,\gamma(L,L',L'''). $$

Indeed, associativity of composition gives

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ (\mathcal F_{L',L''}\circ \mathcal F_{L'',L'''}) = (\mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ \mathcal F_{L',L''}) \circ \mathcal F_{L'',L'''}. $$

Compute the left-hand side in two applications of the defining relation. First,

$$ \mathcal F_{L',L''}\circ \mathcal F_{L'',L'''} = \gamma(L',L'',L''')\mathcal F_{L',L'''}. $$

Composing with \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\) gives

$$ \gamma(L',L'',L''')\,\gamma(L,L',L''')\mathcal F_{L,L'''}, $$

because

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ \mathcal F_{L',L'''} = \gamma(L,L',L''')\mathcal F_{L,L'''}. $$

Similarly, for the right-hand side,

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}\circ \mathcal F_{L',L''} = \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F_{L,L''}, $$

and then

$$ \gamma(L,L',L'')\,\gamma(L,L'',L''')\mathcal F_{L,L'''}. $$

Since \(\mathcal F_{L,L'''}\neq0\), the cocycle identity follows.

Normalization Dependence

The scalar \(\gamma\) depends on the raw normalization of the intertwiners \(\mathcal F_{L,L'}\). If one rescales

$$ \mathcal F_{L,L'}^\alpha=\alpha_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L,L'}, \qquad \alpha_{L,L'}\in R^\times, $$

then the cocycle changes by the coboundary

$$ \gamma^\alpha(L,L',L'') = \frac{\alpha_{L,L'}\alpha_{L',L''}}{\alpha_{L,L''}} \gamma(L,L',L''). $$

Indeed,

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal F^\alpha_{L,L'}\mathcal F^\alpha_{L',L''} &= \alpha_{L,L'}\alpha_{L',L''} \mathcal F_{L,L'}\mathcal F_{L',L''}\\ &= \alpha_{L,L'}\alpha_{L',L''} \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F_{L,L''}\\ &= \frac{\alpha_{L,L'}\alpha_{L',L''}}{\alpha_{L,L''}} \gamma(L,L',L'')\mathcal F^\alpha_{L,L''}. \end{aligned} $$

Thus the Weil index records the failure of the chosen raw transition maps to compose strictly; it is not itself an invariant of the abstract Heisenberg representation without a normalization convention.
