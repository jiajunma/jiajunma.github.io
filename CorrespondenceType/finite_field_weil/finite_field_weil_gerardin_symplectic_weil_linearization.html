Gérardin Linearization of the Finite Symplectic Weil Cocycle

This node records the proof in Gérardin, Section 2.4, with the reduction lemma from Section 1.5 made explicit. It is different from the formal base-Lagrangian coboundary argument in Base-Lagrangian Trivialization of the Finite Weil-Index Cocycle: here the cocycle is the projective cocycle for the action of the full symplectic group on a fixed Heisenberg representation.

Statement

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:gerardin-symplectic-weil-linearization} Let \(k\) be a finite field of odd order \(q\) and characteristic \(p\). Let \((E,j)\) be a nondegenerate symplectic \(k\)-space, let \(H(E,j)\) be the finite Heisenberg group, and let \(S(E,j)\) be the symplectic group. Fix a nontrivial central character \(\xi:k\to \mathbb C^\times\) and let \(\eta_\xi\) be the corresponding finite Heisenberg representation.

The projective action of \(S(E,j)\) on \(\eta_\xi\) is linearizable. Equivalently, \(\eta_\xi\) extends to a representation of \(S(E,j)\ltimes H(E,j)\). The restriction to \(S(E,j)\) is the finite symplectic Weil representation. \end{theorem}

\begin{proof} We write \(S=S(E,j)\) and \(H=H(E,j)\). Since \(S\) acts on \(H\) by \(s(w,z)=(sw,z)\) and fixes the center, the Stone-von Neumann class \(\eta_\xi\) is \(S\)-stable. Therefore, after choosing a realization of \(\eta_\xi\), one obtains a projective representation of \(S\). Its cocycle is exactly the obstruction to extending \(\eta_\xi\) from \(H\) to \(S\ltimes H\).

We first construct an honest extension over a maximal parabolic subgroup. Choose a maximal isotropic subspace \(E_+\subset E\), and let \(P=P(E_+)\subset S\) be its stabilizer. Gérardin defines a character \(\chi_{E_+}\) of \(P\) by

$$ \chi_{E_+}(s)=\bigl(\det(s|_{E_+})\bigr)^{(q-1)/2}. $$

This is the unique nontrivial real character of \(P\) that is trivial on the subgroup acting trivially on \(E_+\). The subgroup \(E_+\times k\subset H\) is abelian. On the semidirect-product subgroup \(P\ltimes(E_+\times k)\subset P\ltimes H\), take the one-dimensional representation

$$ (s,x,z)\longmapsto \chi_{E_+}(s)\xi(z). $$

Induce it to \(P\ltimes H\). On restriction to \(H\), this induced representation has central character \(\xi\) and degree \([H:Z]^{1/2}\). By the finite Heisenberg Stone-von Neumann criterion, its restriction to \(H\) is a realization of \(\eta_\xi\). Hence the projective action is already linearized on \(P\).

Every parabolic subgroup of the finite symplectic group contains a \(p\)-Sylow subgroup of \(S\). Since \(P\) contains such a \(p\)-Sylow, the restriction of the projective cocycle to a \(p\)-Sylow is trivial. The group \(H\) is a \(p\)-group and \(\xi\) has order \(p\). Therefore Projective Cocycle Linearization Criterion for Finite Heisenberg Representations applies: the projective cocycle is trivial on all of \(S\). Thus \(\eta_\xi\) extends to \(S\ltimes H\).

This proves existence. For uniqueness, any two extensions differ by a one-dimensional character of \(S\), because \(\eta_\xi\) is irreducible on \(H\). Gérardin uses that finite symplectic groups in odd characteristic are perfect except for \(S=\operatorname{Sp}_2(\mathbb F_3)\). In the perfect cases, the character is trivial. In the exceptional case \(\operatorname{Sp}_2(\mathbb F_3)\), the possible extensions are separated by their decomposition into the known irreducibles of the binary tetrahedral group; Gérardin selects the unique extension disjoint from its complex conjugate. This gives the distinguished Weil representation. \(\square\) \end{proof}

Explicit Normalization in a Witt Model

Gérardin also records the normalization of the extension in a concrete Schrödinger model. This is where the finite quadratic Weil-index constants of Finite Quadratic Weil Index enter.

Fix a Witt decomposition

$$ E=E_+\oplus E_-, $$

with \(E_+\) and \(E_-\) maximal isotropic and paired by \(j\). Realize \(\eta_\xi\) on the space \(C[E_-]\) of functions on \(E_-\). If \(\tau:k\to \mathbb C^\times\) is the additive character corresponding to \(\xi\), the Heisenberg action is

$$ (\eta(x_++y_0,z)F)(y) = \tau(z+i(y,x_+))F(y+y_0), $$

for \(x_+\in E_+\), \(y_0,y\in E_-\), and \(z\in k\). The stabilizer \(P(E_+)\) acts by the parabolic formulas:

$$ (\pi_+(s)F)(y)=\chi_{E_+}(s)F(a_-^{-1}y) $$

for a Levi element with \(E_-\)-block \(a_-\), and

$$ (\pi_+(u_b)F)(y)=\tau(i(by,y))F(y) $$

for the unipotent element defined by an \(i\)-symmetric map \(b:E_-\to E_+\). These formulas give the already constructed \(P(E_+)\ltimes H\)-extension.

To extend from \(P(E_+)\) to \(S\), one must normalize the operator attached to an element exchanging \(E_+\) and \(E_-\). Let \(c:E_+\to E_-\) be an isomorphism. Let \(c^\vee:E_-\to E_+\) be the adjoint map required by the symplectic form, so that \(s_c\in S\) is represented by the block matrix

$$ s_c= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & c^\vee\\ c & 0 \end{pmatrix} $$

with respect to \(E_+\oplus E_-\). The underlying operator is a Fourier transform. With Gérardin's self-dual finite measure, put

$$ \widehat F(x)=\int_{E_-}F(y)\tau(i(y,x))\,dy, \qquad x\in E_+. $$

The operator has the form

$$ (\sigma(s_c)F)(y)=\varphi(c^{-1})\widehat F(c^{-1}y) $$

for \(y\in E_-\), where \(c^{-1}y\in E_+\). The scalar \(\varphi(c^{-1})\) is not arbitrary: the relations between \(P(E_+)\) and the element \(s_c\) force it.

Let \(b:E_-\to E_+\) be an \(i\)-symmetric isomorphism. Comparing the two sides of Gérardin's relation (2.13) gives a Gaussian sum \(w_\tau(b)\) with

$$ \int_{E_-}\tau(i(by',y')-i(by',y))\,dy' = w_\tau(b)\,\tau(i(by,y))^{-1}. $$

The same relation forces

$$ \varphi(b)w_\tau(b)=1. $$

The Gaussian sum is evaluated by diagonalizing the quadratic form \(y\mapsto i(by,y)\). If \(d(b)\) is its discriminant, then

$$ w_\tau(b)=W(\tau)^{\dim E_-}d(b)^{(1-q)/2}, \qquad W(\tau)=\int_k \tau(t^2/2)\,dt. $$

Thus the Fourier operator normalization is determined by the one-dimensional Gauss sum \(W(\tau)\) and the discriminant of the quadratic form. Gérardin's formula (2.18) can be written as

$$ (\sigma(s_c)F)(y) = W(\tau)^{-\dim E_-}(\det c)^{(1-q)/2}\widehat F(c^{-1}y), $$

up to the notational convention for identifying \(E_+\) and \(E_-^\vee\).

The calculation also eliminates the remaining possible characters in the extension. A possible modification on the parabolic side is a character of the Levi and a character of the unipotent radical. Conjugation by the Levi forces the unipotent character to be trivial, except for the small \(\operatorname{Sp}_2(\mathbb F_3)\) case already handled above. The Gaussian relation then forces the Levi character to be trivial. Consequently the Fourier-Gaussian formula fixes the same distinguished extension obtained abstractly from the \(p\)-Sylow argument.

Nonmaximal Isotropic Parabolics

Gérardin also proves the compatible statement for a nonzero isotropic subspace \(E_1\subset E\). Choose a decomposition

$$ E=E_1\oplus E_0\oplus E_-, $$

where \(E_-\) is isotropic and dual to \(E_1\), and \(E_0\) is symplectic. The representation over the stabilizer \(P(E_1)\) is realized on

$$ C[E_-]\otimes \mathcal H_0, $$

where \(\mathcal H_0\) carries the already constructed Weil representation of \(S(E_0)\ltimes H(E_0)\). On the \(E_1\oplus E_-\) part the formulas are the same Fourier and quadratic-character formulas as in the maximal case; on the \(E_0\) part the action is the lower-dimensional Weil representation.

The remaining ambiguity could only be a one-dimensional character on the \(S(E_0)\)-factor. Gérardin rules this out by restricting to rank-one symplectic subgroups and their unipotent radicals; the characters agree on the relevant Sylow subgroups, hence the possible character is trivial. This finishes the proof that the parabolic model in part (b) of the symplectic Weil representation theorem is the restriction of the same global Weil representation.

Relation to Lagrangian Transition Cocycles

The theorem trivializes the projective cocycle for the \(S(E,j)\)-action on a fixed Heisenberg representation. Once one writes this representation in different Schrödinger models, the same choice of linear Weil representation provides coherent normalizations of the Fourier intertwiners between those models. The resulting scalar corrections are the finite-field Weil-index or Gaussian-sum constants. This is the Gérardin source for the statement that the finite-field obstruction is not a genuine metaplectic obstruction: over a finite field of odd characteristic, the projective Weil cocycle is a coboundary.
