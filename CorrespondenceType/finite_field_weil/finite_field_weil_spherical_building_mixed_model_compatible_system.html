Compatible System of Mixed Schrödinger Models on the Spherical Building

This node extends the Lagrangian compatible system of Compatible System of Schrödinger-Model Intertwiners over a Finite Field from chambers to all facets of the spherical building of a finite symplectic group, using the Gérardin mixed model attached to a parabolic. It is the finite-field prototype of the compatible system one wants on the $p$-adic Bruhat–Tits side.

What a "compatible system" is, in one paragraph

For each facet $\mathbf F$ we have a concrete model $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ of the unique abstract Weil representation $V$. The collection $\{\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\}_{\mathbf F}$ is a priori a "discrete bundle" of vector spaces over the facet poset, with each fiber isomorphic to $V$ only abstractly (Schur gives a one-dimensional Hom for every ordered pair, with no canonical generator). A compatible system is a coherent choice of canonical generators of all these Hom-lines — equivalently, a family

$$\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\qquad(\text{for all ordered pairs of facets})$$

satisfying identity on the diagonal, cocycle composition (middle index cancels), inverse on the swap, and $G$-equivariance. Such a family trivializes the discrete bundle: it identifies every $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ with a single ambient $V$. The theorem below says this trivialization exists and is unique up to per-facet rescaling, and the only essential input is Gérardin's linearization theorem at every relevant rank — the input that fails on the $p$-adic side and forces the genuine metaplectic cover there.

Setup

Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field of odd order $q$ and residue characteristic $p$. Let $(E,j)$ be a nondegenerate symplectic $k$-space of dimension $2m$, $i=j/2$, $H(E,j)$ its Heisenberg group, $G=\operatorname{Sp}(E,j)$, and $\xi\colon k\to R^{\times}$ a nontrivial character. The coefficient field $R$ has $\operatorname{char}R\neq p$, contains the values of $\xi$, and is a splitting field for the relevant finite Heisenberg groups.

The spherical building $\Delta(G)$ has facets indexed by isotropic flags

$$\mathbf F\colon\ C_1\subsetneq C_2\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq C_r,\qquad C_i\subseteq C_i^{\perp},\ 1\leq r\leq m,$$

with convention $C_0=0$; the stabilizer $P_{\mathbf F}=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\mathbf F)$ is the parabolic of $\mathbf F$, with Levi $\prod_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{GL}(C_i/C_{i-1})\times\operatorname{Sp}(E_{C_r})$ where $E_{C}:=C^\perp/C$. Chambers are saturated flags ending in a Lagrangian. The face relation is sub-flag inclusion: $\mathbf F\leq\mathbf E$ iff $\mathbf F$ is obtained from $\mathbf E$ by removing some elements of the flag; in particular the maximal element of $\mathbf F$ is a subspace of the maximal element of $\mathbf E$.

Rank hypothesis. For every rank $2k$ that appears in the recursion below ($0\leq k\leq m-\dim C_r$ as $\mathbf F$ ranges over the facets), the group $\operatorname{Sp}_{2k}(k)$ is perfect. This excludes $(k,q)\in\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,2)\}$; in particular it is automatic for $q\geq 5$ or for $m\geq 3$.

Recursively define $W_\xi^{E_C}$ for each isotropic $C\subseteq E$ to be the canonical linear Weil representation of $\operatorname{Sp}(E_C)$ from Gérardin Linearization of the Finite Symplectic Weil Cocycle; the rank hypothesis ensures uniqueness. Write $\eta_\xi^{E_C}$ for its Heisenberg action of $H(E_C)$. The base $V=W_\xi^E$ has dimension $q^m$.

The mixed model at a facet

For a facet $\mathbf F$ with maximal isotropic $C=C_r$, define

$$ \mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\;:=\;\Big\{\varphi\colon E\to W_\xi^{E_C}\ \big|\ \varphi(v+x)=\xi\!\big(i(v,x)\big)\,\eta_\xi^{E_C}(\bar x)\,\varphi(v)\ \forall x\in C^{\perp}\Big\}, $$

where $\bar x$ is the image of $x$ in $E_C=C^\perp/C$. The standard $H(E,j)$-action

$$ \big(\eta_\xi(u,s)\varphi\big)(v)=\xi\!\big(s+i(u,v)\big)\,\varphi(v-u) $$

preserves $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$, and the Levi acts canonically: each $\operatorname{GL}(C_i/C_{i-1})$ acts on the coset variable through Gérardin's quadratic determinant character $\chi_{C_i/C_{i-1}}$, while $\operatorname{Sp}(E_C)$ acts on the values via $W_\xi^{E_C}$. Counting dimensions,

$$\dim_R\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}=[E:C^{\perp}]\cdot\dim_R W_\xi^{E_C}=q^{\dim C}\cdot q^{m-\dim C}=q^{m},$$

so each $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ is a full model of the irreducible $V$. By induction-in-stages, $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\cong\operatorname{ind}_{H(C_r^{\perp})}^{H(E,j)}W_\xi^{E_{C_r}}$ — Gérardin's parabolic-induction description of the mixed model.

Canonical intertwiners: explicit on one direction

For any ordered pair of facets we use the convention

$$\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\longrightarrow\mathcal M_{\mathbf F},$$

so that composition cancels the middle index. Schur on the irreducible $V$ gives $\dim_R\operatorname{Hom}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal M_{\mathbf E},\mathcal M_{\mathbf F})=1$, hence $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ is determined up to a unique nonzero scalar.

Incident pair, the pointwise direction. Let $\mathbf F\leq\mathbf E$ with $C_r:=\max\mathbf F\subseteq C_s:=\max\mathbf E$. The Gérardin reduction at the smaller rank gives a canonical surjective $H(E_{C_s})$-equivariant projection

$$\rho_{C_s/C_r}\colon W_\xi^{E_{C_r}}\twoheadrightarrow W_\xi^{E_{C_s}}$$

onto the $C_s/C_r$-invariants (up to Gérardin's $\chi$-twist on the Levi quotient). The pointwise formula

$$ \boxed{\;\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf E,\mathbf F}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\longrightarrow\mathcal M_{\mathbf E},\qquad \varphi\longmapsto\rho_{C_s/C_r}\circ\varphi\;} $$

is a well-defined $H(E,j)$-isomorphism. The map goes from the smaller-isotropic mixed model to the bigger-isotropic one, not the reverse. To check well-definedness: for $x\in C_s^{\perp}\subseteq C_r^{\perp}$ the covariance of $\varphi$ gives $\varphi(v+x)=\xi(i(v,x))\,\eta_\xi^{E_{C_r}}(\bar x_{E_{C_r}})\varphi(v)$; applying $\rho_{C_s/C_r}$ and using $\bar x_{E_{C_r}}\in(C_s/C_r)^{\perp}\subseteq E_{C_r}$ gives the corresponding $E_{C_s}$-covariance of $\rho_{C_s/C_r}\circ\varphi$, which is exactly the covariance defining $\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}$. Bijectivity follows from Schur on irreducibles since the dimensions agree at $q^m$.

Incident pair, the abstract direction. The reverse map

$$\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\longrightarrow\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$$

is the inverse of the pointwise reduction. It exists and is canonical by Schur, but it is not pointwise — the naive "$\psi\mapsto\iota\circ\psi$" using a section $\iota$ of $\rho_{C_s/C_r}$ would land in a function with $C_s^{\perp}$-covariance only, not the $C_r^{\perp}$-covariance demanded by $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$. The explicit inverse is the parabolic induction-in-stages unfolding: write $\varphi(v)\in W_\xi^{E_{C_r}}$ via the Gérardin realization of $W_\xi^{E_{C_r}}$ as functions $E_{C_r}\to W_\xi^{E_{C_s}}$ with $(C_s/C_r)^{\perp}$-covariance, then the value $\varphi(v)$ at the coset of $\bar c\in C_s/C_r\subseteq E_{C_r}$ is determined by $\psi(v+c)$ for $c\in C_s$ lifting $\bar c$, up to a Heisenberg phase. The cleanest concrete unfolding is via the Lagrangian-collapse identification below.

Non-incident pair. For general $\mathbf F,\mathbf E$ the canonical $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ is defined by composing pointwise reductions and their inverses along any chain in the facet poset from $\mathbf E$ to $\mathbf F$; chain-independence is forced by the cocycle property below.

Composition cocycle

There are unique $\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G)\in R^{\times}$ such that

$$\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\circ\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}\;=\;\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G)\,\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G},$$

and $\Gamma$ satisfies the standard $2$-cocycle identity by associativity, verbatim as in Orthogonality and Trivialization of the Change-of-Lagrangian Cocycle:

$$\Gamma(\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf C)\,\Gamma(\mathbf A,\mathbf C,\mathbf D)=\Gamma(\mathbf B,\mathbf C,\mathbf D)\,\Gamma(\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf D).$$

Statement

  1. (Trivialization.) Fix any base facet $\mathbf F_0$ (e.g. the empty flag, the facet with no $C_i$'s) and set $c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}:=\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf F_0)$. Then $\Gamma=\delta c$, and $$ \widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\;:=\;c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}^{-1}\,\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\;\colon\;\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal M_{\mathbf F} $$ is an exact cocycle of $H(E,j)$-isomorphisms: $$ \widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf F}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}},\quad \widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\circ\widetilde T_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}=\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G},\quad \widetilde T_{\mathbf E,\mathbf F}=\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}^{-1}. $$

  2. ($G$-equivariance.) Under the rank hypothesis the system can be chosen $G$-equivariant: $g\,\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\,g^{-1}=\widetilde T_{g\mathbf F,g\mathbf E}$ for $g\in G$.

  3. (Uniqueness.) Any other system of $H(E,j)$-equivariant isomorphisms $T'_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\to\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ satisfying the compatibility differs from $\widetilde T$ only by per-facet rescaling: there are $\nu_{\mathbf F}\in R^{\times}$ with $T'_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=(\nu_{\mathbf E}/\nu_{\mathbf F})\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$.

Proof

Cocycle trivialization. Applying the $2$-cocycle identity of $\Gamma$ to the quadruple $(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G,\mathbf F_0)$ gives

$$ \Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G)\,\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf G,\mathbf F_0)=\Gamma(\mathbf E,\mathbf G,\mathbf F_0)\,\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf F_0), $$

so $\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G)=c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\,c_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}\,c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}^{-1}$, i.e. $\Gamma=\delta c$ as $1$-cochain coboundary. Substituting into $\widetilde T=c^{-1}\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}$ gives

$$\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\circ\widetilde T_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}=\frac{\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf G)}{c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\,c_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}}\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}=c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}^{-1}\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}=\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}.$$

Diagonal: $c_{\mathbf F,\mathbf F}=\Gamma(\mathbf F,\mathbf F,\mathbf F_0)=1$ because $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf F}=\operatorname{id}$. Inverse: taking $\mathbf G=\mathbf F$ in the composition law gives $\widetilde T_{\mathbf E,\mathbf F}=\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}^{-1}$.

$G$-equivariance. The cocycle $\Gamma$ is $G$-invariant because the pointwise reductions $\rho_{C_s/C_r}$ are natural in the symplectic data and $G$ permutes facets. Under the rank hypothesis and Gérardin Linearization of the Finite Symplectic Weil Cocycle, each value representation $W_\xi^{E_C}$ is the unique linear $\operatorname{Sp}(E_C)$-representative of its class; hence $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ is $G$-equivariant on the nose, and the same is true of $\widetilde T$ after the coboundary rescaling.

Uniqueness. Let $T'$ be another compatible system. Since each Hom-line is one-dimensional, there are unique $\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\in R^{\times}$ with $T'_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\,\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$. The compatibility of both $T'$ and $\widetilde T$ forces $\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\,\mu_{\mathbf E,\mathbf G}=\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf G}$ and $\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf F}=1$. Fix any base $\mathbf B$ and set $\nu_{\mathbf F}:=\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf B}$; taking $(\mathbf F,\mathbf E,\mathbf B)$ in the cocycle gives $\mu_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=\nu_{\mathbf F}/\nu_{\mathbf E}$. So $T'_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=(\nu_{\mathbf F}/\nu_{\mathbf E})\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$, which under the convention $\widetilde T\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\to\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ is the relabeling of each $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ by the scalar $\nu_{\mathbf F}$. $\square$

Concrete realization: Lagrangian collapse

For computation, the abstract direction $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}\to\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ (which is not pointwise) is best replaced by the fully concrete Schrödinger model. Assign to each facet $\mathbf F$ a Lagrangian extension $L_{\mathbf F}\supseteq C_r^{\mathbf F}$ (equivalently, a chamber refining $\mathbf F$). By induction-in-stages,

$$ \mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\;=\;\operatorname{ind}_{H(C_r^\perp)}^{H(E)}\,\operatorname{ind}_{\bar L_{\mathbf F}\times k}^{H(E_{C_r})}\bar\xi\;=\;\operatorname{ind}_{L_{\mathbf F}\times k}^{H(E)}\xi\;=\;\mathcal S_{L_{\mathbf F}}, $$

where $\bar L_{\mathbf F}=L_{\mathbf F}/C_r$ is the descended Lagrangian of $E_{C_r}$. So the mixed model is the ordinary Schrödinger model on the chosen Lagrangian, the recursive value-space data unfolds into ordinary $L_{\mathbf F}$-covariance, and

$$ \widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}\;=\;\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L_{\mathbf F},L_{\mathbf E}} $$

from Compatible System of Schrödinger-Model Intertwiners over a Finite Field, with an explicit character-sum kernel in both directions. The Lagrangian assignment $\mathbf F\mapsto L_{\mathbf F}$ is a gauge: different assignments yield equivalent compatible systems by part 3 above.

Recovering the chamber case

For a chamber $\mathbf F$ ending in Lagrangian $L=C_m$, $L_{\mathbf F}=L$ is forced, $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}=\mathcal S_L$, and $\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}$ exactly as in the Lagrangian theorem. The spherical-building theorem is thus a strict extension of the Lagrangian theorem from chambers to all facets, realized through the parabolic structure of $P_{\mathbf F}$.

Role of odd characteristic and Gérardin's linearization

Odd characteristic provides $i=j/2$ and $q\in R^{\times}$, exactly as for the Lagrangian theorem. The new ingredient at the facet level is the recursive use of Gérardin's linearization theorem at every rank $2k$ that appears as $E_C$ for some isotropic $C\subseteq E$. At each rank the linear lift exists and is unique by perfectness (the rank hypothesis), so the value spaces $W_\xi^{E_C}$ are canonically determined and the canonical intertwiners propagate without projective ambiguity. Without this input, only the projective system of intertwiners exists (one Hom-line per pair, no preferred generator), and the theorem reduces to the existence of a compatible system rather than a canonical one.

This is the input that fails on the $p$-adic side: the metaplectic cover $\operatorname{Mp}(W)\to\operatorname{Sp}(W)$ is a genuine nontrivial extension, the linearization is unavailable, and the residue is the genuine splitting character $\delta$.

What this is for

This is the finite-field model of the Bruhat–Tits compatible system one wants in the $p$-adic generalized lattice-model transition problem: facets ↔ generalized lattice models, parabolics ↔ parahorics, mixed model on values ↔ oscillator-rep coefficients at the residual symplectic. The finite-field statement carries no metaplectic obstruction; the $p$-adic counterpart will carry the genuine splitting character $\delta$ as its only residue.

Summary of corrections from the previous version

The earlier draft had the explicit pointwise formula in the wrong direction: it claimed $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}=\iota_{C_s/C_r}\circ(-)$ pointwise from $\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}$ to $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$, but this map fails the $C_r^{\perp}$-covariance demanded by $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}$ (its image carries only $C_s^{\perp}$-covariance). The corrected pointwise formula uses the Gérardin reduction $\rho_{C_s/C_r}$ instead of an inclusion, and goes the other way: $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf E,\mathbf F}=\rho_{C_s/C_r}\circ(-)\colon\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}\to\mathcal M_{\mathbf E}$. The reverse direction $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{mix}}_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ is canonical by Schur but not pointwise; the Lagrangian collapse below makes it concretely computable via a character sum.
