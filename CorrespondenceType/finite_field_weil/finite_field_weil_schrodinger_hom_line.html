One-Dimensional Hom Line for Finite Schrödinger Models

Use the setup of Change-of-Lagrangian Intertwiner for Finite Schrödinger Models. Thus \(k\) has odd order, \((E,j)\) is symplectic, \(i=j/2\), and \(\mathcal S_L\) is the Schrödinger model attached to a Lagrangian \(L\).

Statement

For Lagrangians \(L,L'\subset E\),

$$ \operatorname{Hom}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal S_{L'},\mathcal S_L) = R\cdot \mathcal F_{L,L'}. $$

In particular this Hom space is one-dimensional over \(R\), and every \(H(E,j)\)-endomorphism of \(\mathcal S_L\) is scalar.

Proof

First prove the endomorphism statement for \(\mathcal S_L\). Choose a Lagrangian complement \(M\) of \(L\), so

$$ E=M\oplus L. $$

Such an \(M\) exists by extending a basis of \(L\) to a symplectic basis of \(E\). Restriction to \(M\) identifies \(\mathcal S_L\) with all functions on \(M\): if \(v=m+a\) with \(m\in M\), \(a\in L\), then the covariance rule forces

$$ \varphi(m+a)=\xi(i(m,a))\varphi(m). $$

Let \(e_m\) be the function corresponding to the delta function at \(m\in M\). The action of \(M\subset H(E,j)\) is translation:

$$ \eta_\xi(u,0)e_m=e_{m+u} \qquad (u,m\in M), $$

because \(M\) is isotropic. The action of \(L\subset H(E,j)\) is diagonal:

$$ \eta_\xi(a,0)e_m=\xi(j(a,m))e_m \qquad (a\in L,\ m\in M). $$

Indeed,

$$ (\eta_\xi(a,0)\varphi)(m) =\xi(i(a,m))\varphi(m-a) =\xi(i(a,m))\xi(i(m,-a))\varphi(m) =\xi(j(a,m))\varphi(m). $$

The characters

$$ a\longmapsto \xi(j(a,m)) $$

of \(L\) are distinct as \(m\) varies in \(M\). If \(m\neq m'\), then \(m-m'\neq0\); nondegeneracy of \(j\) and \(L^\perp=L\) imply that the linear functional \(a\mapsto j(a,m-m')\) on \(L\) is nonzero, hence takes some value outside \(\ker\xi\).

Let \(T\in\operatorname{End}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal S_L)\). Write

$$ T e_m=\sum_{n\in M} t_{n,m}e_n. $$

Commuting with every \(\eta_\xi(a,0)\), \(a\in L\), gives

$$ \xi(j(a,n))t_{n,m}=\xi(j(a,m))t_{n,m} \qquad \text{for all }a\in L. $$

By distinctness of the characters, \(t_{n,m}=0\) for \(n\neq m\). Thus \(T\) is diagonal:

$$ T e_m=\lambda_m e_m. $$

Commuting with translations by \(u\in M\) gives

$$ \lambda_{m+u}=\lambda_m \qquad (m,u\in M). $$

Hence all \(\lambda_m\) are equal, and \(T\) is scalar.

Now let

$$ T:\mathcal S_{L'}\to\mathcal S_L $$

be \(H(E,j)\)-linear. By Orthogonality of Change-of-Lagrangian Intertwiners, \(\mathcal F_{L',L}\) is an isomorphism. Therefore

$$ \mathcal F_{L',L}T\in \operatorname{End}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal S_{L'}) $$

is scalar, say \(\mathcal F_{L',L}T=\lambda\operatorname{id}\). Since

$$ \mathcal F_{L',L}^{-1} = q^{-d_{L,L'}}\mathcal F_{L,L'}, $$

we get

$$ T=\lambda q^{-d_{L,L'}}\mathcal F_{L,L'}. $$

Thus

$$ \operatorname{Hom}_{H(E,j)}(\mathcal S_{L'},\mathcal S_L) =R\cdot\mathcal F_{L,L'}. $$

\(\square\)
