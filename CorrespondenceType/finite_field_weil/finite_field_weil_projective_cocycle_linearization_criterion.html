Projective Cocycle Linearization Criterion for Finite Heisenberg Representations

This is Gérardin's reduction lemma, written in the notation needed for the finite-field Weil representation.

Setup

Let \(H\) be a finite group with center \(Z\), and let \(\xi\) be a character of \(Z\) satisfying the central-commutator condition of Finite Heisenberg Central Character Criterion. Thus there is a unique irreducible class \(\eta_\xi\) of representations of \(H\) whose restriction to \(Z\) is \(\xi\).

Let \(G\) be a finite group of automorphisms of \(H\) acting trivially on \(Z\). Fix a realization \(\eta\) of \(\eta_\xi\). For each \(g\in G\), the representation

$$ h\longmapsto \eta(g h g^{-1}) $$

has the same central character \(\xi\). By uniqueness it is isomorphic to \(\eta\). Choose an intertwining operator \(Y(g)\), so

$$ Y(g)\eta(h)Y(g)^{-1}=\eta(g h g^{-1}). $$

The operator \(Y(g)\) is unique up to scalar. Therefore Schur's lemma gives a scalar \(c(g,g')\in \mathbb C^\times\) with

$$ Y(g)Y(g')=c(g,g')Y(gg'). $$

After choosing \(Y(g)\) unitary, \(c(g,g')\in U(1)\). Associativity of operator multiplication gives the cocycle identity

$$ c(g,g')c(gg',g'')=c(g,g'g'')c(g',g''). $$

The class \([c]\in H^2(G,U(1))\) is the projective Weil cocycle. It is trivial exactly when the operators \(Y(g)\) can be rescaled to satisfy \(Y(g)Y(g')=Y(gg')\), equivalently when \(\eta\) extends to a representation of the semidirect product \(G\ltimes H\).

Statement

\begin{lemma} \label{lem:finite-heisenberg-projective-cocycle-linearization} Assume in addition that \(H\) is a \(p\)-group and that the nontrivial central character \(\xi\) has order \(p\). Let \(G_p\subset G\) be a \(p\)-Sylow subgroup. The projective representation of \(G\) attached to \(\eta_\xi\) has a linearization if and only if its restriction to \(G_p\) has a linearization. \end{lemma}

\begin{proof} One direction is immediate: a linear representation of \(G\) restricts to a linear representation of \(G_p\). We prove the converse.

Let \([c]\in H^2(G,U(1))\) be the projective cocycle class. First we show that \([c]\) is \(p\)-primary. Since \(\xi\) has order \(p\), the character \(\xi^{-1}\) is \(\xi^{p-1}\). Gérardin's finite Heisenberg character calculation says that the tensor power \(\eta^{\otimes(p-1)}\) is a direct sum of copies of the irreducible representation with central character \(\xi^{-1}\). Indeed, on \(Z\) the tensor power has central character \(\xi^{p-1}=\xi^{-1}\), and its character is still supported on \(Z\); the degree comparison gives the multiplicity.

The projective \(G\)-action on \(\eta^{\otimes(p-1)}\) has cocycle \(c^{p-1}\). The representation with central character \(\xi^{-1}\) is the contragredient, equivalently the complex conjugate, of the representation with central character \(\xi\). Its projective cocycle is therefore \(c^{-1}\). Since \(\eta^{\otimes(p-1)}\) is a multiple of that conjugate class, the two projective cocycles define the same class:

$$ [c]^{p-1}=[c]^{-1}. $$

Hence

$$ [c]^p=1. $$

Thus \([c]\) lies in the \(p\)-primary subgroup of \(H^2(G,U(1))\).

Now suppose the restriction to \(G_p\) is linearizable. Then

$$ \operatorname{res}^{G}_{G_p}([c])=0. $$

For finite groups, restriction and corestriction satisfy

$$ \operatorname{cor}^{G}_{G_p}\operatorname{res}^{G}_{G_p}(x) =[G:G_p]\,x $$

for every \(x\in H^2(G,U(1))\). Applying this to \(x=[c]\) gives \([G:G_p][c]=0\). The integer \([G:G_p]\) is prime to \(p\), while \([c]\) has \(p\)-power order. Therefore \([c]=0\).

Thus the projective cocycle is a coboundary on \(G\), so the projective representation of \(G\) can be rescaled to a genuine representation. This is the desired linearization. \(\square\) \end{proof}

Role in Gérardin's Proof

For the symplectic finite-field Weil representation, \(H=H(E,j)\) is a \(p\)-group and \(G=S(E,j)\). A parabolic subgroup stabilizing a maximal isotropic subspace contains a \(p\)-Sylow subgroup of \(S(E,j)\). Therefore it is enough to construct the Heisenberg representation over that parabolic; Gérardin then invokes this lemma to obtain a global linearization over \(S(E,j)\).
