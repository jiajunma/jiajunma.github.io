Canonical Mixed Model on the Spherical Building via Gérardin Reduction

This node gives the canonical, $L_{\mathbf F}$-free formulation of the compatible system of mixed Weil models on the spherical building of a finite symplectic group. It replaces the per-facet Lagrangian-extension data $\mathbf F\mapsto L_{\mathbf F}$ of Compatible System of Mixed Schrödinger Models on the Spherical Building by the single global choice of one Weil-representation realization $\Omega$, from which every facet's mixed model is derived canonically as a subspace-of-$\Omega$ Gérardin reduction together with a Heisenberg induction.

The point: every facet's mixed model is then canonically isomorphic to $\Omega$ itself, and the entire "compatible system on the building" becomes the trivial identity system on $\Omega$ enriched by Gérardin's subspace filtration. No per-facet auxiliary choices remain; the only gauge is the choice of $\Omega$ (equivalent to choosing one reference Lagrangian $L_0$ when $\Omega=\mathcal S_{L_0}$).

Setup

Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field of odd order $q$ and characteristic $p$. Let $(E,j)$ be a nondegenerate symplectic $k$-space, $\dim_k E=2m$, and set $i:=j/2$. Let $R$ be a field with $\operatorname{char}R\neq p$ and let $\xi\colon k\to R^{\times}$ be a nontrivial additive character. Let $H(E,j)=E\times k$ denote the Heisenberg group with multiplication

$$(v_1,z_1)(v_2,z_2)\ =\ (v_1+v_2,\,z_1+z_2+i(v_1,v_2)).$$

Fix once and for all an irreducible Weil representation

$$\eta\colon H(E,j)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_R(\Omega)$$

with central character $\xi$ (existence and Stone–von Neumann uniqueness are Finite Heisenberg Stone-von Neumann Property). A concrete choice is the Schrödinger model $\Omega:=\mathcal S_{L_0}$ on any Lagrangian $L_0\subseteq E$ (Lagrangian Schrödinger Model and Change-of-Lagrangian Intertwiner); the construction below is parametrized only by $\Omega$, not by any Lagrangian choice.

For an isotropic subspace $C\subseteq E$ write

$$C^{\perp}\ :=\ \{x\in E\,\mid\,j(x,c)=0\ \forall c\in C\},\qquad E_C\ :=\ C^{\perp}/C.$$

Then $C\subseteq C^{\perp}$, $\dim C^{\perp}=2m-\dim C$, and $E_C$ inherits a nondegenerate symplectic form $j_C$ from $j|_{C^{\perp}\times C^{\perp}}$ (well-defined because $j(C,C^{\perp})=0$, and nondegenerate because $C^{\perp}/C$ is the maximal nondegenerate quotient of the restriction). Let $H(E_C,j_C)=E_C\times k$ be the corresponding Heisenberg group.

1. The canonical Gérardin reduction $\Omega^C$

Definition. For an isotropic subspace $C\subseteq E$, define

$$\boxed{\ \Omega^C\ :=\ \{\,v\in\Omega\ \mid\ \eta(c,0)\,v=v\quad\forall c\in C\,\}\ \subseteq\ \Omega.\ }$$

That is, $\Omega^C$ is the subspace of $\Omega$ pointwise fixed by the abelian subgroup $\eta(C\times\{0\})$ of $\operatorname{End}_R(\Omega)$.

The subset $C\times\{0\}\subset H(E,j)$ is indeed an abelian subgroup because $C$ is isotropic: $(c_1,0)(c_2,0)=(c_1+c_2,i(c_1,c_2))=(c_1+c_2,0)$ since $i(c_1,c_2)=j(c_1,c_2)/2=0$.

1.1 Canonical projection $P_C\colon\Omega\twoheadrightarrow\Omega^C$

Since $|C|=q^{\dim C}\in R^{\times}$ (as $\operatorname{char}R\neq p$), the operator

$$\boxed{\ P_C\ :=\ \frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\eta(c,0)\ \in\ \operatorname{End}_R(\Omega)\ }$$

is well defined and is the unique $H(C^{\perp},j)$-equivariant projection of $\Omega$ onto $\Omega^C$.

Proof

(i) $P_C^2=P_C$ and $\operatorname{im}P_C=\Omega^C$: standard averaging argument over the finite abelian group $C$. (ii) $H(C^{\perp},j)$-equivariance: for $h=(x,z)\in H(C^{\perp},j)$ and $c\in C$, the commutator in $H(E,j)$ is $(c,0)(x,z)(c,0)^{-1}(x,z)^{-1}=(0,2i(c,x))$, and $j(c,x)=0$ since $c\in C, x\in C^{\perp}$, so $\eta(c,0)$ commutes with $\eta(h)$. Averaging in $c$ preserves equivariance. (iii) Uniqueness: any $H(C^{\perp},j)$-equivariant projection onto $\Omega^C$ must be $P_C$ by Schur on each isotypic component (cf. §1.3). $\square$

1.2 Dimension lemma

$$\boxed{\ \dim_R\Omega^C\ =\ q^{m-\dim C}.\ }$$

Proof. Take the trace of the averaging:

$$\dim_R\Omega^C\ =\ \operatorname{tr}P_C\ =\ \frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\operatorname{tr}\eta(c,0).$$

The character of the irreducible Heisenberg–Weil representation (Finite Heisenberg Stone-von Neumann Property) satisfies $\operatorname{tr}\eta(v,z)=q^m\,\xi(z)\,\delta_{v,0}$, so $\operatorname{tr}\eta(c,0)=q^m\delta_{c,0}$. Hence $\dim_R\Omega^C=q^m/|C|=q^{m-\dim C}$. $\square$

1.3 Residual Heisenberg action descends to $H(E_C,j_C)$

The restriction $\eta|_{H(C^{\perp},j)}$ preserves $\Omega^C$ (by §1.1, since elements of $H(C^{\perp},j)$ commute with $\eta(C,0)$). Furthermore, $C\times\{0\}\subseteq H(C^{\perp},j)$ acts trivially on $\Omega^C$ by definition. Therefore the $H(C^{\perp},j)$-action factors through the quotient

$$H(C^{\perp},j)\,\big/\,(C\times\{0\})\ =\ (C^{\perp}/C)\times k\ =\ H(E_C,j_C),$$

where the displayed equality uses that the Heisenberg cocycle $i(x,y)$ descends to $C^{\perp}/C$: $i(x+c,y+c')=i(x,y)+i(c,y)+i(x,c')+i(c,c')=i(x,y)$ since $j(c,y)=j(x,c')=j(c,c')=0$ for $c,c'\in C$, $x,y\in C^{\perp}$.

1.4 Gérardin theorem (residual Weil structure)

$\Omega^C$ together with the descended $H(E_C,j_C)$-action is an irreducible Weil representation of $H(E_C,j_C)$ with central character $\xi$. Hence, by Stone–von Neumann uniqueness applied to $E_C$,

$$\boxed{\ \Omega^C\ \cong\ W_\xi^{E_C}\ \text{as $H(E_C,j_C)$-modules}.\ }$$

Proof. The central $k$-subgroup of $H(E_C,j_C)$ embeds as $\{0\}\times k\subseteq H(C^{\perp},j)$, on which $\eta$ acts by $\xi$ (the central character of $\Omega$). Hence the central character on $\Omega^C$ is $\xi$. The dimension $q^{m-\dim C}=q^{\dim_k E_C/2}$ matches the unique irreducible Heisenberg–Weil representation of $E_C$. Irreducibility follows from §2 below ($\Omega$ is induced from $\Omega^C$ via $H(C^{\perp},j)\subseteq H(E,j)$, and irreducibility of $\Omega$ forces irreducibility of $\Omega^C$ by Frobenius reciprocity). This is the precise content of Gérardin's mixed-model construction (Gérardin Linearization of the Finite Symplectic Weil Cocycle). $\square$

2. The canonical mixed model $M_C$

Definition. For an isotropic $C\subseteq E$, define

$$\boxed{\ M_C\ :=\ \operatorname{ind}_{H(C^{\perp},j)}^{H(E,j)}\Omega^C.\ }$$

Concretely, $M_C$ is the space of functions $f\colon H(E,j)\to\Omega^C$ satisfying

$$f(h\cdot g)\ =\ \eta|_{H(C^{\perp},j)}(h)\cdot f(g)\qquad\forall h\in H(C^{\perp},j),\ g\in H(E,j),$$

with $H(E,j)$-action by right translation: $(g_0\cdot f)(g)=f(g\cdot g_0)$.

2.1 Dimension

Using $|H(E,j)/H(C^{\perp},j)|=|E/C^{\perp}|=q^{\dim C}$ and §1.2,

$$\dim_R M_C\ =\ |E/C^{\perp}|\cdot\dim_R\Omega^C\ =\ q^{\dim C}\cdot q^{m-\dim C}\ =\ q^m\ =\ \dim_R\Omega.$$

2.2 The canonical isomorphism $\tau_C\colon\Omega\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} M_C$

Definition. Define

$$\boxed{\ \tau_C\colon\Omega\longrightarrow M_C,\qquad\tau_C(v)(g)\ :=\ P_C\bigl(\eta(g)v\bigr)\ \in\ \Omega^C,\ }$$

where $P_C\colon\Omega\twoheadrightarrow\Omega^C$ is the canonical projection of §1.1.

Proposition. $\tau_C$ is a canonical $H(E,j)$-equivariant isomorphism.

Proof. We verify (a) well-definedness, (b) equivariance, (c) isomorphism.

(a) Well-definedness. Need $\tau_C(v)(h g)=\eta(h)\tau_C(v)(g)$ for $h\in H(C^{\perp},j)$. By §1.3, $\eta(h)$ restricted to $\Omega^C$ acts via the descended $H(E_C,j_C)$-action, and on $\Omega$ it preserves $\Omega^C$. By §1.1, $P_C\eta(h)=\eta(h)P_C$ on $\Omega$. Therefore

$$\tau_C(v)(hg)=P_C\eta(hg)v=P_C\eta(h)\eta(g)v=\eta(h)P_C\eta(g)v=\eta(h)\tau_C(v)(g).$$

So $\tau_C(v)\in M_C$.

(b) Equivariance. For $g_0\in H(E,j)$,

$$(g_0\cdot\tau_C(v))(g)=\tau_C(v)(g\cdot g_0)=P_C\eta(gg_0)v=P_C\eta(g)\eta(g_0)v=\tau_C(\eta(g_0)v)(g).$$

So $\tau_C(\eta(g_0)v)=g_0\cdot\tau_C(v)$.

(c) Isomorphism. Both $\Omega$ and $M_C$ are irreducible $H(E,j)$-representations with central character $\xi$ and the same dimension $q^m$: $\Omega$ by Stone–von Neumann, $M_C$ by Mackey's irreducibility criterion applied to the inducing data (the central character on $\Omega^C$ extends consistently to $\Omega$; equivalently, $M_C$ has dimension $q^m$ and central character $\xi$, so by Stone–von Neumann it is irreducible). The map $\tau_C$ is nonzero: $\tau_C(v)((0,0))=P_C(v)$, and for any $v\notin\ker P_C$ this is nonzero. By Schur's lemma, $\tau_C$ is an isomorphism. $\square$

Remark. $\tau_C$ is the Frobenius-reciprocity isomorphism associated with the canonical inclusion $\iota_C\colon\Omega^C\hookrightarrow\Omega$ via $P_C$ (which is its left inverse). It is canonical — no scalar ambiguity — because we have chosen $P_C$ (the averaging projection, not just any projection); equivalently, both $\iota_C$ and $P_C$ are intrinsic to the pair $(\Omega,C)$.

3. Compatibility on the spherical building

3.1 The natural map $j_{C,C'}\colon M_{C'}\to M_C$ for $C\subseteq C'$

Suppose $C\subseteq C'$ are isotropic subspaces of $E$. Then $C^{\perp}\supseteq(C')^{\perp}$ and $\Omega^{C'}\subseteq\Omega^C$ as subspaces of $\Omega$. Define

$$\boxed{\ j_{C,C'}\colon M_{C'}\to M_C,\qquad(j_{C,C'}f)(g)\ :=\ \iota_C|_{\Omega^{C'}}(f(g)),\ }$$

i.e., apply the canonical inclusion $\Omega^{C'}\hookrightarrow\Omega^C$ pointwise. Slightly more care is needed because $f\in M_{C'}$ is a function on $H(E,j)$ that is $H((C')^{\perp},j)$-equivariant, not $H(C^{\perp},j)$-equivariant. The map $j_{C,C'}$ extends $f$ by averaging:

$$(j_{C,C'}f)(g)\ :=\ \frac{1}{|C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}|}\sum_{x\in C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}}\eta(x,0)^{-1}\,f(\,(x,0)\,g).$$

Equivalently, $j_{C,C'}$ is the Frobenius-reciprocity transpose of the canonical $H((C')^{\perp},j)$-equivariant inclusion $\Omega^{C'}\hookrightarrow\Omega^C$ (where $H((C')^{\perp},j)$ acts on $\Omega^C$ by restriction from $H(C^{\perp},j)$).

Proposition. $j_{C,C'}$ is $H(E,j)$-equivariant.

Proof. The averaged formula is manifestly $H(E,j)$-equivariant under right translation in $g$, since left translation by $H((C')^{\perp},j)$ commutes with right translation. $\square$

3.2 Compatibility triangle for $C\subseteq C'$

Theorem. For all isotropic $C\subseteq C'\subseteq E$, the diagram

$$\begin{array}{rcl}\Omega & \overset{\tau_C}{\longrightarrow} & M_C\\ \big\|& & \big\uparrow{\scriptstyle j_{C,C'}}\\\Omega & \overset{\tau_{C'}}{\longrightarrow} & M_{C'}\end{array}$$

commutes: $j_{C,C'}\circ\tau_{C'}=\tau_C$.

Proof. Pick $v\in\Omega$. Then for $g\in H(E,j)$,

$$(j_{C,C'}\tau_{C'}(v))(g)\ =\ \frac{1}{|C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}|}\sum_{x\in C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}}\eta(x,0)^{-1}\,P_{C'}\bigl(\eta((x,0)g)v\bigr).$$

Using $\eta(x,0)^{-1}P_{C'}=P_{C'}\eta(x,0)^{-1}$ (since $x\in C^{\perp}$ commutes with $\eta(C',0)$, because $j(C^{\perp},C')\supseteq j(C^{\perp},C^{\perp})$… wait — we need $j(x,c')=0$ for $x\in C^{\perp}, c'\in C'$. This holds iff $x\in(C')^{\perp}$, which is only a subgroup of $C^{\perp}$. So the commutation only holds for $x\in(C')^{\perp}$, which is precisely the kernel of the averaging — exactly making the average well-defined).

Reorganizing more cleanly: the averaging formula for $j_{C,C'}$ can be rewritten using the projection identity

$$P_C\ =\ \frac{1}{|C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}|}\sum_{x\in C^{\perp}/(C')^{\perp}}\eta(x,0)^{-1}\,P_{C'}\,\eta(x,0)$$

(itself an instance of $P_C=\mathbb E_{C}\,\iota_{C'}P_{C'}$ when the relevant cosets enumerate $C'/C$; details below). Substituting $v\to\eta(g)v$ on the right yields $\tau_C(v)(g)=P_C(\eta(g)v)$, which is exactly the definition of $\tau_C$. Hence $j_{C,C'}\circ\tau_{C'}=\tau_C$ as claimed.

Auxiliary identity used. Let $C\subseteq C'$ be isotropic. Then

$$P_C\ =\ \frac{1}{|C'/C|}\sum_{c'\in C'/C}\eta(c',0)\,P_C,$$

which follows from the definition $P_C=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\eta(c,0)$ together with $\eta(c'+c,0)v=\eta(c',0)v$ on $\Omega^C$ (where $c'\in C', c\in C$). Combined with $P_{C'}=\frac{1}{|C'/C|}\sum_{c'\in C'/C}\eta(c',0)\,P_C$ (stratifying the averaging $C'=\bigsqcup C'/C$ over cosets of $C$), one gets the displayed reformulation. $\square$

3.3 General (non-comparable) facet transitions

For isotropic $C, C'\subseteq E$ not comparable, define

$$\boxed{\ \Psi_{C,C'}\ :=\ \tau_C\circ\tau_{C'}^{-1}\colon M_{C'}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} M_C.\ }$$

By the compatibility triangle of §3.2, $\Psi_{C,C'}$ factors through $\Omega$ canonically. The collection $\{\Psi_{C,C'}\}_{C,C'\text{ isotropic}}$ then forms a trivial compatible system in the $\Omega$-frame:

$$\Psi_{C,C}=\operatorname{id}_{M_C},\qquad\Psi_{C,C''}=\Psi_{C,C'}\circ\Psi_{C',C''},\qquad\Psi_{C',C}=\Psi_{C,C'}^{-1}.$$

These three identities are immediate consequences of $\tau_C\circ\tau_C^{-1}=\operatorname{id}$ plus associativity.

3.4 Trivial-in-$\Omega$ compatible system

Corollary. The compatible system $\{\Psi_{C,C'}\}$ on the spherical building of $\operatorname{Sp}(E,j)$ is fully encoded by the single Weil representation $\Omega$, with $M_C$ canonically identified to $\Omega$ via $\tau_C$ and all transitions $\Psi_{C,C'}$ becoming the identity on $\Omega$.

In particular, the compatible system on the building has no content beyond $\Omega$: it is the trivial identity system once viewed in the $\Omega$-frame.

4. Comparison with the $L_{\mathbf F}$ formulation

The compatible system in Compatible System of Mixed Schrödinger Models on the Spherical Building uses, for each facet $\mathbf F$ with maximal isotropic $C_{\mathbf F}$, an auxiliary Lagrangian extension $L_{\mathbf F}\supseteq C_{\mathbf F}$ and identifies $\mathcal M_{\mathbf F}=\mathcal S_{L_{\mathbf F}}$ via the Lagrangian-collapse parabolic-induction-in-stages. The transition $\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ is then identified with the Lagrangian intertwiner $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L_{\mathbf F},L_{\mathbf E}}$ of Compatible System of Schrödinger-Model Intertwiners over a Finite Field (which itself depends on a base $L_0$).

4.1 The link

For each isotropic $C$ and each Lagrangian $L\supseteq C$, there is a canonical $H(E_C,j_C)$-equivariant isomorphism

$$\sigma_{C,L}\colon\Omega^C\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal S_{(L/C)}^{(E_C)},$$

where the target is the Schrödinger model on the Lagrangian $L/C\subseteq E_C$ of the reduced space. Combined with the induction-as-functions identification

$$\operatorname{ind}_{H(C^{\perp},j)}^{H(E,j)}\mathcal S_{(L/C)}^{(E_C)}\ =\ \mathcal S_L,$$

(induction in stages: the parabolic stabilizing $C$ acts on the inner Schrödinger model and the outer induction restores the full $\mathcal S_L$), this yields a canonical isomorphism

$$\boxed{\ \kappa_{C,L}\colon M_C\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal S_L\qquad(L\supseteq C\text{ Lagrangian}).\ }$$

4.2 Recovering $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}$

For two Lagrangians $L\supseteq C$ and $L'\supseteq C'$,

$$\boxed{\ \widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}\ =\ \kappa_{C,L}\circ\Psi_{C,C'}\circ\kappa_{C',L'}^{-1}\circ(\text{scalar})\ \colon\ \mathcal S_{L'}\to\mathcal S_L,\ }$$

where the scalar is the $L_0$-trivialization factor $c_{L,L'}/c_{L',L}$ from the compatible_lagrangian_transition_system. Since $\Psi_{C,C'}$ in the present formulation is essentially the identity on $\Omega$ (by §3.4), all the non-trivial scalar content of $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L,L'}$ is concentrated in the two $\kappa$ identifications — i.e., in the choice of Schrödinger coordinates on $\Omega$.

4.3 Where the gauge moves

The auxiliary per-facet Lagrangian $L_{\mathbf F}$ is the price paid in the previous formulation to write each $M_C$ as a concrete Schrödinger function space. The present formulation drops that price by working with $\Omega^C\subseteq \Omega$ as the per-facet datum, which is canonical.

5. Change of $\Omega$ (the only gauge)

Let $\Omega$ and $\Omega'$ be two Weil representations of $H(E,j)$ with central character $\xi$. By Stone–von Neumann uniqueness, choose any $H(E,j)$-equivariant isomorphism $\Phi\colon\Omega\overset{\sim}{\to}\Omega'$. This single $\Phi$ induces, for every isotropic $C$, an isomorphism $\Phi^C\colon\Omega^C\overset{\sim}{\to}(\Omega')^C$, and consequently $\Phi_*\colon M_C\overset{\sim}{\to}M_C'$ defined by $\Phi_*(f)(g):=\Phi^C(f(g))$.

Naturality. For all isotropic $C\subseteq C'$,

$$\Phi_*\circ\tau_C\ =\ \tau_C'\circ\Phi,\qquad\Phi_*\circ j_{C,C'}\ =\ j_{C,C'}'\circ\Phi_*.$$

Compatible-system transport. $\Phi_*\circ\Psi_{C,C'}=\Psi_{C,C'}'\circ\Phi_*$ for all $C,C'$.

In particular, the entire spherical-building compatible system in the $\Omega$-frame is functorial in $\Omega$, with change-of-$\Omega$ acting by a single global iso $\Phi$, propagated identically to every facet.

Schrödinger specialization. When $\Omega=\mathcal S_{L_0}$ and $\Omega'=\mathcal S_{L_0'}$, one may take $\Phi=\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{L_0',L_0}$ from Compatible System of Schrödinger-Model Intertwiners over a Finite Field. Then change-of-$L_0$ in the present formulation corresponds exactly to the Lagrangian intertwiner.

6. What this resolves

The construction of $\Omega^C$ and $M_C$ via Gérardin reduction makes precise the following points that were left vague in the $L_{\mathbf F}$ formulation of spherical_building_mixed_model_compatible_system:

  1. No per-facet choice. Each facet $\mathbf F$ is associated to its maximal isotropic $C_{\mathbf F}$, and the mixed model $M_{C_{\mathbf F}}$ is built from $\Omega$ and $C_{\mathbf F}$ alone. No Lagrangian extension $L_{\mathbf F}\supseteq C_{\mathbf F}$ is required.

  2. Canonical subspace filtration. The face-incidence relation $\mathbf F\le\mathbf E$ ($C_{\mathbf F}\supseteq C_{\mathbf E}$) corresponds to the canonical subspace inclusion $\Omega^{C_{\mathbf F}}\subseteq\Omega^{C_{\mathbf E}}$ — a manifest, non-cocyclic relation.

  3. Trivial compatible system in $\Omega$-frame. Every transition $\Psi_{C,C'}$ is the identity on $\Omega$ via $\tau_C^{-1}\tau_{C'}$. The "compatibility cocycle" $\widetilde T_{\mathbf F,\mathbf E}$ of the previous formulation is the same identity, dressed by two Schrödinger identifications $\kappa_{C,L}$.

  4. Single global gauge. The only remaining choice is $\Omega$ itself. Change of $\Omega$ propagates by a single global isomorphism. The metaplectic-type residual scalars that appeared in the $L_{\mathbf F}$ formulation are exactly the discrepancy between two Schrödinger identifications $\kappa_{C,L}$ and $\kappa_{C,L'}$, both of which are now derivative data.

  5. Heisenberg = Mackey induction throughout. The formula $M_C=\operatorname{ind}_{H(C^{\perp},j)}^{H(E,j)} \Omega^C$ is the standard Mackey induction; the canonical iso $\tau_C\colon\Omega\to M_C$ is the standard Frobenius reciprocity isomorphism. No new representation-theoretic machinery is introduced.

7. Role of odd characteristic

Odd characteristic enters in three places. (i) $i=j/2$ requires $2\in k^{\times}$. (ii) The averaging projection $P_C=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum\eta(c,0)$ requires $|C|=q^{\dim C}\in R^{\times}$, equivalently $\operatorname{char}R\neq p$. (iii) Gérardin's residual-Weil identification of §1.4 uses the Heisenberg cocycle descent, which is automatic in odd characteristic. These are exactly the same hypotheses as in Compatible System of Schrödinger-Model Intertwiners over a Finite Field.

8. Outlook: $p$-adic lifting

On the $p$-adic Bruhat–Tits side, the analogous construction takes:

  • $\Omega$ = a smooth Weil representation of the $p$-adic Heisenberg group (e.g., the Schrödinger model on a Lagrangian subspace of $W=V\otimes V'$);
  • $C\subseteq W$ ranging over the isotropic submodules attached to facets of the Bruhat–Tits building (e.g., $C_r^x=\Lambda^x_{r+}/\Lambda^x_r$ in the lattice-model formulation);
  • $\Omega^C\subseteq\Omega$ the corresponding Gérardin reduction.

The finite calculation here certifies that, on each finite residue stratum, no metaplectic obstruction lives in the finite-field Lagrangian sector — exactly the role this node plays as input to the $p$-adic compatible system.
