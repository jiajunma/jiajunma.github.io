#

Finite-Field Maximal-Parabolic Weil Input

Let $k$ be a finite field of odd order, let $(\ell,j)$ be a finite symplectic $k$-vector space, and choose a maximal-parabolic decomposition

$$ \ell=X\oplus \ell_0\oplus Y. $$

Here $X$ and $Y$ are totally isotropic and paired perfectly by $j$, $\ell_0$ is a nondegenerate symplectic subspace, and $\ell_0\perp (X\oplus Y)$. Let

$$ (\omega_0,\mathbf{S}_0) $$

be the finite Weil representation of $\operatorname{Sp}(\ell_0)$ for the same central character. The mixed Schrödinger realization of the finite Weil representation of $\operatorname{Sp}(\ell)$ is

$$ \omega_X \quad\text{on}\quad \mathcal{S}(Y,\mathbf{S}_0):=\operatorname{Fun}(Y,\mathbf{S}_0). $$

Let $\psi_k$ denote the finite additive character used in this model.

Write

$$ P_X\subset \operatorname{Sp}(\ell) $$

for the parabolic subgroup stabilizing $X$. Its Levi subgroup preserving the decomposition $X\oplus\ell_0\oplus Y$ is

$$ M_X\simeq \operatorname{GL}(X)\times\operatorname{Sp}(\ell_0). $$

For

$$ m(a,s)(x+v+y)=a x+s v+d_a y, $$

with $a\in\operatorname{GL}(X)$ and $s\in\operatorname{Sp}(\ell_0)$, the map $d_a\in\operatorname{GL}(Y)$ is determined by

$$ j(a x,d_a y)=j(x,y) \qquad (x\in X,\ y\in Y). $$

Equivalently, $d_a=(a^\vee)^{-1}$, where $a^\vee$ is the dual of $a$ under the pairing $X\times Y\to k$. The finite Weil action is

$$ (\omega_X(m(a,s))F)(y) = \epsilon_k(\det a)\, \omega_0(s)\bigl(F(d_a^{-1}y)\bigr), $$

where

$$ \epsilon_k:k^\times\to\{\pm1\} $$

is the quadratic character.

The unipotent radical $N_X$ may be written as follows. Let $C:Y\to \ell_0$, and let $C^\dagger:\ell_0\to X$ be determined by

$$ j_0(v,Cy)+j(C^\dagger v,y)=0, \qquad v\in\ell_0,\ y\in Y, $$

where $j_0=j|_{\ell_0}$. Let $B:Y\to X$ satisfy

$$ j(By,y')-j(By',y)+j_0(Cy,Cy')=0 \qquad (y,y'\in Y). $$

Then

$$ n(C,B)(x+v+y) = x+C^\dagger v+B y+v+C y+y $$

lies in $N_X$, and every element of $N_X$ is of this form. In the mixed model its action is

$$ (\omega_X(n(C,B))F)(y) = \psi_k\!\left(\tfrac12 j(By,y)\right)\, \rho_0(Cy)\,F(y), $$

where $\rho_0$ is the finite Heisenberg representation attached to $\ell_0$, and $\rho_0(Cy)$ means the action of the vector $Cy\in\ell_0$ inside that Heisenberg group.

In particular, evaluation at $0\in Y$,

$$ \lambda_X(F):=F(0)\in \mathbf{S}_0, $$

is fixed by $N_X$ and transforms under the Levi by

$$ \lambda_X\circ\omega_X(m(a,s)) = \epsilon_k(\det a)\,\omega_0(s)\circ\lambda_X. $$

Thus the scalar part contributed by the maximal parabolic is the quadratic determinant character on the $\operatorname{GL}(X)$-factor; the $\operatorname{Sp}(\ell_0)$-factor remains as the residual finite Weil representation. Only in the Siegel case $\ell_0=0$ does this reduce to a genuine $P_X$-eigencharacter on a line, trivial on $N_X$ and equal to $\epsilon_k(\det a)$ on the Levi.

In the lattice situation, let $L^+=L^\perp\subset L$, and put

$$ \ell_L:=L/L^+. $$

Let

$$ K_L:=\{g\in\operatorname{Sp}(W):gL=L,\ gL^+=L^+\}. $$

If $\ell_L$ contains an isotropic subspace $X$, choose a decomposition

$$ \ell_L=X\oplus \ell_0\oplus Y $$

as above. The subgroup of $K_L$ stabilizing $X$ maps to the parabolic $P_X$, and the scalar part of its action on the mixed-model evaluation at $0\in Y$ is the inflated character

$$ \chi_{L,X}(g)=\epsilon_k(\det a_g), $$

where $g\in K_L$ stabilizes $X$, and $a_g\in\operatorname{GL}(X)$ is the induced action on $X$. The residual $\operatorname{Sp}(\ell_0)$-Weil action must be kept as an operator, not collapsed to a scalar, unless the subgroup under consideration fixes a chosen line in $\mathbf{S}_0$.

If

$$ L^+\subset A\subset L, \qquad A=A^\perp, $$

then

$$ X_{L,A}:=A/L^+ $$

is Lagrangian in $\ell_L$, so $\ell_0=0$. The subgroup of $K_L$ that stabilizes $A$ acts through the Siegel parabolic $P_{X_{L,A}}$, and on the characteristic-vector line determined by $\mathbf{1}_A$ the finite scalar is the inflated character $\chi_{L,A}:=\chi_{L,X_{L,A}}$.

Thus, in a nested incidence $L_F\subseteq L_E$ with a common self-dual choice $A_0\subseteq L_F$, compare the two finite parabolic characters attached to

$$ X_{F,A_0}:=A_0/L_F^+, \qquad X_{E,A_0}:=A_0/L_E^+. $$

For elements $k\in K_F$ that also stabilize $A_0$, the expected local calculation is the ratio

$$ \delta^{\mathrm{lm}}_{E,F}(k) = \chi_{L_F,A_0}(k)\,\chi_{L_E,A_0}(k)^{-1}, $$

unless the chosen comparison between the two $A_0$-test lines contributes an additional finite Fourier normalization scalar. This last point must be checked directly in the transition-map problem. For general $k\in K_F$ with $kA_0\ne A_0$, the same calculation should be made after comparing the line $\mathbb{C}\,\mathbf{1}_{A_0}$ with the line $\mathbb{C}\,\mathbf{1}_{kA_0}$ using the normalized finite intertwiner.
